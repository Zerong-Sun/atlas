## city.maabar.name

Maabar

## ev.maabar.entry.body

At the Maabar market, a mother and son are moving their fish baskets into the shade before the sun reaches them. He has put his largest basket in front, blocking the cloth seller next door. “She needs to open her stall too,” his mother says. The boy drags it aside and asks the neighbour whether the space is enough. She spreads a bolt of cotton and thanks him. A horse groom passes through with an animal recently brought by sea; the boy moves the last basket so its hooves have room. You ask which road continues along the coast. The cloth seller points beyond the market well and says carriers use it toward Coilum. The mother offers you water, then asks her son to bring the groom a cup as well. “He can pay for fish later,” she says. The boy runs after him, pleased to have a reason to see the horse up close. If you stay, the two sellers will show you where the day's goods go after the market closes.

## ev.maabar.entry.choice.look

Spend a day getting to know the market

## ev.maabar.entry.choice.pass

Ask the road through town and continue

## ev.maabar.entry.title

The Fish Baskets at Maabar

## ev.ev_maabar_entry.choice_1_result

The cloth seller points you toward the market well and the road beyond it. The boy carries water to the groom as you pass. You continue with the way through Maabar clear in mind.

## ev.ev_maabar_entry.choice_2_result

You stay for a day. The mother sells her fish before the heat grows strong, and the cloth seller shows how she folds the last bolt for the carriers. The boy returns to tell them all about the horse. By evening you know both the coast road and the people of the market.

## ev.maabar.site_gap.title

The Weaver’s Broken Thread

## ev.maabar.site_gap.body

At a weaving shed in Maabar, a woman has stopped her loom because a thread in the warp has snapped. Her apprentice reaches for a thick strand to replace it. “Try that against the others first,” she says. He pulls it between his fingers and sees that the weave would stand out. You ask how they can join a thread without leaving a hard knot in the finished cloth. She overlaps the ends, rolls them gently and tucks the join toward the edge. The apprentice tries the next one and asks whether dampening it helps. “A little,” she answers, handing him a wet cloth. A carrier comes to collect cloth for the coast road toward Coilum. She asks him to wait until the loom is moving again, and he sits near the door watching the boy work. He points out the road he will take when you ask, then waits for the woman to check the repaired line. The carrier puts his hands behind his back rather than touching the unfinished cloth.

## ev.maabar.site_gap.choice.route

Ask the carrier about the Coilum road

## ev.ev_maabar_site_gap.choice_1_result

The carrier walks you to the corner where the land road turns toward Coilum. He shows which track his loaded cart uses and asks you to repeat the first turn. You leave him to collect his cloth once the weaver finishes.

## ev.maabar.site_gap.choice.interact

Help the apprentice join the thread

## ev.ev_maabar_site_gap.choice_2_result

You hold the neighbouring threads apart while the apprentice overlaps the broken ends and rolls them together. The weaver lets the loom move once, then checks that the join lies inside the edge. She shows you why the thicker strand would have left a mark across the cloth.

## ev.maabar.courtyard.title

A Crate for the Fig Tree

## ev.maabar.courtyard.body

In a courtyard off the market, an old man is trying to move a wooden crate away from a young fig tree. His granddaughter has put it there to hold the shade cloth, but its corner is pressing against the trunk. “I was making a place for you to sit,” she says. He thanks her and asks if the cloth can be tied to the wall instead. She finds an old ring in the masonry; it comes loose when she pulls it. A neighbour who works with wood offers a sound hook from his box and asks someone to hold the cloth while he fits it. The old man promises to pay for the new hook and the work. The girl wants to help but her grandfather sends her first to bring water for the neighbour. “You can hold the other end when you come back.” The tree's leaves are already bending in the heat. You can help the neighbour fit the hook, or help the girl move the crate and make a safe seat for her grandfather.

## ev.maabar.courtyard.choice.hook

Help fit the new hook

## ev.maabar.courtyard.result.hook

You hold the shade cloth while the neighbour sets his new hook in the wall. The granddaughter returns with water and ties her end. The old man sits beside the fig tree without the crate against its trunk and asks the neighbour to pay you a share of the repair fee.

## ev.maabar.courtyard.choice.seat

Help the girl make her grandfather a seat

## ev.maabar.courtyard.result.seat

You and the granddaughter move the crate clear of the tree and turn it into a steady seat. Her grandfather tries it and asks her to bring the cloth a little lower. The neighbour fixes the hook, and the household thanks you for making the courtyard comfortable again.
