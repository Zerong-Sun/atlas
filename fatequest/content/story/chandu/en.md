## city.chandu.name

Chandu

## ev.chandu.a.body

At a builder’s yard, carpenter Batar is setting a light reed wall into a wooden frame. His daughter holds the lashing while her grandfather asks whether the joint can be untied without breaking it. Batar loosens one loop and shows him. The family has built shelters for travelling households for years; an older drawing on the wall shows a much grander hall that people here still tell stories about.

You ask whether the wall will stand in rain. Batar shows the coating on the reeds and points to a covered test frame outside. His daughter says she spent the morning painting a blue border on the new panel, then hid it because she thought it looked crooked. Grandfather finds it and says the line follows the curve of the reed. She takes another look before choosing to repaint it.

Batar charges 5 silver to take visitors through the working yard and explain the frame closely. The family also welcomes you to look from the meadow or spend a day sketching their joinery. The daughter is tying the next loop as you decide.

## ev.chandu.a.title

The Reed Builder’s Yard

## ev.chandu.b.body

At the meadow edge, a herder named Hulan is bringing a small group of white mares toward water. Her son counts them, then counts again because one foal keeps walking behind its mother’s tail. A falconer on the next path asks Hulan to keep the mares clear of the place where he is training a bird. She points to the stream and says she will cross after the bird has settled.

You ask whether visitors may watch the training. The falconer says his fee is 8 silver for a lesson with the bird and lure. Hulan says you can walk the outer path freely and meet the herd where it stops. Her son asks what a falcon eats. The falconer answers, then asks him what the young foal needs after a long walk. ‘Water, and a quiet place,’ the boy says.

The two families have used these grounds in different seasons. Hulan moves her mares slowly toward the stream, and the falconer checks his bird’s hood before letting anyone nearer.

## ev.chandu.b.title

Along the Meadow Path

## ev.chandu.c.body

At morning milking, Hulan’s aunt is setting out wooden bowls while her granddaughter brings the white mares one by one. The girl asks why her aunt keeps a small bowl apart from the rest. ‘That one is for our family’s thanks before we drink,’ the older woman says. She tells the girl where to place it, then asks her to wash her hands.

You ask what the family gives thanks for. The aunt names good grass, healthy animals and relatives who returned safely from the road. A neighbour who shares the pasture says her own family uses different words for the same morning. The two women talk as they work, and the granddaughter asks whether she may hear both versions. The aunt lets the neighbour speak first.

The aunt asks you to pass the small bowl to her granddaughter, then asks the neighbour what words her mother once used. They talk while the milk warms in their hands. The granddaughter returns and listens, waiting for a chance to ask another question.

## ev.chandu.c.title

The White Mares at Morning

## ev.chandu.entry.body

At a stable yard on the grassland, a woman named Orina is sorting saddle straps while her son holds a foal that keeps trying to nose the bundles. He asks whether the animal can carry a small pack yet. ‘When it can stand quietly for the fitting,’ she says. She gives him a brush instead of a load, and the foal settles under his hand.

You ask where to declare your goods. Orina points toward the clerk’s desk and says a full check takes two days. Her sister, a cloth trader, has a full camlet trading bale for 146 silver. She also knows families who have lived beside these meadows through changes of camps and roads; each keeps a different account of the old buildings. The son asks which one his aunt tells best. ‘The one about Grandfather losing his sandals,’ she says.

A rider arrives asking for a repaired strap. Orina checks the stitches, then tells her son to walk the foal while she finishes the work. The clerk calls the next traveller to the desk.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.title

A Groom’s Introduction

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.body

After your goods are checked, the groom at Orina’s yard asks you to look at a mare whose pack keeps sliding left. ‘My brother tied it,’ he says, ‘and now he insists the mare walks crooked.’ The brother comes over carrying a strap and hears him. ‘She does walk crooked when you put all the pots on one side,’ he replies. You help them unload two pots, and the mare stands comfortably.

The groom asks how far you intend to travel. You tell him, and he names a horse keeper across the meadow who knows which animals can take a long road. His brother asks whether that keeper will remember a traveller sent over without a note. The groom tears a corner from the clean tally sheet, writes his own name, and asks the brother to witness it. ‘There,’ he says, ‘now he’ll know why you came.’ He lets you decide whether to take the introduction.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.choice_1

Take the signed introduction

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.choice_1_result

You take the note and ask both brothers where the keeper works. The younger one points to a low paddock beside the stream. The groom says you may mention the mare and the lopsided pots when you arrive; the keeper knows the family.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.choice_2

Thank the brothers and leave the note

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a.choice_2_result

You thank them for the offer and leave the note on the tally board. The brothers return to loading the mare, this time dividing the pots between both sides.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.title

At the Stream Paddock

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.body

The horse keeper reads the groom’s note, then looks up when you mention the lopsided pots. ‘That brother will put a whole kitchen on one flank if you let him,’ she laughs. A mare beside her has a rubbed patch under the saddle, and she is waiting for a new pad before letting anyone ride her.

You ask whether she keeps another animal for a longer journey. She shows you a quieter horse and explains what it eats during a march. A young helper interrupts to say the saddle pad is ready. The keeper asks him to bring it over and lets you watch how she checks the fit. When the helper pulls too hard, she loosens the strap and makes him try again. She gives you a choice: carry the groom’s introduction onward to a rider who needs it, or stay a day to help finish fitting the horses.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.choice_1

Carry the introduction to the waiting rider

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.choice_1_result

You pass the note to the waiting rider and explain who sent you. The keeper nods as he reads it; the helper takes him to the quiet horse. Your name is remembered among the people at the paddock.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.choice_2

Stay a day and help fit the saddles

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_a_resolution.choice_2_result

You stay for the day and help check each saddle pad. By evening the rubbed mare can take a short walk without flinching. The keeper thanks you and keeps the groom’s paper with her other notes.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.title

The Falconer’s Ground

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.body

The falconer who asked Orina about the meadow families is putting fresh padding on a bird’s perch. His niece holds the roll of cloth, but a gust sends it across the grass. The bird shifts, and the falconer asks everyone to stand still until it settles. His niece retrieves the cloth and apologizes. ‘The wind has tried to take that roll three times,’ he says, and she answers that he might finally tie it down.

You ask where a traveller can watch birds without troubling the herd. He draws the edge of the meadow in dust with a stick, marking the stream and the low rise where the bird returns. His niece adds the place where Hulan takes the foal to water. She says the drawing would help a visiting rider who keeps asking the wrong family for directions. The falconer offers to send the drawing along with you, or to keep it here for the next visitor.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.choice_1

Take the drawing to the visiting rider

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.choice_1_result

You copy the falconer’s drawing, including his niece’s mark for the foal’s watering place. She runs after you to add one last bend in the path. The rider is still waiting at the far yard.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.choice_2

Leave the drawing with the family

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b.choice_2_result

You leave the drawing with the family. The niece puts a stone on each corner so the wind cannot lift it; the falconer finally ties down the loose cloth roll.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.title

The Rider at the Far Yard

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.body

The rider at the far yard has been sent to collect a bird perch, but the path he was given takes him straight through Hulan’s herd. He studies your drawing and asks which mark is the low rise. You point it out; the falconer’s niece has drawn a tiny horse beside the watering place, and he laughs when he recognizes why.

His own horse is thirsty. You walk with him to the stream and show the outer path that keeps the mares undisturbed. Hulan’s son is there counting the foals again. He tells the rider to wait until the smallest one has crossed, then waves him through. The rider says another person is expected later with a second perch. He asks whether you can pass on the directions, or whether you would rather stay and make a clearer copy with him.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.choice_1

Tell the next rider how to cross the meadow

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.choice_1_result

You meet the next rider and explain the stream crossing and Hulan’s herd. He follows the outer path and reaches the bird yard without scattering the foals. The first rider thanks you as he carries off the perch.

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.choice_2

Stay a day to make a clearer route drawing

## ev.ev_chandu_consequence_b_resolution.choice_2_result

You spend a day walking the turns with the rider and drawing them again. Hulan’s son checks the crossing mark. The finished sheet stays at the yard for travellers who come after you.

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.title

Orina’s Horse Keeper

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.body

Orina’s horse keeper is brushing a quiet mare while a young helper tries to read a worn hoof print in the mud. ‘This one belongs to the grey horse,’ the helper says. The keeper asks him to look at the chipped edge and try again. He finds the right animal and grins.

You ask how she decides which horse should carry a traveller. She looks at your pack, then at the roads you have marked. ‘How much weight? How many days between water?’ she asks. Her helper fetches two saddles so you can feel their different balance. When you say one sits better, she asks why. You explain what you noticed, and she adds the point you missed. She is willing to talk now, spend a whole day showing you her work, or let you return when your route is settled.

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_1

Ask how she judges a horse for the road

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_1_result

She shows you where the saddle has rubbed the mare and how a short walk reveals it. Her helper takes the animal round the yard while you watch. You thank her for the first lesson and remember her name.

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_2

Stay a day and learn at the horse yard

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_2_result

You stay the day. By evening you can tell why the grey horse needs a lighter load and why the quiet mare should rest before a long march. The keeper asks you to come back for a final demonstration.

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_3

Thank her and return to your journey

## ev.chandu.mentor_closure.choice_3_result

You thank the keeper and leave the yard. The helper is still comparing hoof prints as you go; she lets him arrive at the answer himself.

## ev.ev_chandu_a_followup.title

The Next Reed Frame

## ev.ev_chandu_a_followup.body

Batar has begun a second frame, and his daughter has copied his demonstration onto scrap wood. She asks whether the drawing shows the knot from the front or the back. You turn the board around for her; she sees why the first lashing twisted. Her grandfather says he made the same mistake when he was learning. ‘You said you never did,’ she reminds him. ‘I was hoping you had forgotten,’ he says.

You ask where the reeds come from. Batar names households farther along the road toward Cacanfu that cut and dry them, and his daughter adds which bundles bend best. She has kept one loose knot for you to try. The grandfather is going to stay all day to help raise the frame and offers you a place at the workbench. Batar hands his daughter the cord and waits for her to choose the next knot.

## ev.ev_chandu_b_followup.title

At the Bird Yard

## ev.ev_chandu_b_followup.body

At the bird yard, the falconer lets his niece feed a trained hawk while he repairs a perch. She asks why the bird refuses the first piece. He says the meat is too cold and tells her to warm it in her hand. You ask whether he taught her the same way he learned. ‘My uncle shouted more,’ she says before he can answer. He laughs and admits that his own teacher shouted still louder.

A messenger from the road to Cambaluc comes for directions. The falconer asks his niece to describe the first river crossing; she remembers the bridge but forgets the fork beyond it. He corrects her and lets her finish. A younger bird is ready for a short feeding exercise, and she asks whether you would watch. He puts down his tools so both of you can see the bird clearly.

## ev.ev_chandu_c_followup.title

The Small Wooden Bowl

## ev.ev_chandu_c_followup.body

Hulan’s aunt has dried the small bowl and is showing her granddaughter how to mend a loose rim with a narrow strip of wood. The girl asks why they keep using the old bowl when her mother has made two new ones. ‘Your grandmother shaped this one,’ the aunt says. A neighbour comes by with fresh grass for the mares and adds that her own family keeps an older bowl for a different morning custom. The women compare what each remembers, and neither expects the girl to take notes. She does, on the back of a feed tally.

You ask how the bowl became part of the family gathering. The aunt recalls a winter when her mother shared milk with a stranded household. Hulan’s son arrives, sees the feed tally covered in writing, and asks whether the horses have somehow learned to read. The granddaughter laughs and sets the repaired bowl where he cannot kick it.

## ev.ev_chandu_mentor_followup.title

One More Morning at the Horse Yard

The helper brings her out and lets you watch the first few steps. He wants to keep the corrected map for the next visitor. ## ev.chandu.mareblanket.title

The Mare’s Old Blanket

## ev.chandu.mareblanket.body

Behind the horse yard, an older woman named Saran has laid out a worn felt blanket. Her grandson wants to sew a new border around it, but his mother has set out fresh felt for a different blanket. ‘This one belonged to my first mare,’ Saran says. The boy asks whether the mare was white like Hulan’s. ‘Brown, and quicker to steal a drink than any of them.’ His mother smiles; she has heard the story many times, but the boy asks for the part about the stream again.

You ask why they do not mend the old blanket together. The mother turns over a corner to show you thin patches that would tear under a saddle. She wants the new felt for the working horses and the old piece kept at home. Saran agrees to the new blanket, yet wants the boy to remember which horse taught her to ride. The boy fetches a scrap of blue wool. ‘Could her name go on this corner?’ he asks. Saran cannot write it, but she remembers the shape of the mark she once stitched. You hold the blanket as she searches for the right thread.

## ev.chandu.mareblanket.choice.mark

Help Saran find the old stitched mark

## ev.chandu.mareblanket.choice.felt

Stay a day and make the working blanket

## ev.chandu.mareblanket.result.mark

You lift the faded edge into the light. Saran finds three blue stitches under an old repair and shows her grandson how to copy them on the new corner. The mother folds the old blanket carefully; Saran tells the stream story once more while the boy sews.

## ev.chandu.mareblanket.result.felt

You stay a day cutting fresh felt and binding its edges. The boy makes a small practice patch before touching the working blanket. At evening Saran lays her old piece beside the new one and says the horses will have warm backs this winter.
