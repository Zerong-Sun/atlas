## city.chandu.name

Chandu

## ev.chandu.a.body

The Great Kaan has a summer palace at Chandu built entirely of gilded cane — the
columns, the walls, the roof-beams, everything. The cane is harvested from a forest in
the south of Manzi, split, lacquered, and gilded with gold leaf before being lashed
together with silken cords. The palace can be taken down and moved when the Kaan
travels, for it is held together only by the silk bindings. Dragons in green, blue,
vermilion, and gold coil up every pillar. Inside, the floors are laid with marble slabs
so polished that they reflect the gilded ceiling. No other building in the world is so
light and yet so splendid.

## ev.chandu.b.body

Behind the palace, enclosed by a wall that runs for sixteen miles, is the Kaan's game
park — a forest so dense and so large that a man on horseback cannot cross it in a
single day. It is stocked with deer, boar, wild ass, bear, and leopard for the Kaan's
sport. At the center is a lake fed by a stream, and on the lake every species of
waterfowl known to man. The Kaan rides here with his falconers, his huntsmen, and his
cheetah-keepers. In one corner of the park, a herd of a thousand white mares is kept —
their milk is reserved for the imperial family alone, and the mares are so sacred that
no one of lower rank may ride them, touch them, or even walk in their pasture.

## ev.chandu.b.title

The Game Park

## ev.chandu.c.body

In a meadow at the northern end of the park, the monks of the Palace of the Spring tend
the sacred white mares. These animals are not ridden or worked. They are milked twice a
day, and the milk is carried in golden vessels to the Kaan's table. No one of lesser
rank may taste it. Once a year, on the day of the great Mongol festival, the Kaan
himself rides to the meadow and pours the milk on the ground as an offering to the
spirits of the Eternal Blue Sky and the Golden Earth — a rite that goes back to the days
when the Mongols were a tribe of wandering herdsmen on the northern steppe, long before
they ruled the world. No foreigner has ever been permitted to witness this ceremony.

## ev.chandu.c.title

The Meadow of the Milky Kine

## ev.chandu.entry.body

And when you have ridden three days from the city last mentioned, between north-east and
north, you come to a city called CHANDU, which was built by the Kaan now reigning. There
is at this place a very fine marble Palace, the rooms of which are all gilt and painted
with figures of men and beasts and birds, and with a variety of trees and flowers, all
executed with such exquisite art that you regard them with delight and astonishment.
Round this Palace a wall is built, inclosing a compass of 16 miles, and inside the Park
there are fountains and rivers and brooks, and beautiful meadows, with all kinds of wild
animals (excluding such as are of ferocious nature), which the Emperor has procured and
placed there to supply food for his gerfalcons and hawks, which he keeps there in mew.
Of these there are more than 200 gerfalcons alone, without reckoning the other hawks.
The Kaan himself goes every week to see his birds sitting in mew, and sometimes he rides
through the park with a leopard behind him on his horse’s croup; and then if he sees any
animal that takes his fancy, he slips his leopard at it, and the game when taken is made
over to feed the hawks in mew. This he does for diversion. Moreover [at a spot in the
Park where there is a charming wood] he has another Palace built of cane, of which I
must give you a description. It is gilt all over, and most elaborately finished inside.
[It is stayed on gilt and lackered columns, on each of which is a dragon all gilt, the
tail of which is attached to the column whilst the head supports the architrave, and the
claws likewise are stretched out right and left to support the architrave.] The roof,
like the rest, is formed of canes, covered with a varnish so strong and excellent that
no amount of rain will rot them. These canes are a good 3 palms in girth, and from 10 to
15 paces in length.
