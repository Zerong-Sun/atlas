## ev.ev_unique_mien.title

The Two Old Towers

## ev.ev_unique_mien.body

At two old towers near Mien, caretaker Nanda is sweeping leaves from a shaded doorway. Her niece asks why visitors always point to the taller tower first. ‘Because they see it first,’ Nanda says. The girl insists the smaller one has a better stone carving. Nanda asks her to show you the carving; once she does, a porter waiting nearby admits he walked past it last time.

You ask who cares for the towers. Nanda says several local families share the work, and she keeps a list so no one carries the heavy water jar twice in the same week. Her niece has begun writing visitors’ questions beside the chores. The porter is preparing to return toward the hills. He knows the land road to Sindafu but asks 2 silver for a guided account and a marked route sheet. Nanda asks him to check the marks against his own last trip, since one bend may have changed.

A visitor arrives with a small broom. The niece hands it to him and asks whether he wants to see her favourite carving before the sun moves off the wall.

## ev.ev_unique_mien.choice_a

Spend a day with Nanda’s family at the towers

## ev.ev_unique_mien.choice_a_result

You spend a day sweeping, carrying water and listening as Nanda and her niece compare what visitors have asked. The niece lets you read her new page before she puts it away. You leave knowing the family better.

## ev.ev_unique_mien.choice_b

Pay the porter 2 silver to check the road to Sindafu

## ev.ev_unique_mien.choice_b_result

You pay 2 silver. The porter marks the land route toward Sindafu and corrects the bend Nanda warned about. Her niece copies the change onto the tower’s visitor sheet so the next traveller can ask a better question.
