## city.mien.name

Mien

## ev.mien.entry.body

At a tea stall near the old road through Mien, owner May is washing clay cups while her brother counts sacks of rice. He says there are seven; she says the smallest sack is her mother’s and must not be sold. Their mother comes from the back with a bowl of cooked rice and asks whether the two of them have fed the porter yet. Neither has. The porter laughs and takes the bowl.

You ask how a traveller finds the town after the hill paths. May points to the houses beyond the trees. She says old buildings and lanes have changed hands over time, but families still meet here to trade and rest. Her mother has kept a notebook of the people who helped repair the tea stall after a hard season. She offers to show it to you if you stay a day.

A child runs in to ask whether an elephant at the far yard has really learned to open a gate. May’s brother says the gate has a loose latch. His mother tells him to go fix it before the animal proves clever again.

‘Did it open the gate?’ the child asks. May’s mother answers, ‘Go and ask An after he fixes it. He’ll tell you which bit of wood was loose.’ The child promises to come back with the whole story.

## ev.ev_mien_entry.choice_1_result

## ev.ev_gap_mien.title

At the Hill Path Water Stop

## ev.ev_gap_mien.body

At a water stop on the hill path, porter Ko is cooling a boiled bamboo flask in the shade. His younger sister wants to fill another from the stream at once. Ko asks her to wait until the water pot has boiled. She points out that a caravan is coming and they will need twice as much. He agrees and sets a second pot on the fire.

You ask how the path turns toward Sindafu. Ko shows you the river bend he uses as a landmark. His sister says a fallen tree now hides part of it from the road; she saw it yesterday. Ko had not been there since the tree fell, so he asks her to draw the new turn in the dirt. A caravan driver arrives and confirms her mark.

The driver’s mule is thirsty. Ko’s sister gives it the cooled water only after its owner lets it rest. Ko hands you the bamboo flask so you can feel that it is no longer hot, and asks whether you want the route recorded or a place beside the fire for a while.

‘You remembered the bend better than I did,’ Ko tells his sister. She points to the fallen tree: ‘I walked there yesterday. Next time, let me draw first.’ Ko moves the flask aside so the driver can see her map.

## ev.ev_gap_mien.choice.route

Record the hill road toward Sindafu

## ev.ev_gap_mien.choice.route_result

Ko’s sister shows you the turn around the fallen tree, and the driver confirms where the path rejoins the river. You mark the road toward Sindafu on your map. Ko sets a fresh pot on the fire for those coming after you.

## ev.ev_gap_mien.choice.scene

Sit with the family while the water cools

## ev.ev_gap_mien.choice.scene_result

You help Ko’s sister move the cooled flasks into the shade. When the caravan arrives, she serves the first drink herself. Ko thanks you for staying with them through the busy hour.

## ev.mien.gatelatch.title

The Elephant Yard Latch

## ev.mien.gatelatch.body

At the far yard, May’s brother An is fitting a new wooden latch while a young elephant tests the gate with its trunk. The animal’s keeper, Nanda, asks An to stop hammering for a moment so she can lead it to the shaded side. An asks whether the elephant actually learned the old latch. Nanda says it learned that a loose board gives way if pushed long enough. May’s mother arrives with a cup of tea for them both and says that is clever enough for her.

You ask what keeps the animal comfortable when the yard is busy. Nanda points to the water trough and the path the elephant uses to turn around. An realizes his new latch must not jut into that path. He takes it off and cuts it shorter. Nanda’s niece watches, then asks whether she may be the one to test it after the elephant has moved. An hands her the smooth end of the latch and asks her to wait for his signal.

The elephant has already turned toward its water. Nanda tells the girl to watch that path before touching the gate.

‘Wait until she has her drink,’ Nanda tells her niece. ‘I can wait,’ the girl says, watching the elephant lower its trunk into the trough. An uses the pause to smooth one more sharp edge.

## ev.mien.gatelatch.choice.test

Help the niece test the repaired gate

## ev.mien.gatelatch.choice.carve

Stay a day learning to carve the safe latch

## ev.mien.gatelatch.result.test

You and the niece open and close the gate while Nanda watches the elephant’s turning path. The latch holds and leaves enough space. May’s mother brings another cup of tea, this one for the niece who did the test.

## ev.mien.gatelatch.result.carve

You stay a day shaping a second latch with An. Nanda checks its edge against the animal’s path, and her niece learns to fit it without rushing. The first gate is finished before evening.
