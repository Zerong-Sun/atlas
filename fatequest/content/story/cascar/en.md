## city.cascar.name

Cascar

## ev.cascar.a.body

Cascar is the richest oasis on the Silk Road, where the melt-water from the Pamir and
the Karakorum pours down the mountain valleys to feed a green paradise in the heart of
the desert. Orchards of apricot, pomegranate, mulberry, walnut, and almond stretch for
miles along the irrigation canals; the vines grow so heavy with grapes that the
trellises must be replaced every year. The people are Muslims, but they keep many old
customs from before the time of the Prophet. The grapes of Cascar are dried into
raisins; the melons are the sweetest and largest in the world. A traveler who has
crossed the mountains and tasted the melons of Cascar for the first time will speak of
nothing else for a month.

## ev.cascar.b.body

The carpets of Cascar are woven so fine that a man can pass one through a finger-ring.
The women of the city work for months on a single carpet, drawing the pattern from
memory and knowing every dye by its smell: madder-root for red, indigo for blue,
pomegranate rind for yellow, walnut hull for brown, and a secret mineral from the
mountains for a green that fades only after a hundred years. The patterns are said to
contain prayers — a carpet woven with the right designs will protect its owner from the
evil eye, from snake-bite, and from the spirits of the desert. A merchant who buys a
Cascar carpet never travels without it, and the carpets that reach the courts of Europe
are treated as the treasure of kings.

## ev.cascar.c.body

Outside the city walls, in the face of a limestone cliff, there is a cave where seven
holy men are said to sleep. The Muslims say they have been sleeping since before the
time of the Prophet, waiting for the Day of Judgment. The Christians say they are their
own Seven Sleepers of Ephesus, miraculously transported here. A mosque and a chapel
stand side by side at the cave entrance, sharing the same cliff-face wall. Pilgrims of
both faiths come here, and neither side disturbs the other. The monks and the mullahs
take turns sweeping the cave; the offerings of candles, incense, and silver coins are
divided equally. It is said that anyone who stays in the cave overnight will hear the
breathing of the sleepers.

## ev.cascar.entry.body

Cascar is a region lying between north-east and east, and constituted a kingdom in
former days, but now it is subject to the Great Kaan. The people worship Mahommet. There
are a good number of towns and villages, but the greatest and finest is Cascar itself.
The inhabitants live by trade and handicrafts; they have beautiful gardens and
vineyards, and fine estates, and grow a great deal of cotton. From this country many
merchants go forth about the world on trading journeys. The natives are a wretched,
niggardly set of people; they eat and drink in miserable fashion. There are in the
country many Nestorian Christians, who have churches of their own. The people of the
country have a peculiar language, and the territory extends for five days’ journey.
