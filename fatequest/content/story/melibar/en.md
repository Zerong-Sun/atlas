## city.melibar.name

Melibar

## ev.melibar.entry.body

At Melibar, a pepper grower has brought a small sample to the market and left the rest of her sacks under a dry awning. Her son wants to open the largest one so buyers can smell it. “Let them smell this bowl first,” she says, pushing it toward him. A ship's cook tries a few grains and asks whether the next load will be ready before his boat leaves. The grower asks which boat he means; he points across the harbour. “Then ask your captain to wait until I have weighed it,” she answers. You ask where the road through town runs. The son points past the water stall and the shrine, then offers to introduce you to the pepper buyers if you stay. His mother tells him to cover the sample before he goes: a shower is gathering beyond the houses. The cook promises to return with his captain after the market has had time to hear the price. The grower asks the boy to keep the bowl inside until the captain comes back. He covers it with a clean cloth.

## ev.melibar.a.title

The Pepper Shed by the Harbour

## ev.melibar.a.body

A harbour watchman has taken a pepper sack off the wet stones and set it on boards beneath the shed. A young loader asks why he moved it when a boat is about to take cargo. “Because it will spend much longer on the boat than it spent here,” he says. The pepper seller arrives and checks the seam. You ask which vessels she sends toward Cail. She points out one loading beyond the fishers' boats, then asks the watchman whether the harbour entrance is clear. He says a larger ship is still turning and asks everyone to leave room at the quay. The loader lifts another sack without blocking the path. The seller asks you to watch the loading for a day if you want to understand how the dry sacks reach the ships. A pilot nearby offers to name the southern coast roads when he finishes bringing a boat in. The seller asks the loader to read the mark on the sack before it joins the others. ‘Cail,’ he says. She checks the board. ‘Good. Keep it with that boat's sacks.’ He carries it to the dry pile without blocking the quay.

## ev.melibar.b.title

A Pearl Seller after Sunset

## ev.melibar.b.body

On the steps near a harbour shrine, a pearl seller waits for her brother to bring a small lamp. She has a parcel of pearls to show a buyer, but the last light is too dim to judge them. “He went for the lamp before you came,” she tells you. A priest tending the shrine asks her to keep the steps clear for visitors, so she moves her basket aside. An older sailor sitting nearby points toward the water. “When the sky opens, you can find the northern star low over that mast.” You ask how he uses it. He shows you where the mast meets the horizon and says that he compares the star with a fixed point rather than guessing its height. Her brother returns with the lamp, carefully shielding the flame. The pearl seller opens the parcel and invites you to examine it while the sailor continues showing the younger boatmen how to sight along the mast. Her brother asks whether the pearls will look different by lamplight; she says the buyer can compare them again in the morning.

## ev.ev_melibar_a_followup.title

Melibar: The Pepper Seller’s Carriers

## ev.ev_melibar_a_followup.body

The pepper seller calls you back to the shed while the carrier she trusts is checking an outgoing boat. Her son has copied the cargo marks, but one sack on the board is listed for Cail and another for a nearer landing. “Do not swap them because they look alike,” she tells him. ‘Cail, and the nearer landing,’ he reads aloud. ‘Now which pile?’ his mother asks. He moves the sacks apart and points to the board before she lets him tie the first bag. The carrier offers a full sack for sale at the market's price. You ask where he will take the rest. He traces the coast toward Coilum and Maabar on a board, then asks the boy to show the first turn again. A boatman bringing in a wet rope calls from the quay that the next departure depends on the wind. The seller nods and tells her son to keep the sacks under cover until they know which boat is ready. The son asks whether the sacks may stay there overnight. His mother checks the roof for leaks before she promises.

The carrier marks the roads toward Coilum and Maabar, then the boatman explains which passage is open in the present wind. The boy repeats both directions until his mother is satisfied. You leave knowing the two destinations and how cargo reaches them.

## ev.melibar.seedlings.title

The Pepper Vines behind the Market

## ev.melibar.seedlings.body

Behind Melibar's market, a grower is tying young pepper vines to supports. Her nephew has pushed one support too close to an older root. “That vine has been here longer than you,” she tells him. He pulls the stake back and asks how to know where the older roots end. She loosens the soil with her fingers and shows him a fine pale root near the surface. You ask why the young vines are being moved today. “A neighbour gave us cuttings,” she says. “I promised her I'd look after them until they take.” The nephew carries a small pot of water between the rows, careful not to spill it on the market path. A woman from next door arrives to see where her cuttings went. She laughs when she sees the boy taking such care and asks whether they need another pair of hands. The grower offers you a place beside her nephew, or a seat to watch how they tend the vines through the day. The neighbour asks the boy which support he moved, and he proudly shows her the spot that no longer presses on the old root.

## ev.melibar.seedlings.choice.plant

Help set the young vine supports

## ev.melibar.seedlings.result.plant

You hold each support away from the older roots while the nephew presses it into place. The neighbour sees that her cuttings have room to grow and thanks you for helping the family keep its promise. The grower gives the boy the last stake to set on his own.

## ev.melibar.seedlings.choice.stay

Stay a day with the grower’s family

## ev.melibar.seedlings.result.stay

You stay through the day while the family waters and ties the young vines. At noon the neighbour brings rice and you eat together in the shade. The nephew returns the borrowed pot before evening. You leave rested, knowing the hands behind the pepper sold at the market.
