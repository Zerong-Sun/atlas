## codex.cx-road-23.name

How Barca and His Army Advanced to Meet Alau

## codex.cx-road-23.body

You read how Barca came on with three hundred and fifty thousand horse and
encamped on the plain within ten miles of Alau. He addressed his men,
declaring his intention of fighting after three days, and his confidence of
success: for they were in the right, and had fifty thousand men more than the
enemy.

## codex.cx-road-24.name

How Toctai Marched Against Nogai

## codex.cx-road-24.body

You record the usual description of their advance to meet one another, with
no circumstance singled out. Toctai is joined by the two sons of Totamangu
with a goodly company, and the two hosts encamp within ten miles of each
other on the Plain of Nerghi.

## codex.cx-road-25.name

Second Message That Toctai Sent to Nogai

## codex.cx-road-25.body

You note how the second embassy ends in open defiance: the messengers carry
a threat of attack should Nogai refuse to present himself before Toctai, and
Nogai refuses with defiance. Both sides prepare for war, and Toctai's force
is the greater in numbers.

## codex.cx-road-26.name

How Toctai and Nogai Address Their People

## codex.cx-road-26.body

The chronicle gives the whole of this in the usual formula, with no
circumstance worth transcribing; you set it down for the record all the
same. Two things stand out: Nogai's forces, though inferior in numbers, are
the better men-at-arms; and King Toctai shows great valour.

## codex.cx-road-27.name

How Alau Addressed His Followers

## codex.cx-road-27.body

Alau calls together a numerous parliament of his worthies and addresses
them. The chronicle keeps the old French phrase — he assembled again a
parliament of a great quantity of good men — and you copy it into your record
as the surest form of the fact.

## codex.cx-road-28.name

Great Battle Between Alau and Barca

## codex.cx-road-28.body

You find the great battle between Alau and Barca described in the usual
style, with nothing characteristic to single out; only the end stands clear
— the rout of Barca, and great slaughter. No circumstance of the field is
set down, and none is yours to invent.

## codex.cx-road-29.name

Christians Were in Great Dismay Because of What the Calif Had Said

## codex.cx-road-29.body

The Christians, hearing what the Calif had said, were in great dismay, but
they lifted all their hopes to God their Creator, that He would help them in
their strait. Their wisest took counsel together — among them a number of
bishops and priests — and found no resource but to turn to Him from whom all
good things come, beseeching Him to protect them from the cruel hands of the
Calif.

## codex.cx-road-30.name

One-Eyed Cobler Was Desired to Pray for the Christians

## codex.cx-road-30.body

When the vision had visited the bishop several times, he related the whole
matter to the Christians, and with one consent they called the cobbler
before them. They told him it was their wish that he should pray, for God
had promised to accomplish the matter by his means. He made many excuses,
declaring he was not so good a man as they represented; but they persisted
with so much sweetness that at last he said he would not tarry, but do what
they desired.
