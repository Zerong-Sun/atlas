## city.aden.name

Aden

## ev.aden.entry.body

At Aden's harbour, a boy is trying to pull a water cart between bales newly carried off a ship. His mother calls from the shade, “Go round the pepper, unless you want the whole cart smelling of it.” A spice seller lifts one bale so the boy can pass. You ask him where the road into town begins. “Behind the cloth stalls,” he says, “but it is easier to see when the market clears.” His mother gives you a cup of water and asks where you have come from. When you name your last stop, she recognizes a trader who buys water from her each season. “He still owes me a cup,” she says. The boy corrects her: “A whole jar.” They laugh, and she sends him toward the ships again. Beyond the market roofs, the mosque is open to visitors who will leave their shoes at the door. The seller points out both the through road and the lanes where sailors buy food before another crossing.

## ev.aden.a.title

The Pepper Seller’s Two Lists

## ev.aden.a.body

Under a harbour awning, a pepper seller has two lists spread beneath the same weight. One is for bags coming off an Indian Ocean ship; the other is for a caravan buyer who will take part of the cargo onward. His niece notices that a full trading sack with a torn corner appears on both lists. “Was it counted twice?” she asks. He shakes his head. “I meant to mark it for repair before anyone loads it.” She puts the bag aside and fetches a needle. You ask whether the caravan leaves by the same road as the coastal boats. “No,” he says, moving his finger over a map of the harbours and inland stops. “Ask each carrier what he takes from here. A destination on the list is not the whole journey.” The niece finishes sewing and asks you to hold the bag while she checks that no pepper escapes. The seller offers to introduce you to the carriers before they leave.

## ev.aden.b.title

The Customs Ledger and the Mosque

## ev.aden.b.body

At the customs desk above the harbour, an officer has set out a bowl of dates for the people waiting with cargo lists. Her daughter is learning to copy the marks on the forms. “This one says the incense seller came by sea,” the girl tells you. The officer checks the form. “It says he brought incense. Ask him how he came.” A merchant from across the water supplies the answer and opens the trade bundle so she can inspect it. He asks whether he may take some to his brother in Esher. The officer tells him to finish the fee and take the stamped paper with him. The call to prayer reaches them from the mosque nearby. She folds the merchant's form and gives her daughter the next one to read after the prayer. “One line at a time,” she says. The girl moves her bowl of dates away from the ink and asks if you will be there when she tries the next name.

## ev.ev_aden_entry.choice_1_result

The boy walks his cart to the corner and points you toward the road out of Aden. His mother calls him back before he can follow you farther: the next ship's crew has come for water. You continue, able to find your way through the harbour lanes.

## ev.ev_aden_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day in the market. The spice seller shows you where pepper is weighed, and the water seller introduces the carriers who take food to the boats. When her customer returns, the boy reminds him about the jar he owes. By evening you know the streets and the talk of Aden's merchants.

## ev.ev_aden_entry.choice_3_result

You leave your shoes at the mosque door and wait until the gathering has room for a visitor. An attendant shows you where to stand and thanks you for treating the place with care. Outside, the water seller points out the lane back to the harbour; you leave with the town's ways and turns in mind.

## ev.ev_aden_a.choice_1_result

You spend the day beside the handover. The niece asks you to hold each repaired bag while she compares the two lists, and by dusk she has corrected the duplicate mark. The seller explains how the pepper will pass from ship to caravan; later he asks you to stay for a second conversation with the carriers.

## ev.ev_aden_a.choice_2_result

You pay for a bag of pepper. The niece checks its seam in front of you and ties on a clean label bearing your mark. “Keep it above the wet baggage,” she says. The seller points out the carriers who handle the spice after it leaves Aden and invites you back to hear their plans.

## ev.ev_aden_a.choice_3_result

The seller names the road toward Dufar and the sea passage toward Cambaet. He asks his niece to show you how the two carriers mark their loads, and she makes you repeat the turns before she closes the map. You leave with a firmer idea of the next stages of travel.

## ev.ev_aden_b.choice_1_result

You stay for a day at the customs desk. The officer lets her daughter read the next cargo form aloud, then checks the goods against it. The girl corrects her own mistake before the merchant takes his stamped paper. By evening the officer knows you waited patiently and showed respect for the harbour's work.

## ev.ev_aden_b.choice_2_result

You pay the harbour fee and buy a full trade bundle of frankincense from the merchant. He ties it shut and tells you about the road to Esher, where his brother receives shipments. The customs officer gives him his paper, and you leave with the incense safely packed.

## ev.ev_aden_b.choice_3_result

You stand quietly outside the mosque during prayer. Afterwards the officer's daughter joins you in the shade and asks whether she read the merchant's name correctly. Her mother confirms it. You spend a little time talking with them before returning to the harbour, on easier terms with the people there.

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.title

Aden: The Carrier’s Turn

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.body

The pepper seller calls you back to the awning before the next load leaves. His niece has repaired the torn bag and is waiting beside a caravan master and a sailor. “Tell our visitor where you take the cargo,” the seller says. The sailor names the next coastal landing; the caravan master points to the inland road and asks what you plan to carry. You say you have not decided. “Then look at the load while it is still here,” the niece says. She opens one bag to show how tightly it must be packed and closes it again. The caravan master offers you a bag at his own price. The sailor spreads a route sheet on an empty crate, but the wind catches its edge. All three hold it down and begin arguing cheerfully about which departure will happen first. The niece asks which carrier will write the next cargo mark. Both men answer at once, and the seller hands her the pen.

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.choice_1_result

You pay the caravan master for his bag of pepper. The niece turns the seam toward you so you can inspect her repair, then ties your mark beside it. He loads the bag with your goods and explains how the spice is kept dry on the next stage.

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.choice_2

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.choice_2_result

The sailor marks Cambaet on the sea passage, and the caravan master points out Dufar along the coast. The niece adds the water stops each knows firsthand. You follow their marks with a finger and leave better prepared to choose a departure.

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.choice_3

Spend another day with the lighter crews

## ev.ev_aden_a_followup.choice_3_result

You spend a day seeing cargo pass from the large ship to smaller boats. The niece calls out each bag's mark, and the sailor answers when it is safely aboard. By evening you have heard the stages by which pepper leaves the harbour and why each handover needs a fresh count.

## ev.aden.letter.title

A Letter Waiting for a Boat

## ev.aden.letter.body

In a shaded lane, a mother is dictating a letter to her son while he tries to keep ink off his sleeve. Her brother sails with a coastal crew and has asked how their father is recovering. “Tell him Father is walking to the courtyard again,” she says. The boy writes that he is running. She leans over the page. “He is walking. Your uncle will picture him racing the goats.” The boy grins and crosses out the word. Their neighbour arrives with a basket and says the boat can take the letter if it is sealed before evening. The mother asks you to read it once, because the boy's final line has crowded into the margin. He is embarrassed until you point out that his uncle will enjoy the drawing of their courtyard. The neighbour waits at the door, wondering whether the family wants someone to carry the letter to the boat or help make a clean copy before he leaves.

## ev.aden.letter.choice.copy

Help the boy make a clean copy

## ev.aden.letter.result.copy

You sit with the boy while he copies the crowded final line onto a clean sheet. His mother reads it back and asks him to keep the little courtyard drawing. She gives you a copying fee, then seals the letter in time for the neighbour's basket.

## ev.aden.letter.choice.carry

Carry the sealed letter to the boat

## ev.aden.letter.result.carry

The boy hands you the sealed letter. You bring it to the boat before departure and see the sailor put it in a dry box with the crew's papers. The neighbour later tells the family you delivered it; when you return to the lane, the mother thanks you herself.
