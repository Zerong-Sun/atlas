## city.sapurgan.name

Sapurgan

## ev.sapurgan.entry.body

At a melon stall in Sapurgan, an older seller is stacking the fruit his daughter brought from the fields. Her little boy asks to keep the smallest one for his father, who is out with the pack animals. The seller says he may choose it after they count the load. The boy counts aloud, then discovers one melon still hiding beneath the cloth.

You ask where travellers fill their water skins. The daughter points to a working well beyond the stalls and says the carters use it before they leave. Her father adds that some stretches beyond the cultivated ground are dry, so each party checks the water it can carry. The boy asks whether a melon counts as water. His mother hands him a cup and says, ‘Drink this first. Then you can have a slice.’

The carters return with dust on their sleeves. The boy runs to show his father the small melon he saved. The seller offers you shade beside the stall for the day and has time to tell you which road the carters use.

## ev.sapurgan.entry.choice.look

Stay a day and hear the carters’ accounts

## ev.sapurgan.entry.choice.pass

Fill your water skin and continue

## ev.sapurgan.entry.title

Melons at the Sapurgan Roadside

## ev.ev_sapurgan_entry.choice_1_result

You fill your water skin at the working well. The seller’s daughter points out the road beyond the stalls, and you mark Sapurgan on your map before going on.

## ev.ev_sapurgan_entry.choice_2_result

You stay a day by the stall. The carters tell you where pasture gives way to dry ground, and the boy proudly shares the melon he saved for his father. You record their road accounts and the place you stopped.

## ev.sapurgan.site_gap.title

Two Copper Weights

## ev.sapurgan.site_gap.body

At the market scale, coppersmith Amina has set out two weights. Her brother says the old one still bears the right mark; she puts it beside the standard and shows how its rounded edge changes the balance. A neighbour carrying a sack of dried fruit asks which one they used when he sold his last load. Amina checks the date in her small book before answering.

You ask whether the neighbour lost money. He says the difference was slight but wants the next sale weighed fairly. Amina offers to calibrate the scale in front of him. Her brother wipes the green tarnish from the old weight and asks her not to throw it away. She says it can stay on the shelf as long as everyone knows which one now sets the measure.

The neighbour will soon join a caravan for Taykan and can name its stops. Amina has left the empty scale ready if you want to help her check the beam while the buyer watches.

## ev.sapurgan.site_gap.choice.route

Ask the neighbour about the road to Taykan

## ev.ev_sapurgan_site_gap.choice_1_result

The neighbour names the caravan stops toward Taykan. Amina’s brother points to the pass they use for copper loads, and you mark the road on your map while they finish weighing the fruit.

## ev.sapurgan.site_gap.choice.interact

Help Amina calibrate the scale

## ev.ev_sapurgan_site_gap.choice_2_result

You hold the empty pan while Amina sets the standard weight. The neighbour tests his fruit sack and agrees with the new measure. You remember how she checks a scale before a sale.

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.title

The Cup with a New Seal

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.body

Beside a public well, a woman named Maryam is carving a small mark into the handle of a wooden cup. Her mother had kept the cup for travellers to use, but its old mark wore away and two neighbours each thought it belonged to their own well. Maryam has asked them both to come and watch her cut a new sign. One arrives with a water jar; the other brings a piece of cloth to dry the cup.

You ask why the mark matters if everyone shares the water. Maryam says the cup must be cleaned and returned to the right shelf each evening. Her mother used to do it; now Maryam’s young son has that job and needs a mark he can recognise. The boy asks whether he may carve it himself. Maryam lets him try on a scrap of wood, then guides his hand on the handle.

The neighbours agree on the sign. Maryam needs someone to fetch a fresh leather cord from the market for 1 silver, or to stay until tomorrow and teach the boy how to keep the well shelf clean. The child holds the new cup up to the light.

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.choice.cord

Buy a new leather cord for the cup for 1 silver

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.result.cord

You bring back the cord, and Maryam ties the cup to the agreed shelf. Both neighbours see the new mark and thank you. Her son can now find the cup when he cleans the well at dusk.

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.choice.stay

Stay another day and show the boy how to tend the shelf

## ev.sapurgan.cupseal.result.stay

You spend the next day with Maryam’s son, washing the cup and keeping the shelf clear. His mother checks it at dusk, and the neighbours see that the shared cup is where they expect it. You leave with their well routine in mind.
