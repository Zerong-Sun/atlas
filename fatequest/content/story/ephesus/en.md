## city.ephesus.name

Ephesus

## ev.ephesus.entry.choice.pass

Thank Leyla and continue past the old stones

## ev.ephesus.entry.choice.rest

Stay a day with Leyla's family

## ev.ephesus.entry.body

The road climbs toward the houses on Ayasuluk Hill. Below, the older stones of Ephesus spread across the ground where streets once led to the sea. Leyla and her daughter Nisa are gathering fallen twigs beside the path. Nisa has found a smooth piece of marble and is trying to carry it with the firewood.

“That one will not burn,” Leyla says. Nisa knows. She wants to show it to her grandfather, who tells stories about the old town whenever he sees a stone with a carved edge. Leyla agrees to take the marble if Nisa carries the smaller bundle of wood.

They ask where you have travelled from. Leyla points out the path to the houses and the place where travellers can draw water. Nisa asks whether the stones in your own town are as white as this one. She holds it up beside your map, leaving a pale dust mark on her thumb.

## ev.ephesus.entry.title

The Houses Above Old Ephesus

## ev.ev_ephesus_entry.choice_1_result

You thank Leyla and continue past the old stones. Nisa holds up the marble to show you the carved edge she found. You mark Ephesus and the path up the hill on your map.

## ev.ev_ephesus_entry.choice_2_result

You stay with Leyla's family for the day. Nisa's grandfather washes the marble and shows her the tool marks along one edge. After supper he tells you both about the older streets below the hill, and Leyla gives you a place to sleep. You leave the next morning stronger and mark Ephesus on your map.

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.title

A Mason's Wooden Rule

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.body

A mason named Yusuf has laid stones beside a water channel near the houses. His apprentice Arda measures the next block with a worn wooden rule. They are making a low step so Yusuf's mother can reach the water without putting her jar on the ground.

Arda has marked the block too short. Yusuf asks him to measure again against the gap, and Arda sees where the rule slipped. “My grandmother will notice,” he says. Yusuf says she notices everything at the water place, including who has forgotten to eat.

A carrier who once took worked stone north toward Constantinople is waiting for the men to finish. He has time to tell you where he stopped on that journey while Arda makes a new line.

You ask whether Yusuf’s mother has asked for the step herself. ‘She says she can manage,’ Yusuf answers, ‘then leaves her jar halfway up while she catches her breath.’ Arda asks if she will be cross when she sees they changed the stones. Yusuf says she will probably test the step before giving an opinion. The carrier offers to bring a smaller flat stone from his cart. Arda measures it with the worn ruler and finds it fits the gap. He marks the edge again, more slowly, while Yusuf steadies the cord.

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.choice.talk

Help Arda measure the step again

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.choice.route

Listen to the carrier's northern journey

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.result.talk

You hold the wooden rule against the gap. Arda makes a new mark and cuts the block to fit. Yusuf's mother comes for water and tries the step with her jar. She thanks Arda first, then Yusuf. You add the water place and this part of Ephesus to your map.

## ev.gap.ephesus.scene.result.route

The carrier names the places where he stopped while travelling north toward Constantinople. Arda repeats the names under his breath as he works; he says he wants to see one of those towns someday. You mark Ephesus, Constantinople, and the route between them on your map. When the block is ready, the carrier helps set it in place before leaving.

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.title

The Oil Jar for Smyrna

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.body

At the foot of the hill, Marika and her son Petros are sealing jars of olive oil. Petros's older sister has gone to Smyrna to learn a trade. Her last letter said she missed the oil their mother pours on warm bread. Marika has saved a small jar for her, wrapped in cloth so the stopper will stay firm.

Petros wants to carry it himself. Marika points to the larger jars ready for sale and reminds him he has promised to help finish them first. He says he can do both, then gets oil on his sleeve while testing a lid. Marika hands him a rag and asks him to slow down.

One sealed jar is available for travellers to buy. Marika also asks if you will accompany Petros to the caravan stop the next day. He knows the stops toward Smyrna from his sister's letters and would be glad of company on the first stretch.

You ask whether the sister knows a parcel is coming. ‘She asked for a taste, not a whole jar,’ Marika says. Petros answers, ‘Then she can share it with the family who lets her work there.’ His mother checks the stopper and tells him to write her name on the cloth before the oil goes into anyone else’s load. He reaches for charcoal, then remembers his sleeve is still oily and asks you to hold the jar steady. Outside, a driver calls that he can wait until morning if the road is wet. Petros looks to his mother; she says to finish the labels tonight and decide about departure after they hear the weather.

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.choice.buy

Buy a sealed jar of olive oil for 120 silver

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.choice.walk

Walk with Petros to the caravan stop

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.result.buy

You pay Marika and take a sealed jar of olive oil. She checks the stopper once more before handing it over. Petros wraps his sister's smaller jar in fresh cloth and tells you she will put the oil on bread before reading the rest of his letter. You carry your purchase onward.

## ev.ephesus.olivejar.result.walk

You spend the next day walking with Petros. He tells you about the sister who taught him to read and the stopping places she named on the road toward Smyrna. At the caravan stop he finds a traveller going that way and gives her the small jar and his letter. You add Ephesus, Smyrna, and the route between them to your map. Petros heads home to help his mother with the larger jars.
