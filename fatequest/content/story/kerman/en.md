## city.kerman.name

## ev.kerman.entry.title

The Kerman Workshop Street

## ev.kerman.entry.body

At a Kerman workshop, a young embroiderer named Soraya holds a silk panel while her mother checks the stitches. Her brother arrives from the smiths’ lane with a new buckle for the pack saddle. He has bought the wrong size again.

‘Leave it by the door,’ their mother says. ‘The smith can exchange it after lunch.’ Soraya points out the market, the mosque, and the road to the hills. Turquoise traders, metalworkers, and needleworkers share the streets here; each family seems to know someone in the next shop.

Soraya’s brother asks if he should return the buckle himself. ‘Of course,’ his mother says. ‘Tell Rashid what you measured, not what you guessed.’ He looks at the saddle strap and admits he never measured it at all. You ask Soraya whether the embroiderers and smiths often work for the same caravans. ‘My mother sews covers for their packs,’ she says. ‘They repair our frames when the wood splits. If you stay, you’ll see half the street come through this door.’

## ev.kerman.a.title

The Smith and the Stone Seller

## ev.kerman.a.body

The buckle Soraya’s brother brought back belongs to a smith named Rashid. He sets out two sizes for the boy to compare. Beside his shop, a trader has a merchant parcel of rough turquoise from the hills and keeps turning one sample stone in the light.

Rashid says he can show you how he tests a saddle fitting. The trader will sell you the parcel, and the caravan masters in the lane can name the roads toward Yazd and Hormuz.

Rashid hands the boy a strap and asks him to find the worn hole. The boy finds two. ‘That is why your new buckle was too short,’ Rashid says. The stone trader asks whether you have come to buy or only to look. You ask how he knows the stones belong together in a trade parcel. He opens the wrapping and shows the marks he made while sorting them. ‘The buyer may check every piece,’ he says. ‘I would rather he did it here, before we tie the parcel again.’

## ev.kerman.a.choice.watch-forge

## ev.kerman.a.choice.ask-roads

Ask the caravan masters about Yazd and Hormuz

## ev.ev_kerman_a.choice_1_result

You stay at the forge for a day while Rashid fits a buckle to a leather strap. You record his method in your travel book. Before you leave, he invites you back to discuss the road’s demands on the metal.

## ev.ev_kerman_a.choice_2_result

You pay 100 silver for the parcel of turquoise and write down where the trader says it came from. Rashid sees you looking at his workshop and asks you to return when he has finished the next fitting.

## ev.ev_kerman_a.choice_3_result

The caravan masters name Yazd to the north and Hormuz toward the gulf. You mark both places on your map. They ask where you have travelled; after a long conversation, they treat you as a familiar traveller on this road.

## ev.kerman.b.title

Soraya’s Embroidery and the Falcon Mews

## ev.kerman.b.body

Soraya and her mother spread an embroidered cloth on their worktable. It has birds among branches, and the hem still needs finishing before a buyer arrives. A falconer comes in to collect a small cloth hood they have sewn for his bird.

He says you may visit the mews in the hills; he has a trained falcon for sale. Soraya shows you a full merchant bundle of embroidered silk if you wish to trade in cloth, or you can stay and watch how she finishes the hem.

Soraya’s mother asks the falconer if the hood sits properly over the bird’s eyes. ‘It does, but this tie is long,’ he says. Soraya folds the cord back and takes two quick stitches. You ask whether the silk bundle is all one pattern. ‘No,’ she says, opening a corner so you can see several colours. ‘A merchant can offer them in different towns. I would keep one length for someone I know.’ The falconer waits for his hood, listening to her explain the work.

## ev.ev_kerman_b.choice_2_result

You pay 100 silver for the merchant bundle of silk. Soraya wraps it securely and suggests keeping one length aside. On the road you give that length to a travelling companion whose coat has worn thin at the cuffs. She thanks you and helps tie the rest of the bundle more securely before the party moves on.

## ev.ev_kerman_b.choice_3_result

At the mews you pay 135 silver for a trained falcon. The falconer shows you how to hold its jesses and names Camadi beyond the hills. You mark the place on your map.

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.title

Rashid’s Saddle Fitting

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.body

When you return, Rashid has laid out a finished saddle fitting and a piece of worn leather. His niece rides with a caravan next month, and he wants her pack saddle to last the whole journey. An unworked merchant bundle of Damascus steel waits on the shelf beside him.

‘She would rather I made her a sword,’ he says. ‘She needs this more.’ He offers you the bundle at his usual price, can explain the road toward Hormuz, or would be glad if you stayed the day to help him test the straps and later spoke well of his work.

His niece arrives with the pack saddle and asks whether the new strap will slip when the animal climbs. Rashid makes her pull it herself. She braces a foot against the bench and tugs; the buckle holds. ‘That is what I wanted to know,’ she says. You ask what is in the bundle on the shelf. ‘Damascus steel for a trader,’ Rashid answers. ‘I can sell you the full lot. The smaller fittings for my niece are already spoken for.’ He turns back to her saddle, waiting to hear whether you need the road or the goods.

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.choice_1

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.choice_1_result

You pay 146 silver for the merchant lot of Damascus steel. Rashid checks the wrapping before handing it to you and shows you the piece from which he forged his niece’s buckle. You record what he taught you about the material and his forge.

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.choice_2_result

## ev.ev_kerman_a_followup.choice_3

## ev.kerman.patternbook.title

The Pattern for Soraya’s Brother

## ev.kerman.patternbook.body

Soraya’s brother has found an old cloth with a small bird stitched in one corner. He wants to copy it for their mother’s birthday, but the cloth is too worn to leave on the table for long. Soraya promises to show him the first stitches after her current order is done.

He asks you to sit with him while he draws the shape. You can spend the afternoon helping him make a paper copy, or walk with him to the shop that sells fresh thread and hear about the road to Yazd from its owner.

‘The wing looks like a leaf,’ the boy says, holding up his drawing. Soraya looks over and turns the old cloth a little: ‘See the tail? Leave room for that, or your bird will be sitting on its own feathers.’ He rubs out one line and asks you to hold the cloth steady. His mother calls from the other room to ask whether a customer’s blue cloth has been cut yet. Soraya quickly covers the drawing with a plain sheet. You can help finish it quietly, or take the boy out for thread while she distracts their mother.

## ev.kerman.patternbook.choice.copy

Help him copy the bird pattern

## ev.kerman.patternbook.choice.thread

Walk to the thread shop and ask about Yazd

## ev.kerman.patternbook.result.copy

You help him trace the bird’s outline. Soraya adds a few marks showing where to begin each colour, and her brother tucks the paper away for their mother’s birthday. The family thanks you warmly.

## ev.kerman.patternbook.result.thread

At the thread shop, the owner names the caravan that goes toward Yazd and shows you where it leaves town. You mark the route and Yazd on your map while Soraya’s brother chooses his colours.
