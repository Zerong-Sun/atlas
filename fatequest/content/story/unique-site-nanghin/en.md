## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.title

A Tax Clerk’s Three Receipts

## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.body

At Nanghin’s tax desk, clerk He has three receipts for the same bolt of silk. A dyer, a weaver and the final seller each kept one as the cloth changed hands. His apprentice thinks the total should be added three times. He asks her to read the names of the people who actually paid. She sees two entries refer to the same fee and starts a fresh line.

You ask whether traders need to carry every old receipt. He says it helps when the cloth returns for another repair, but the current charge should still be clear. A merchant waiting at the desk offers to show you the price he paid for the finished bolt. The apprentice checks that against the original thread cost; she finally understands why Fang the weaver keeps three columns.

He can let you spend a day studying the accounts. A stamped passage note costs 2.3 silver, and the clerk asks the apprentice to write the traveller’s name herself. She looks up from the page to make sure she has heard yours correctly.

## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.choice_a

Spend a day learning to read the tax accounts

## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.choice_a_result

You spend a day comparing the three receipts with the apprentice. She can tell which fees are new and which were merely copied. You leave with a clearer sense of how a trader’s costs change with each sale.

## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.choice_b

Pay 2.3 silver for a stamped passage note

## ev.ev_unique_nanghin.choice_b_result

You pay 2.3 silver. The apprentice writes your name, reads it back, and lets He stamp the note. Merchants waiting at the desk recognize that you took care to settle the record properly.
