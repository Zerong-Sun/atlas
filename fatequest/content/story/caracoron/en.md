## city.caracoron.name

Caracoron

## ev.caracoron.entry.body

At the wagon yard outside Caracoron, a carpenter is fitting a new wheel spoke while his sister holds the rim steady. A boy beside them wants to take the wagon to his grandmother before sunset. ‘It will travel faster once the wheel stays round,’ the carpenter tells him. The boy crouches to watch the last peg go in.

You ask whether the yard has been here long. The sister says families have used this ground through many changes of rulers and routes. Her mother still calls one lane by an older name, and travellers who ask for the market sometimes end up at the wrong gate. The carpenter laughs that his mother gives better directions once she knows which family the visitor wants. A neighbour arrives with tea and asks whether the boy has checked the other wheel too. He has not.

The sister invites you to stay a day and hear how the roads meet here. The boy takes the tea to his grandmother first, then comes back to hold the wagon while his uncle tests it.

The Wagon Yard at Caracoron

## ev.ev_caracoron_entry.choice_1_result

The carpenter’s sister names the road beyond the wagon yard. Her son points out the gate his grandmother still calls by its old name. You mark Caracoron on your map and go on.

## ev.ev_caracoron_entry.choice_2_result

You stay a day while the family repairs wagons and compares old and new street names. The boy takes the finished cart to his grandmother, then returns to show you the market gate. You add the city and their accounts to your book.

## ev.caracoron.site_gap.title

The Groom’s Horsehair Cord

## ev.caracoron.site_gap.body

Outside a camp tent, groom Altan is twisting washed horsehair into a thin cord. His daughter holds the loose end around a wooden peg. A young horse reaches toward her sleeve, and she pulls back just before it catches the cord in its teeth. ‘He likes anything that moves,’ she says. Altan asks her to keep the peg beyond the horse’s reach.

You ask what the cord is for. Altan says his brother’s saddlebag has torn, and the family will need it when they leave. His daughter asks whether a knot tied tight in dry weather will still hold after rain. He wets a short length so she can feel it draw in, then leaves a little slack in the practice knot. His brother brings the bag and thanks her for remembering the question.

Altan has travelled toward Camul with pack trains and can tell you the road. He also has spare strands ready if you want to try twisting while his daughter finishes the repair.

## ev.caracoron.site_gap.choice.route

Ask Altan about the road to Camul

## ev.ev_caracoron_site_gap.choice_1_result

Altan names the stopping places toward Camul and the ground where the herds find pasture. His daughter checks two names from her uncle’s last trip. You mark the route before they close the saddlebag.

## ev.caracoron.site_gap.choice.interact

Try twisting the spare horsehair

## ev.ev_caracoron_site_gap.choice_2_result

Altan shows you how to twist two strands so they hold one another. His daughter gives you the peg to keep the tension even. You learn the family’s repair method while his brother tries the mended bag.

## ev.caracoron.teapot.title

The Kettle Sent Across the Yard

## ev.caracoron.teapot.body

At a yard where several families have stopped, Bayan is heating tea in a dented kettle. Her father repaired the handle last winter, and she now wants to take it to an aunt whose wagon broke at the far gate. Her youngest son asks why they cannot wait until the aunt comes over. Bayan says the older woman has spent the morning under the wagon and deserves tea before she gets back up.

You ask whether the handle will hold. Bayan lifts the kettle by a cloth and shows you the loose pin. Her brother, a metalworker, arrives with a replacement pin but no one to steady the handle while he fits it. The child offers both hands; his uncle asks him to keep them away from the hot side. Bayan sets the kettle down and lets it cool.

A pin costs 1 silver at the market, or you can spend the following day helping the family mend the wagon and carry tea to the aunt. The son has already found a clean cup for her.

## ev.caracoron.teapot.choice.pin

Pay 1 silver for the replacement pin

## ev.caracoron.teapot.result.pin

You bring back the pin. Bayan’s brother fits it while you steady the cool handle, and the boy carries the first cup to his aunt. The families at the yard thank you for helping them finish the repair.

## ev.caracoron.teapot.choice.wagon

Spend another day helping repair the wagon

## ev.caracoron.teapot.result.wagon

You spend the next day beside the aunt’s wagon, handing tools and holding the wheel steady. Bayan brings tea at midday; her aunt finally sits down to drink it. You remember how the family shared the work.
