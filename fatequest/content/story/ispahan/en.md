## city.ispahan.name

Ispahan

## ev.ispahan.entry.title

The Street by the Friday Mosque

## ev.ispahan.entry.body

The lanes around Isfahan’s Friday Mosque are full of work. A dyer carries folded cloth into the shade, and a boy runs after her with the wooden clips she forgot. Beyond the houses, water from the Zayandeh River feeds gardens and workshops.

The dyer, Parvin, says the boy is her nephew. ‘He remembers every customer’s name and never remembers the clips,’ she says. The boy catches up and hands them over. ‘I remembered today,’ he protests. Parvin counts them, nods, and points out a place where you can rest or continue toward the market.

You ask whether the cloth is for a customer nearby. ‘My sister lives beyond the city,’ Parvin says. ‘She sent the measurements for a wedding cloth and then sent another message because the bride changed her mind about the blue.’ Her nephew asks whether they must dye it again. Parvin unfolds one corner and shows him that the darker border is already there. He leans close and admits he had only looked at the middle. A neighbour calls from the water channel to ask when her own linen can be rinsed. Parvin says after this length is hung, then asks the boy to keep the clips where she can reach them.

## ev.ispahan.entry.choice.pass

Continue toward the market

## ev.ispahan.entry.choice.rest

Spend a day resting nearby

## ev.ev_ispahan_entry.choice_1_result

You follow Parvin’s directions through the lanes and mark Isfahan on your map.

## ev.ev_ispahan_entry.choice_2_result

You stay in the shade for the day. Parvin’s nephew brings water after his last errand. You feel stronger when you leave, with Isfahan marked on your map.

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.title

Parvin’s Blue Cloth

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.body

Parvin lifts a blue cloth from a washing tub. Her sister ordered it for a wedding in another town, and the cloth must be dry before the carriers leave. Her nephew brings a fresh bucket from the channel, careful now to keep the wooden clips tucked into his belt.

‘The colour still comes away on my fingers,’ Parvin says. ‘Another rinse, then we can hang it.’ Her nephew tries to lift the far end alone, and Parvin tells him to wait until someone can take the other side. A caravan master bound for Basra has a courtyard nearby; she has spoken to him this morning.

You ask whether the bride has chosen the darker edge herself. Parvin says her sister asked the girl to come and look at two samples before deciding; she chose this one and wrote her name on the back of the order. The nephew wants to find that paper so they can check the colour again. Parvin tells him to keep his hands dry before he touches it. The neighbour from the channel arrives with her linen and offers to help hold the line when the blue cloth goes up. Parvin thanks her and tests the rinse water one more time.

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.choice.talk

Help rinse and hang the cloth

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.choice.route

Ask for the Basra caravan

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.result.talk

You and her nephew lift the cloth between you. Parvin checks the water and finally says it is clear enough. She clips one end to the line and leaves the other to him, watching as he fastens it securely. You mark her workshop in Isfahan on your map.

## ev.gap.ispahan.scene.result.route

Parvin tells you which courtyard the caravan master uses and where the road turns west toward Basra. You mark the route and the city while her nephew repeats the courtyard name so you can find it.

While you ask for the caravan courtyard, Parvin and her nephew finish the last rinse and clip the blue cloth to the line. The neighbour holds the rope steady. The cloth is ready to dry before its later carrier arrives.

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.title

A Note Sewn into the Hem

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.body

Parvin has finished the blue cloth, but her sister’s letter to the bride has arrived too late for the first carrier. Her nephew suggests sewing it into the hem before the next caravan leaves. Parvin worries that the bride might never think to look there.

The boy finds a reel of red thread and asks whether one bright stitch will make the letter easy to find. Parvin takes the letter and reads the bride’s name aloud, checking it against the order before anyone sews.

You ask whether the first carrier has already left. ‘At dawn,’ Parvin says. ‘My sister’s letter came after he closed his pack.’ Her nephew says he could run to the caravan courtyard before supper, but Parvin asks who will watch the cloth while she finishes the wrapping. The neighbour who helped with the washing passes the door and offers to stay a moment. The boy looks at you, then at the letter: ‘If it goes separately, tell the carrier whose hands should receive it. My aunt writes neatly, but he has many families’ letters.’ Parvin folds the paper once and keeps the address facing out.

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.choice.carry

Carry the letter to the Basra caravan

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.choice.stitch

Help mark the hem with red thread

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.result.carry

The caravan master places the letter in a separate pouch and tells you when his people will take the road toward Basra. You mark that route and destination. Parvin’s nephew runs back to tell her the letter is safe.

## ev.ispahan.clothnote.result.stitch

You hold the hem while Parvin sews a red stitch beside the folded letter. Her nephew writes the bride’s name on the wrapping. Parvin thanks you; the family speaks of your care to their neighbours.
