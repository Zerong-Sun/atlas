## city.coigangiu.name

Coigangiu

## ev.coigangiu.entry.body

At the canal landing in Coigangiu, a boatman is setting a low bench beneath the shade of a willow. His father used it to mend nets, and now the old man wants it moved away from the damp bank. ‘Here the legs will stay dry,’ the son says. The father sits down to test it and finds one foot rocking on a stone. You lift that corner while the son clears the stone away. A fish seller comes by with a covered pot of hot broth for the boat crews. She asks whether the old man will take a bowl. ‘After I wash my hands,’ he says, showing the cord he has just been repairing. The seller sets the pot on a flat plank and tells you there is room beside him if your journey has been long. You ask how to cross town; the son points to the road beyond the landing and names the next gate. His father asks you to sit for a little while and listen to the canal workers before setting off again.

## ev.coigangiu.entry.choice.rest

Stay a day to rest by the landing

## ev.coigangiu.entry.title

The Boatman’s Resting Bench

## ev.ev_coigangiu_entry.choice_1_result

The old boatman settles on the steady bench while the seller serves him broth. His son points out the street beyond the landing. You cross town with its gate and waterside path clear in mind.

## ev.ev_coigangiu_entry.choice_2_result

You stay another day. The fish seller brings food at noon, and the old boatman lets you rest beside him while he finishes mending the cord. You eat, sleep well, and leave with renewed strength after the son shows you the road out of town.

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.title

The Fish Basket in the Shade

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.body

A fisher is moving her baskets beneath the shade of a boat shed. Her nephew has lined one with dry reeds, but he packed them so tightly that there is no room for the fish. ‘Leave a little space near the top,’ she says. He takes out a handful and asks why the basket lid must stay loose. She lifts the lid and lets him feel the warm air trapped beneath it. You help set another basket on a low plank, clear of the sunlit stones. A traveller pushing a small handcart stops to buy fish for a family near the road toward Paukin. He asks if they will last until evening. The fisher chooses the coolest basket and tells him to keep it beneath the cart cloth. The nephew asks how far the road goes before the first hamlet. The traveller points past the canal bridge and describes the turn. The fisher says he can mark it for you while she finishes the baskets.

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.choice.route

Ask the traveller about Paukin

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.choice.route_result

The traveller draws the land road toward Paukin on your board, beginning at the canal bridge. The nephew repeats the turn while he helps tie the basket to the cart. The fisher sends them off with the fish kept beneath the shade cloth.

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.choice.scene

Help line and shade the fish baskets

## ev.ev_gap_coigangiu.choice.scene_result

You set the baskets on the plank while the nephew spreads a thinner layer of reeds inside each one. The fisher checks that air can pass beneath the lid. He carries the last basket into shade himself and thanks you for showing him where to put it.

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.title

The Missing Sums

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.body

A child at the landing is searching every plank of his father’s small boat for a sheet of arithmetic work. He used it in the morning and thinks he left it beneath the seat. His father needs the boat for work, but waits while the boy looks. ‘I promised the teacher I would bring back the sums,’ the child says. You ask whether he took the paper ashore. His older sister remembers carrying it to the fish seller so she could check a number in the family’s account. The seller brings it from a high shelf, where she kept it clear of splashes. One corner is damp and a number has blurred. The boy wants to guess what he wrote. ‘Tell the teacher what happened,’ his sister says, ‘and I can read the earlier account with you.’ Their father asks whether you can walk with her now. The teacher will also be at the school tomorrow and can show the boy how to copy a damaged page while keeping the old one. The boy holds the sheet by its dry edge until his sister is ready to leave.

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.choice.return

Carry the sums to the teacher

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.result.return

You walk with the sister and carry the sheet past the crowded fish stalls. The teacher checks the legible sums, asks the sister about the blurred number and lets the boy explain the damp corner himself. He dries the page before putting it with the other work, and the family thanks you for bringing it in.

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.choice.wait

Return a day later for the copying lesson

## ev.coigangiu.tablet.result.wait

You return the next day. The teacher lays the dry page beside a new sheet and has the boy copy the numbers he can still read. His sister brings the family account so they can check the blurred one. Their father sees the corrected sum before taking the boat out.
