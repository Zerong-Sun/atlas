## city.ctesiphon.name

Ctesiphon

## ev.ctesiphon.entry.title

At the Great Arch

## ev.ctesiphon.entry.body

The old brick arch rises above low walls and open ground. A family has set a shade cloth beside the ruins. A girl sits beneath it with a bowl of mortar, helping her father patch their small shelter.

She says her grandmother used to walk farther than anyone in the family and always stopped here to drink before taking the road to Baghdad. The girl has never made that whole journey. She asks you how far a traveller can go with a full skin of water, then laughs when her father reminds her that everyone drinks at a different pace.

She asks her father where the family will put the second water jar once the shelter wall is finished. ‘Inside the shade, where the clay stays cool,’ he says. You ask whether they sell water to travellers. The girl shakes her head: neighbours share the well, and her father offers a cup when someone stops. He tells her to rinse the cup first; she runs to the jar and returns with it dripping. From the road, a cart driver calls a greeting. She recognises him and asks whether his mother has recovered from her cough. He promises to tell her after he has watered the animals.

## ev.gap.ctesiphon.scene.title

Bricks for the Shelter

## ev.gap.ctesiphon.scene.body

The girl’s father, Farid, sorts new bricks beside the shelter. One has a crack through its middle. He sets it aside for paving the threshold instead of putting it in the wall.

His daughter complains that her grandmother would have spotted the crack first. ‘Probably,’ Farid says. ‘She also made me carry every brick I chose badly.’ He asks you to hand him a sound brick. The girl is already asking whether you have come from Baghdad.

You ask why he needs the doorway finished today. Farid says a neighbour will bring a woven mat tomorrow, and there must be a level place to set it. His daughter asks whether she can make the threshold brick herself. ‘You may choose its place after it dries,’ he says. She picks up the cracked brick and turns it in her hands. The split would catch a bare foot if he laid it as it is, so Farid shows her which edge to smooth. The neighbour arrives with a sample of the mat and asks them to measure the opening once more.

## ev.gap.ctesiphon.scene.result.talk

You hold the brick while Farid settles it into the mortar. His daughter presses a wet thumbprint into an unused scrap and announces that this one is hers. Farid promises to put it by the door once it dries. You mark their shelter beside the old arch on your map.

## ev.gap.ctesiphon.scene.result.route

Farid takes you to a rise beyond the fallen wall and points northwest, toward Baghdad. The girl repeats the turns so you will remember them. You mark the road and city on your map.

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.title

Grandmother’s Door Brick

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.body

Farid’s daughter has found a brick with her grandmother’s old mark on it. She wants it at the new doorway, where everyone can see it, but the stone step has to be levelled first. Farid can do that after his work tomorrow.

‘She used to stand there and make us wash our feet before coming in,’ the girl says. Farid brings out a length of cord to measure the step. His daughter keeps the marked brick beside her and asks whether he remembers which room Grandmother carried it from.

‘From the little room behind the oven,’ Farid says. ‘She said the mark would help us find it again.’ The girl asks whether the oven still stands. It does, but the room around it has been repaired more than once. You ask how the brick survived. Farid says his mother kept useful pieces under the shade cloth until she had a place for them. The girl rubs dust from the old mark and asks him to leave that side facing the doorway. A neighbour calls from the lane that the mat is ready; Farid tells her to bring it tomorrow, once the mortar has had a night to set.

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.choice.fit

Spend a day helping fit the step

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.choice.listen

Sit and listen to her stories

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.result.fit

You spend the day levelling the step with Farid. His daughter places the marked brick at the edge and walks through the doorway twice to test it. The family thanks you warmly, and your name becomes familiar here.

## ev.ctesiphon.shelter.result.listen

She tells you how her grandmother once arrived home with a soaked loaf hidden under her coat, after giving her dry bread to another traveller. Farid laughs and brings out tea. You rest in the shade and recover some strength.
