## city.suju.name

Suju

## ev.suju.entry.body

At a canal bridge in Suju, silk weaver Wen is trying to carry a folded length of cloth without letting it brush the damp railing. Her son wants to take the shorter end, but that leaves the long end dragging. ‘Hold it near the middle,’ she tells him. He changes his grip and they cross with the cloth clear of the stone.

You ask where the workshop street begins. Wen points beyond the bridge. Her mother sells medicinal roots in a nearby garden and has asked the boy to bring lunch when the cloth is delivered. He asks whether she will still have time to show him the fishpond. Wen says his grandmother usually finds time if he arrives before she closes the water gate. A neighbour asks them to stop at the little shrine on the bridge street; its caretaker needs a fresh mat carried in.

Wen lays the cloth on a dry bench and checks both edges. She offers to show you the market and workshop over a day, while her son counts how many folds stayed clean.

## ev.suju.a.title

The Gold Thread at Wen’s Loom

## ev.suju.a.body

In Wen’s workshop, her younger sister is adding gold thread to a flower woven into silk. She stops after three passes and asks whether the bright part is large enough. Wen holds the cloth toward the window. ‘One more petal,’ she says. Her sister asks why they do not run gold thread across the whole bolt. Wen shows her how the flower would become heavy and how much thread they would need. The sister tries the extra petal herself.

You ask where finished silk travels. A merchant has a full bale for 150 silver and plans to send goods toward Kinsay. Another customer knows the road to Chinghianfu and is waiting for a sample. Wen asks him not to touch the cloth until her sister has secured the final thread. He agrees and offers to show you his road notes.

The younger sister holds her work at arm’s length. She asks Wen whether the new petal catches the light; Wen answers by moving it to the window again.

## ev.suju.b.title

The Garden Behind the Looms

## ev.suju.b.body

Behind the workshop street, Wen’s mother Auntie Lu is clearing leaves from a garden channel. Wen’s son arrives with lunch and asks whether the fishpond is still open for him to see. ‘When this gate is clear,’ she says. He starts gathering leaves but scoops up a little fish with them. Lu tells him to lower the basket into the pond and let it swim out. The boy does, then asks whether the fish will remember him. She says it will remember the water better.

You ask what grows beside the pond. Lu keeps ginger for the kitchen and a full trade bale of rhubarb roots for sale at 100 silver. A neighbour going toward Chinginju is checking her garden notes. Lu asks the neighbour to point out where the road leaves the canal, while she shows the boy how the water gate moves.

The fish escapes the basket. Wen’s son calls his grandmother over to see, and she lets him open the gate a hand’s width.

## ev.ev_suju_a_followup.title

The Silk Order by the Canal

## ev.ev_suju_a_followup.body

Wen’s younger sister has finished another flower, this time keeping the gold thread lighter at the edge. She asks you whether the difference is visible. Her mother, Auntie Lu, has come from the garden with a neighbour who wants cloth for a family gift. Wen asks the neighbour to describe the recipient’s taste before anyone names a price. The woman says her sister likes plain colour with one small bright detail. The younger weaver points to the new flower and asks if it will do.

You ask where Wen sends her full bales. She has another silk trade bale for 100 silver. A merchant bound for Kinsay is checking his cart, while a customer from Chinghianfu wants the road name written clearly on a sample tag. Wen lets her younger sister write it and asks you to read it back.

Auntie Lu sets a bowl of water on the bench. The neighbour looks at the flower in daylight before answering the weaver.

‘The light detail is just here,’ the younger weaver says. The neighbour nods and asks to hold the cloth by the plain edge.

## ev.suju.bridgeplank.title

The Bridge’s Loose Plank

## ev.suju.bridgeplank.body

At the bridge near Wen’s workshop, a plank over a narrow repair gap has come loose. A carpenter named Bao has set a warning basket beside it, but Wen’s son keeps trying to step across to reach his grandmother’s garden faster. ‘The other side is only three steps away,’ the boy says. Bao asks him what happens when the plank moves under a load of silk. The boy thinks of the cloth he carried with his mother and takes the longer safe path.

You ask when the plank can be fixed. Bao has the right wood, but needs another pair of hands to hold it while he fastens it. A fish seller waiting on the bridge says she can help after she delivers her basket; the fish will not wait in the sun. Wen’s son offers to carry that basket to the shaded stall, with the seller walking beside him. Bao measures the gap again and finds a small stone caught under one edge. He asks you to keep the warning basket in place while he lifts the plank.

‘Will you still come to the pond?’ Wen’s son asks the fish seller. She says she will, once her fish are out of the heat.

## ev.suju.bridgeplank.result.fix

You hold the plank while Bao removes the stone and fastens the wood. The fish seller returns after her delivery to test it with her empty basket. Wen’s son crosses by the safe path and thanks you for finishing the repair.

## ev.suju.bridgeplank.result.day

You spend a day checking the bridge with Bao and marking two other places that need attention. The fish seller brings water for the workers after her delivery. Wen’s son learns how to spot a loose plank before he steps on it.
