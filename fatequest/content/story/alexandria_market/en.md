## city.alexandria.market.name

Alexandria · Harbour Market

## city.alexandria.market.desc

Browse imported cargo and small keepsakes. Prices and cargo space are shown beneath each item; you are free to look without buying.

## market.alexandria.item.frankincense

The seller opens a pouch of pale resin. Ask where the cargo came from before mistaking an imported scent for a local harvest.

## market.alexandria.item.myrrh

Dark resin lies beside the lighter incense. The merchant offers a trade parcel, not a single pinch.

## market.alexandria.item.damascus-steel

A blade catches the light. The stall calls it Damascus steel; the name on a stall is not proof of a blade’s origin.

## market.alexandria.item.baghdad-brocade

Folded brocade takes more room than a bead. Picture it in your pack before paying.

## market.alexandria.item.persian-carpet

The seller unrolls a corner to show the weave. A carpet is cargo for a planned journey, not a light souvenir.

## market.alexandria.item.rosewater

A stoppered bottle is offered for inspection. Keep it apart from paper and cloth in your imagination of the pack.

## market.alexandria.item.dates

These dates are sold as a cargo parcel. The market price is for trade goods, not the meal shared at a host’s table.

## market.alexandria.item.arabian-horse

A horse belongs to the merchant’s trade stock. Buying this cargo does not select a transport mode for the next road.

## market.alexandria.item.glass-lamps

Light passes through the glass walls. The parcel is bulkier than the single bead on the next tray.

## market.alexandria.item.paper

Sheets are stacked into a travel parcel. A blank page could become a letter, a copied route or a merchant’s account.

## market.alexandria.item.tabriz-pearls

The trader calls these pearls a Tabriz consignment. The trading name tells you a claimed route, not where a pearl grew.

## market.alexandria.item.glass-bead

A single glass bead with a hole for a cord: a modest keepsake, sold individually and carried in one cargo slot.
