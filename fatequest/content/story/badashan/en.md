## city.badashan.name

Badashan

## ev.badashan.entry.body

A stone dealer at the Badakhshan market has his son count the bundles on a felt rug. The boy puts a blue lapis parcel beside a box of red stones, and his father moves it back. ‘Those belong on separate pages,’ he says. A visiting buyer asks why. The dealer opens his ledger: one party carried the blue stones down from the high valleys; another brought the red ones.

The son asks whether he may write the buyer’s name. ‘When she agrees to a price,’ his father says. The buyer examines a stone and asks to see it in daylight. You hold the edge of the felt while they take the box toward the doorway. The dealer asks where you have travelled from. When you answer, he names a lodging house used by people on that road.

The boy comes back and reads the figures aloud, stumbling over one line. His father lets him correct it. You can mark Badakhshan and go on, spend a day listening to the stone traders, or visit a nearby place of worship and meet its caretakers.

## ev.badashan.a.title

The Blue Stone Ledger

## ev.badashan.a.body

At a highland stone market, the broker Farid is arguing gently with his sister over a lapis lot. She says the wrapping has rubbed through on one corner and wants him to reduce the price. He says the stone itself is sound. You ask to see the parcel opened, and they place it on felt where the light reaches the blue surface.

Their mother, who knows the road down from the Kokcha valley, arrives with the porter’s tally. ‘You forgot the extra mule,’ she tells Farid. He checks the figures and admits she is right. His sister points to a yellow fleck in the lapis and asks whether you recognise it. Farid says miners call it part of the stone’s natural pattern; he warns the porter not to knock the parcels together.

The family has a full merchant lot of lapis for 135 silver. You can spend a day learning how they examine and record it, buy the lot and hear which way their carriers go toward Balkh, or ask the mother about the mountain approaches toward Taican and Kashmir.

## ev.badashan.b.title

The Clerk’s Different Names

## ev.badashan.b.body

A clerk is writing visitors’ names when his niece asks why the same valley appears under two spellings in his books. He takes down an older volume and says the travellers who gave him the names spoke in different languages. A court messenger waits nearby with a document that needs a careful copy before he can leave.

The clerk reads out a local family’s claim that its rulers descend from Alexander and the daughter of Darius. His niece asks whether the written claim proves every name in the line. ‘It tells us what they say about their family,’ he answers. The messenger asks them to finish his copy before debating the past. You help hold the pages flat while the clerk writes.

The niece wants to learn how to greet the people bringing stones from the hills. The clerk can teach you a few useful words for 2 silver; he also knows a licensed broker offering one uncut balas ruby for 135 silver. You can spend a day hearing the family account, pay for the language lesson, or buy the stone.

## ev.ev_badashan_a_followup.title

Farid’s Second Ledger

## ev.ev_badashan_a_followup.body

Farid opens a second ledger when the porter returns from the lower road. His sister notices one parcel has been marked twice: once for the buyer who changed his mind and once for the new offer. ‘If we leave both names, someone will come back for a stone we no longer have,’ she says. Farid crosses out the old entry and asks his mother to check the carrier’s route.

She finds a water stop omitted from the margin. You ask whether that matters to the price. ‘It matters to the people carrying it,’ she replies. The porter points to Balkh on a rough map and explains where he pays for animal feed. Farid has a second merchant lot available for 135 silver if you want the whole shipment.

His sister dries the corrected ink while you decide. You can buy the lot, ask them to trace the road toward Balkh, or stay the day until the broker’s report is complete and study how the family settles a disputed entry.

## ev.ev_badashan_b_followup.title

The Clerk’s Mountain Words

## ev.ev_badashan_b_followup.body

The clerk’s niece brings you a note with three words she heard from stone carriers. She has written each as it sounded to her, then left room for the clerk’s correction. A messenger from the next valley is waiting; he can speak the words aloud before he takes his letter.

The first word names a pass. The clerk asks the messenger whether it is the approach toward Taican or the one travellers use for Kashmir. The messenger draws two lines on a scrap and says the roads divide after the pasture. You repeat his words, and he grins at your careful accent. The niece asks him to say them more slowly for both of you.

The clerk can give you a short lesson for 2 silver, with a chance to practise among the local traders. You can instead ask the messenger to show the two mountain directions, or buy one uncut balas ruby for 135 silver while the broker is still in the yard.

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.title

The Colour in the Bowl

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.body

At a small workshop, a painter is grinding a pinch of blue pigment while his daughter holds a bowl of water. She wants to colour a wooden panel for her grandmother, but this batch has turned too pale. Her father asks where she put the darker powder. ‘I used it on the border,’ she says, showing him a row of careful blue marks.

A neighbour comes to borrow the grinder and offers another pinch of pigment. The painter thanks her, then asks his daughter whether she wants a darker panel or would rather keep the colour she has made. She looks at the border again: ‘Grandmother will know I painted it.’ You ask how the blue powder reaches the workshop. The neighbour says stone dealers bring it down from the high valleys, and the painter shows you how he keeps different batches apart.

You can spend the day helping the girl finish the panel and carry it to her grandmother, or stay for a short demonstration of grinding and mixing the colour before you travel on.

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.choice.finish

Stay a day and help finish the panel

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.result.finish

You help finish the panel and carry it to her grandmother. The older woman holds it near the light, recognises the border at once, and thanks you for coming with her granddaughter. The family speaks well of you afterward.

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.choice.learn

Watch how the painter mixes his blue

## ev.badashan.bluepigment.result.learn

The painter lets you feel the difference between a coarse grain and a fine one, then shows how little water the powder needs. You write down the method while his daughter returns to her panel.
