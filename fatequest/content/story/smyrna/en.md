## city.smyrna.name

Smyrna

## ev.smyrna.entry.choice.pass

Thank Aysel and continue along the bay

## ev.smyrna.entry.choice.rest

Stay a day at Aysel's house

## ev.smyrna.entry.body

The road reaches Smyrna beside a wide bay. Aysel is rinsing a basket at the water's edge while her daughter Mina keeps a smaller one out of reach of the waves. They brought figs down from the hillside this morning; Mina insists the small basket was full too, though only three figs remain.

“I gave two to the boatman,” she says. Aysel asks about the other three. Mina admits she ate them on the way. The boatman calls from his boat that he received only one. Mina looks at her mother and begins counting again.

Aysel offers you a fig and points out the lane toward the houses above the water. She asks which way you are travelling. Mina adds that the boats know the bay better than anyone, but her mother knows which footpath is shortest. Both wait to see which one you ask.

## ev.smyrna.entry.title

At the Bay of Smyrna

## ev.ev_smyrna_entry.choice_1_result

You thank Aysel and continue along the bay. Mina finally decides that the missing fig fell from the basket; the boatman says he will watch for it. You mark Smyrna and the waterfront path on your map.

## ev.ev_smyrna_entry.choice_2_result

You stay with Aysel's family for the day. Mina shows you the hillside path, then helps her mother dry the baskets. The boatman appears at supper with the missing fig, now rather bruised; Mina gives him a fresh one instead. Aysel shares dinner and a place to sleep. You leave the next morning stronger, with Smyrna marked on your map.

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.title

The Net for Tomorrow's Catch

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.body

At the shore, fisherwoman Dora is mending a net. Her younger brother Theo holds the line while she replaces a torn mesh. Their father has gone to sell today's catch, and the two of them want the net ready before he comes back.

Theo says he can mend it faster. Dora gives him a needle and watches him make one knot. It is tight enough, but he has tied two meshes together. He sighs and begins again. “Our father taught me when I was your age,” Dora says. “I did exactly that.”

Dora can show you where the fishing families work along the bay. Their father once carried fish north toward Constantinople, and Theo knows the stops from hearing the story so often.

You ask Theo who tied the knots in the older part of the net. ‘Father did, before he left this morning,’ he says. Dora points to a patch where she made the same knot last year and asks him to compare their size. Theo tries again with a shorter loop. ‘That is close,’ she says, ‘but pull it once before you call it finished.’ A gull lands beside the fish basket, and Theo waves it away with his free hand while keeping hold of the line. Dora tells him that is exactly why two people mend a net together.

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.choice.talk

Watch Theo finish the torn mesh

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.choice.route

Hear Theo tell his father's northern journey

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.result.talk

Theo unties the extra loop and makes a new mesh. Dora pulls it gently to test it, then lets him finish the next one himself. She walks you past the nearby sheds and points out where the fish sellers gather. You add this part of Smyrna to your map before their father returns.

## ev.gap.smyrna.scene.result.route

Theo recounts his father's journey north, naming each place where the fish was sold or the boat stopped. Dora corrects him when he swaps two towns. You mark Smyrna, Constantinople, and the route between them on your map. Their father returns in time to hear Theo pronounce the last stop correctly.

## ev.smyrna.grapes.title

Grapes for the Brother at Sea

## ev.smyrna.grapes.body

On a terrace above the bay, sisters Esma and Lale are turning grapes that have lain on woven mats for several days. Their older brother sails with a small boat and asked for dried grapes he can keep in a bag. Esma wants to send him the largest bunches. Lale says the small ones are nearly ready, and he leaves in two days.

Their mother comes out with bread and asks why one mat has not been turned. The sisters explain at once, each talking over the other. Their mother takes a grape from each pile, tastes them, and says they can send both. Esma turns the large bunches; Lale shows her how to leave space for air between them.

The family sells some of its dried fruit to travellers. They will sell you a small packet today, or welcome another pair of hands to turn the grapes for the rest of the day and tomorrow morning.

You ask whether their brother knows they have saved him the first dried batch. Esma says she told him to bring a clean bag; Lale says he will forget it and use his cap. Their mother hears both and brings out a cloth pouch she has already sewn. ‘He can keep the cap on his head,’ she says. Lale asks whether the pouch should be tied tight before they finish drying. ‘Not while the fruit is warm,’ their mother answers. Esma holds up a handful for you to feel; it is still soft in the middle. The sisters spread it back on the mat and make room for the breeze.

## ev.smyrna.grapes.choice.buy

Buy dried grapes for 0.8 silver

## ev.smyrna.grapes.choice.stay

Stay until tomorrow and help turn the grapes

## ev.smyrna.grapes.result.buy

You pay for a packet. Lale checks that the grapes inside are dry and ties the cloth closed. Esma says their brother will get a larger bag, with both sorts mixed together. You eat a few on the road and regain strength. The sisters go back to their mats, now both full.

## ev.smyrna.grapes.result.stay

You spend the day helping Esma and Lale turn the grapes. Their mother feeds you supper and gives you a place to sleep. At dawn Lale tests a small bunch and decides it is ready; Esma fills the bag for their brother. He comes by on his way to the boat, tastes one grape, and says he hopes the voyage lasts long enough to eat them all. You leave after breakfast. The family remembers your help when you pass this terrace again.
