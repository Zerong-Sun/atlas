## city.pein.name

Pein

## ev.pein.entry.body

On the riverbank at Pein, an elderly stone picker is washing two pale pebbles in a shallow bowl. Her grandson calls both of them jade. She makes him dry them before deciding what to write on their tags. ‘Your uncle will ask where each one came from,’ she says. ‘If you mix them up, he has to walk back to the river.’

You ask the boy whether his uncle trades the stones. He says the uncle cuts some for local buyers and sends the rest with caravans. The old woman points to a cotton stall across the path. Her daughter works there and keeps the family’s accounts while the boy goes out with the pickers. ‘She knows what I brought in yesterday better than I do,’ the woman says. The daughter hears her and calls back that yesterday’s count was three stones, not four. The boy finds the fourth pebble in the water bowl.

A caravan is preparing to leave the bank. The woman wraps the two stones separately and asks whether you have already settled where you will find water next.

## ev.pein.entry.title

At the Riverbank in Pein

## ev.ev_pein_entry.choice_1_result

The old woman points out the wells used by travellers leaving Pein. You thank the family, mark the town on your map and continue with the caravan.

## ev.ev_pein_entry.choice_2_result

You stay a day and watch the family sort stones, sell cotton and settle their count before sunset. The daughter explains which marks on a bundle tell her who packed it. You add Pein and what you learned to your travel book.

## ev.pein.site_gap.title

The Salt Seller’s Dish

## ev.pein.site_gap.body

A salt seller named Rustam has set two pinches on a clay dish, one grey and one bright. His daughter reaches for the bright pile. He stops her gently: ‘That one belongs to the family who brought it here. We have to finish testing before they take it home.’ She says their camel has been waiting since morning. Rustam looks at the animal chewing dry grass and admits she is right.

You ask what the test is for. He mixes a pinch of each salt with clean water and lets the grit settle. His wife, who keeps the measures, checks the grey pile and says it will do well for the neighbour’s cooking. The daughter asks if the pale one is better. ‘For this buyer, perhaps,’ her mother says. ‘But first let us tell him what is in his own sack.’

The buyer comes back with a water jug. Rustam puts the dish where all of them can see it, and his daughter holds the camel’s lead while they talk.

## ev.pein.site_gap.choice.route

Ask Rustam how caravans reach Yarcan

## ev.ev_pein_site_gap.choice_1_result

Rustam names the wells used on the route to Yarcan. His daughter corrects the order of two stopping places she visited last month, and he lets her draw them on your map.

## ev.pein.site_gap.choice.interact

Help the family compare the two salts

## ev.ev_pein_site_gap.choice_2_result

You hold the dish while Rustam’s wife shows the buyer the grit left in each cup. The family closes the sale with everyone clear about the measure. You remember the simple test they used at Pein.

## ev.pein.jarnames.title

Two Names on a Water Jar

## ev.pein.jarnames.body

At the potter’s yard, a young woman named Amina is tracing two names onto a fired water jar. Her father hands her a brush and asks which name belongs nearest the handle. ‘Both should be easy to see,’ she says. The jar is for her sister and the sister’s new household. They have agreed to share water from one well while the younger family builds its own storage.

You ask why she has written the names herself. Amina says her sister cannot come to the yard today: she is repairing the roof before the next rain. Their aunt, who promised to collect the jar, arrives without a cart and laughs at how large it is. Amina says they can carry it together, but the brushwork must dry first. Her father offers a small wage if someone takes it across town after the writing sets.

The aunt sits in the shade and tells Amina to finish the second name slowly. She wants the sister to see that the whole family was thinking of her when the jar arrived.

## ev.pein.jarnames.choice.deliver

Deliver the jar after the writing dries

## ev.pein.jarnames.result.deliver

You wait for the lettering to dry, then carry the jar with the aunt across town. Amina’s sister sees both names and calls for her family to bring water. Her father pays you 0.8 silver for the delivery.

## ev.pein.jarnames.choice.stay

Stay a day to help Amina finish the household marks

## ev.pein.jarnames.result.stay

You spend the day helping Amina mark smaller cups for both households. Her aunt takes the large jar home, and Amina sets a cup aside for her sister’s first meal beneath the repaired roof. You remember their care for one another.
