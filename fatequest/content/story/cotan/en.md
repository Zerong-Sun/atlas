## city.cotan.name

Cotan

## ev.cotan.a.body

From the mountains above Cotan, two rivers carry down stones of jade — the white jade
that is like congealed mutton-fat, and the green jade that is like the first leaves of
spring after a long desert winter. The men of Cotan wade into the cold water in autumn,
when the rivers run low, and feel for the stones with their bare feet. When they find
one, they dive and bring it up. The largest stones are sold to the Kaan's agents, who
send them to Cambaluc to be carved into cups, bracelets, and belt-buckles for the court.
A fine piece of mutton-fat jade is worth more than its weight in gold. All the jade that
reaches the Great Kaan's court in Cambaluc — and all that reaches Persia, India, and the
West — comes from these two rivers.

## ev.cotan.b.body

The country around Cotan is planted with mulberry trees as far as the eye can see. The
women of the city spin silk finer than any in Cathay — a single thread, finer than a
human hair, can run for a thousand paces without breaking. The silk is dyed with saffron
for yellow, indigo for blue, madder for red, and cochineal for crimson. The looms are
worked by two women sitting opposite each other; they pass the shuttle back and forth
while singing a song that keeps the rhythm. A bolt of Cotan silk large enough to make a
robe sells for its weight in silver in the bazaars of Cambaluc. The Kaan's
tax-collectors demand silk as tribute, and the weavers pay it willingly, for the Kaan's
agents pay more than any merchant.

## ev.cotan.c.body

Beyond the living city, on the old river terrace where the water no longer flows, the
sand has buried an older Cotan — a city of a thousand monasteries, a hundred stupas, and
more painted caves than any man could visit in a lifetime. The wind uncovers fragments
day by day: a painted face of a bodhisattva with kohl-rimmed eyes, a hand raised in
blessing, lotus petals in gold and vermilion, halo-fires of indigo blue. The old people
say the dust of these walls is holy — they mix it with water and drink it as medicine.
The archaeologists of the court in Cambaluc send expeditions to catalogue the ruins, but
the desert reclaims whatever they uncover within a season. The Buddha Dust blows east on
the wind that crosses the Taklamakan.

## ev.cotan.entry.body

Cotan is a province lying between north-east and east, and is eight days’ journey in
length. The people are subject to the Great Kaan, and are all worshippers of Mahommet.
There are numerous towns and villages in the country, but Cotan, the capital, is the
most noble of all, and gives its name to the kingdom. Everything is to be had there in
plenty, including abundance of cotton, [with flax, hemp, wheat, wine, and the like]. The
people have vineyards and gardens and estates. They live by commerce and manufactures,
and are no soldiers.
