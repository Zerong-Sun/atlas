## city.cotan.name

Cotan

## ev.cotan.a.body

At a shallow bend near Khotan, river washer Yusuf tips a tray of pebbles into the sun. His daughter Amina is sorting them by the marks she can see, but she has put a cracked stone with the smooth ones. ‘Turn it once more,’ he says. She finds the fine line only when water runs across it. A merchant waiting by the bank asks whether they have another full jade lot to sell; Yusuf says one sealed package is ready at 135 silver.

You ask how they decide where to search. Yusuf points to the changing current, then asks Amina where she found today’s stones. She remembers the gravel bar but not the exact place. He takes her to the bank to look at the water from above before either of them steps in. An older washer calls that he can show you what his hands learned here if you stay until the light changes.

Amina returns with a small dry cloth. Her father waits while the merchant checks the seal, and the older washer sets an empty tray beside you.

## ev.cotan.b.body

In a Khotan weaving room, sisters Farah and Zuleika are finishing a length of silk for market. Farah reaches for a blue thread that should mark the edge and finds an empty bobbin. Zuleika says she wound another one after breakfast; their aunt has moved it to the shaded shelf so the colour would not fade. ‘I told you,’ the aunt says. Farah answers that she had heard, then forgot while counting the warp.

You ask how much cloth is ready to sell. The aunt points to a complete trading bolt priced at 100 silver, separate from the piece the sisters are still making. She also has a small packet of dye samples for 1.2 silver: indigo, madder, safflower and walnut, all labelled so travellers will not mix them. Zuleika asks whether the samples need extra wrapping. Her aunt says to keep them dry first, then worry about the sun.

Farah finds the blue bobbin and asks you to watch the edge for a day if you want to learn how the sisters divide the work. Zuleika shifts her seat to make room.

## ev.cotan.c.body

Beyond a watered field outside Khotan, farmer Rahman is clearing sand from the low wall that holds his channel in place. His mother brings him a painted pottery fragment she found after the last wind. ‘It came from our field,’ she says, ‘and I kept it so the children could see the colour.’ Rahman wants to set a support beside the wall before the next water turn. His mother warns him not to place a heavy stone over a patch of older brick that has begun to crumble.

You ask what stood here before the fields. She has heard neighbours talk of older religious buildings in this part of the oasis, but she knows only the pieces found by their family. The younger child asks whether the painted curve is a hand. She says it might be; the piece is too small to tell. Rahman asks you to help measure the firm ground where a support can stand.

Rahman sets his measuring cord against the firm ground. His mother gives the children a clay cup to carry water and asks them to stay clear of the wet wall.

## ev.cotan.entry.body

At Khotan’s entry yard, clerk Samira is trying to keep a wet cargo register from curling. Her younger brother brings it inside, but a river washer follows him with two pebbles in his palm. ‘You have written my name beside the merchant’s pack,’ he tells her. Samira checks the marks. The merchant owns the sealed jade lot; the washer merely helped carry it from the riverbank. She asks both men to sign the corrected line.

You ask where travellers rest. Samira points to houses near a water channel and says the river washer can show you the safest place to fill a jar today. The merchant opens a corner of his pack and offers a full trading lot of jade for 135 silver. Her brother asks if the stone is all from this river. ‘Different hands have gathered it,’ the merchant answers, ‘and that is why we keep names.’

Samira invites you to stay two days and learn the register. Her brother wants to show you their mother’s collection of old river stones tomorrow. The washer holds out a jar of clear water; Samira’s brother takes a sip, then asks for the path to the channel.

## ev.ev_cotan_consequence_a.title

The Washer’s Carrying Mark

## ev.ev_cotan_consequence_a.body

The river washer from the entry yard calls you back after two days at Samira’s register. He is Yusuf’s cousin Harun, and he has found that two loads carried the same chalk mark after the rain. ‘One was ours to carry,’ he says. ‘The other belongs to a family across the channel. If a chest goes missing, I do not want them looking in the wrong house.’

You ask whether the family can tell the loads apart. Harun brings out a narrow strip of cloth tied to one handle. Samira’s brother says he saw that strip when the cart first arrived. The other family’s daughter comes over with their own receipt and points to a small tear in the paper. Harun remembers that the cart wheel brushed it against the wall. Their stories fit, but Samira asks them to keep both receipts until the next station has the correct names.

Harun offers to write an introduction to the river carriers who will receive the goods ahead. The daughter asks whether you can help spell her mother’s name on the outside.

## ev.ev_cotan_consequence_b.title

A Weaver Asks About the Channel

## ev.ev_cotan_consequence_b.body

After your visit to Samira’s family, a weaver named Farah asks whether you saw the channel by their house. Her aunt has heard that a shallow patch may leave grit in the water used for rinsing silk. ‘The river washer says it looked clear yesterday,’ Farah says, ‘but our new white cloth has a faint line.’ She holds up the edge so you can see it. Her sister Zuleika thinks the line may be from the old dye tub instead.

You ask whether they can test the water before washing another length. Farah fills one jar at the channel and another at the yard well. Zuleika puts a plain thread in each. Samira comes to check the jar labels; she once confused two cargo marks and does not want this family to mix their samples. The sisters wait while the threads dry. Their aunt asks them to write the time beside each jar, so they can check the water again tomorrow.

Farah asks if you want a copy of their test. Zuleika suggests writing down the time and which jar came from where, so the next weaver can compare it later.

## ev.cotan.mentor_closure.title

## ev.cotan.mentor_closure.body

At a well beside the river road, keeper Mahmud has laid out three clay cups. His daughter says travellers keep confusing the cup for rinsing hands with the ones for drinking. ‘Then I should mark the handles,’ he says. She points out that the cups have no handles. He laughs and asks her to find a piece of coloured cord instead. A river washer arrives to fill a jar and waits until they finish.

You ask how much water is left after the washing families draw their share. Mahmud shows you the depth mark inside the well wall and explains who checks it each morning. His daughter says yesterday’s mark was hard to see in the shade, so she has placed a small lamp nearby. Mahmud asks her to move it farther from the rope before someone knocks it in. The washer offers a stone to keep the lamp steady.

Mahmud invites you to stay a day and watch how the well is shared. The daughter has already tied a blue cord around the drinking cup and wants you to tell her whether it is easy to see.

## ev.cotan.twoscripts.title

## ev.cotan.twoscripts.body

The seller wrote one name in the family’s hand and another in the script the next carrier can read. Her grandson thinks they name two different owners. ‘Look at the pack number,’ Dilara says. He finds the same mark beneath both lines and asks why the names still look so different.

A carrier arrives to collect the cloth and says he can wait while the boy practises one line.

Dilara offers to pay 0.8 silver if you spend the day helping her check other cargo labels. Her grandson also invites you to sit for a short lesson and see how he writes his aunt’s name.
