## city.tauris.name

Tauris

## ev.tauris.a.body

Tauris is the greatest commercial city of the Ilkhanate — a crossroads where Persian,
Turkish, Armenian, Georgian, and Frankish merchants trade the goods of two continents.
The city has outgrown every set of walls ever built around it, and its suburbs now
stretch for miles across the plain, a patchwork of orchards, workshops, gardens, and
caravanserais. The Ilkhan's summer palace stands on a hill to the south, surrounded by
gardens of cypress and rose watered by qanats that bring snow-melt from the mountains.
From the palace rooftop, the whole plain of Tauris is visible — a green island in the
brown Persian plateau, dotted with the smoke of a thousand cooking-fires.

## ev.tauris.b.body

Every nation has its own quarter in Tauris and nearly every foreign merchant-house keeps
an agent here. The Genoese fondaco stands near the Great Bazaar, a fortress-like
building where the Latin merchants store their goods, sleep, and pray. The Armenian
caravanserai by the east gate receives the overland caravans from Trebizond and the
Black Sea. The Moslem traders occupy the covered market, selling silk from China and
Cathay, pearls from the Gulf, and cloth of gold from Baghdad. A merchant who knows four
languages — Persian, Turkish, Arabic, and Italian — can make his fortune in Tauris in a
single caravan season. Every bale that passes through pays a tax to the Ilkhan.

## ev.tauris.c.body

Outside the city, the gardens of Tauris stretch for miles along the qanats that bring
water from the mountains. The great lords of the Ilkhanate — Persian viziers, Mongol
noyans, Armenian merchants, and Turkish amirs — have their summer pavilions here: open
halls with tiled fountains, shaded by plane trees and cypress, surrounded by orchards of
apricot, pomegranate, almond, and pistachio. The air smells of jasmine, rose, and
watered earth. The fruit is the finest in Persia, and the gardeners have a saying: 'A
man who drinks the water of Tauris and eats its apricots will never want to leave.' Many
foreign merchants have found this to be true.

## ev.tauris.entry.body

Tauris is a great and noble city, situated in a great province called YRAC, in which are
many other towns and villages. But as Tauris is the most noble I will tell you about it.
The men of Tauris get their living by trade and handicrafts, for they weave many kinds
of beautiful and valuable stuffs of silk and gold. The city has such a good position
that merchandize is brought thither from India, Baudas, CREMESOR, and many other
regions; and that attracts many Latin merchants, especially Genoese, to buy goods and
transact other business there; the more as it is also a great market for precious
stones. It is a city in fact where merchants make large profits. The people of the place
are themselves poor creatures; and are a great medley of different classes. There are
Armenians, Nestorians, Jacobites, Georgians, Persians, and finally the natives of the
city themselves, who are worshippers of Mahommet. These last are a very evil generation;
they are known as TAURIZI. The city is all girt round with charming gardens, full of
many varieties of large and excellent fruits. Now we will quit Tauris, and speak of the
great country of Persia. [From Tauris to Persia is a journey of twelve days.]

## ev.ev_tauris_a_merchants_followup.body

The foreign merchants do not merely name places. One points south toward Ctesiphon, another warns that the road to Baudas follows a different rhythm of tolls and water. Their answers are useful only if you press them for the detail that belongs to your own journey.
