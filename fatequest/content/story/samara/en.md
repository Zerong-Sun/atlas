## city.samara.name

Samara

## ev.samara.entry.body

Samara — a kingdom on the northern coast of Sumatra, with its own king and its own
language. The people eat rice and fish; the forests are full of elephants and
rhinoceros. Ships from China and India call here for fresh water and fruit. The king
claims descent from Alexander the Great; his palace is built of teak and decorated with
Chinese porcelain.

## ev.samara.a.title

Samara: The Watering Ground of the Long Ships

## ev.samara.a.body

On the shore where the long ships put in for water, the shoremen of Samara draw your boat up on the sand and ask your business. They say the bay is busy when the monsoon turns, and that pepper and camphor are carried down from the hills to these sheds between the tides. The roads inland are few, and the men who know them charge nothing to name them — only an ear for the telling.

## ev.samara.a.choice.1

## ev.samara.a.choice.2

## ev.samara.a.choice.3

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.title

Samara: The Shoremen's Tide Book

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.body

In the shade of the sheds an old shoreman who has counted forty monsoons opens his tide book and lays it on the sand. He says a ship that misses the turning tide waits half a month, and a traveller who misses the road into the hills waits longer — the pepper comes down by the bundle, and the camphor only when the moon is right. He asks what your cargo will bear.

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_1

Buy camphor sealed for the monsoon road

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_1_result

He sells you camphor at his fixed price, sealed in leaf. The cargo will hold to the next monsoon port.

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_2

Ask the tide and the days to the next harbour

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_2_result

He reads the days to the next harbour from his book: the tide, the passage, the weather. Pentam and Cail are marked on your map.

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_3

Sit with the shoremen till the tide turns

## ev.ev_samara_a_followup.choice_3_result

You sit with the shoremen through the turn of the tide. The talk is of ships and cargoes, and in Samara your name now carries a little more weight.
