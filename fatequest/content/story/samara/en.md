## city.samara.name

Samara

## ev.samara.entry.body

At the landing on Sumatra’s northern coast, a boatman named Ismail is loading fresh-water barrels while his mother sells fruit beneath a palm-leaf shade. His little daughter wants to mark each barrel with a strip of red cloth so the crew will know which are full. Ismail says the empty ones should go back to the well first. The girl marks those with a knot in the rope instead.

You ask when the boat leaves. Ismail says the captain is waiting for a change in the wind and will decide after speaking with the pilot. His mother calls him over to eat rice and fish while it is still warm. ‘The barrels will wait for your bowl,’ she says. He checks the last stopper, then sits beside her. A trader from another ship asks for two pieces of fruit and wants to know where the well is. The daughter offers to show him after lunch.

The family has a dry corner where you can rest for a day. If you go on now, Ismail can still point out the safe path along the landing before the tide reaches the steps.

## ev.samara.entry.choice.pass

Ask Ismail about the landing and continue

## ev.samara.entry.choice.rest

Rest a day with the family by the landing

## ev.samara.entry.title

Water Barrels at Samara

## ev.ev_samara_entry.choice_1_result

Ismail points out the higher path along the landing. His daughter calls the full barrels by their red cloth marks as you pass. You mark Samara on your map and continue.

## ev.ev_samara_entry.choice_2_result

You rest for a day beneath the family’s shade. Ismail’s mother shares rice and fish, and his daughter shows you the well after she finishes marking the barrels. You leave rested and add the landing to your map.

## ev.samara.site_gap.title

The Shipwright’s Wind Strips

## ev.samara.site_gap.body

Shipwright Lela has tied three coloured strips to a rope beside a mast. Her son says the blue one is fluttering toward shore, so the boat can leave. She asks him to look at the waves beyond the reef as well. He goes to the landing and comes back less certain. A pilot waiting with them says the wind may change before the tide turns.

You ask why Lela keeps the strips if the pilot still has to judge the water. She says they help the younger crew remember what changed during the day. Her brother brings in a wet sail and asks where to hang it. Lela points to the open shed; folded while damp, the cloth and stitching will wear badly. Her son helps carry it, then asks whether the next voyage might reach Chamba.

The pilot names the coastal stops on that sea passage. He tells the boy where ships wait for a favourable wind and how to recognize the harbour at Chamba. Lela dries her hands on a cloth and invites you to see the old sail’s seams while the wind shifts.

## ev.samara.site_gap.choice.route

Ask the pilot about the sea passage to Chamba

## ev.ev_samara_site_gap.choice_1_result

The pilot shows you the sea passage to Chamba and names the harbours where a ship can wait for wind. Lela’s son marks the first stretch on your map, and the pilot adds the final approach to Chamba.

## ev.samara.site_gap.choice.interact

Help Lela hang and inspect the old sail

## ev.ev_samara_site_gap.choice_2_result

You help Lela spread the old sail in moving air. She points out a worn seam and shows her son how to follow the old needle holes. You remember how this family prepares a boat before the wind turns.

## ev.samara.ropebasket.title

The Rope Basket at Low Tide

## ev.samara.ropebasket.body

Near the landing, rope maker Nila is untangling a basket of mooring lines for her sister’s fishing boat. The younger sister says she coiled them neatly last night. Nila pulls out a loop with sand packed into it and asks where the basket stood. ‘On the lower step,’ her sister says, just as the tide begins to cover it again. They both lift the basket onto the dry wall.

You ask why the lines must be sorted before the boat returns. Nila says her sister’s husband will need them as soon as he brings the catch in, and the oldest cord has frayed near the knot. Their mother arrives with a bundle of palm fibre and offers to twist a new length. The sister says she can make it herself if Nila shows her how much fibre to use. Nila hands her a short practice piece.

A neighbouring captain will pay 0.8 silver if someone brings the finished rope to his boat today. Nila can also spend the following day teaching you to test the old lines with the family. Her mother sits beside the basket and starts twisting the first strands.

## ev.samara.ropebasket.choice.deliver

Deliver the finished line to the captain

## ev.samara.ropebasket.result.deliver

You take the new line to the neighbouring captain. He tests the knot against a cleat and pays you 0.8 silver. Nila’s sister waves from the shore while her own fishing boat comes in.

## ev.samara.ropebasket.choice.learn

Stay until tomorrow and learn to inspect the lines

## ev.samara.ropebasket.result.learn

You spend the next day with Nila and her family, testing old lines and twisting a short new one. The fishing boat returns with its catch, and her sister uses the safer cord you helped prepare. You leave with a better eye for worn rope.
