## city.baldacum.name

Baudas

## ev.baldacum.a.body

The great palace of the Caliph of Baghdad covered more ground than any building in
Islam. Its golden dome could be seen three days' journey away across the flat plain of
the Tigris. Within were halls hung with silk embroidered with gold thread, gardens
watered by channels drawn from the river, courtyards paved with marble brought from
every corner of the Caliphate, and the treasury that held the tribute of a hundred
nations. The Caliph himself was never seen except by a handful of servants and his
vizier. His harem contained a thousand women; his guard was an army of twenty thousand
Turkic slaves. And yet when Hulagu came to the walls, all that gold and all those swords
could not save him.

## ev.baldacum.b.body

Merchants from India, Persia, Syria, and the Levant crowd the covered markets of Baudas.
The bazaars are organized by trade — the silversmiths in one vaulted lane, the
silk-merchants in another, the spice-sellers in a third — and each guild maintains its
own mosque, bathhouse, and coffee-house. Silk and cloth of gold from Mosul, spices from
Hormuz and India, pearls from the Gulf, paper from Samarcand, and Damascus steel blades
change hands by the hour. The money-changers sit cross-legged behind low tables covered
with mountains of silver dirhams; the merchants drink sherbet cooled with snow brought
down from the Kurdish mountains and argue over fractions of a grain of gold.

## ev.baldacum.b.title

The Bazaars of Baudas

## ev.baldacum.c.body

The story of how Hulagu took this city is still told in the bazaars. The Caliph,
trusting in his gold and his twenty thousand guards, refused to pay tribute to the Great
Khan and defied the Mongol envoy. Hulagu marched on the city with a host that blackened
the plain from horizon to horizon. He broke the dikes of the Tigris and flooded the
Mongol camp, but his engineers diverted the water and drained the Caliph's moat. When
the walls fell, Hulagu shut the Caliph in his own treasury — a tower full of gold,
silver, jewels, and silk — and told him to eat his treasure. The Caliph starved among
his wealth, and the city burned for a month.

## ev.baldacum.c.title

The Fall of the City

## ev.baldacum.entry.body

Baudas is a great city, which used to be the seat of the Calif of all the Saracens in
the world, just as Rome is the seat of the Pope of all the Christians. A very great
river flows through the city, and by this you can descend to the Sea of India. There is
a great traffic of merchants with their goods this way; they descend some eighteen days
from Baudas, and then come to a certain city called KISI, where they enter the Sea of
India. There is also on the river, as you go from Baudas to Kisi, a great city called
BASTRA, surrounded by woods, in which grow the best dates in the world. In Baudas they
weave many different kinds of silk stuffs and gold brocades, such as _nasich_, and
_nac_, and _cramoisy_, and many another beautiful tissue richly wrought with figures of
beasts and birds. It is the noblest and greatest city in all those regions. Now it came
to pass on a day in the year of Christ 1255, that the Lord of the Tartars of the Levant,
whose name was Alaü, brother to the Great Kaan now reigning, gathered a mighty host and
came up against Baudas and took it by storm. It was a great enterprise! for in Baudas
there were more than 100,000 horse, besides foot soldiers. And when Alaü had taken the
place he found therein a tower of the Califs, which was full of gold and silver and
other treasure; in fact the greatest accumulation of treasure in one spot that ever was
known. When he beheld that great heap of treasure he was astonished, and, summoning the
Calif to his presence, he said to him: “Calif, tell me now why thou hast gathered such a
huge treasure? What didst thou mean to do therewith? Knewest thou not that I was thine
enemy, and that I was coming against thee with so great an host to cast thee forth of
thine heritage? Wherefore didst thou not take of thy gear and employ it in paying
knights and soldiers to defend thee and thy city?” The Calif wist not what to answer,
and said never a word.
