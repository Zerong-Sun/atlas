## ev.road.00.title

The Marker Between Two Camps

## ev.road.00.body

Two camps share a well on the steppe. Rain has cut a fresh rut across the path, and a cart from the southern camp sits with one wheel sunk to the hub. The courier who moved the route stone after the storm kneels beside it. “I thought I was keeping people off the soft ground,” he tells the herder. “The stone sent us straight into it,” she answers, though she helps him lift the wheel. His daughter brings out a sketch she made before the rain. Her pony slipped at the old bend, so she marked the firmer track by the scrub. “Take this to the keeper at the far well,” the herder says. “He knows where both camps come in.” Going there to settle the marker adds two days to your journey. A guide whose family keeps other wells offers a longer circuit for a fee; it adds four days. The daughter holds the paper flat against the wind while the adults study it.

Take the disputed track with the courier's sketch

## ev.road.00.choice.choice_1

## ev.ev_road_00.choice_1_result

Your party follows the girl's sketch. A cart sinks at the washed-out bend, and its driver asks you to keep the animals back while they lift the wheel. After two extra days, at the far well, the keeper lays the sketch beside his own worn map. “The stone needs to stand here,” he says, tapping the shared turning. He sends his nephew back to show both camps. You leave the sketch with him and continue your journey, after the driver discovers that the wheel hub cracked in the rut. It must be bound for the next stretch; the driver gives up his spare leather to hold it together, and the animals flinch at every deep track.

## ev.ev_road_00.choice_2_result

The guide takes your payment and leads the circuit. At the first well his aunt asks why the usual road is empty. “They moved the stone after the rain,” he tells her. “Then tell the keeper before another wheel breaks,” she says, filling your skins. After four extra days, a rider brings word that both camps have met at the marker. The guide shows you their agreed line on his map before your party moves on.

## ev.road.01.title

The Ferry Keeper's Second Landing

## ev.road.01.body

At a river landing on the road, a ferry keeper has taken up the planks used by heavy wagons. A young clerk arrives with a bag of letters and stops at the gap. “The seals are dry now. How do I get them across?” The keeper points upstream to his mother’s small boat. She is carrying a load of reeds to the bank and calls back, “I can take people and light bags, a few at a time. Wrap the letters before you step in.” The clerk pulls an oilcloth from his sleeve. “Would this do?” She makes him tuck the loose corner under the bag. Her boat charges a fare. The keeper will repair the heavy landing so carts and animals can follow; waiting for both crossings adds two days. The keeper can also arrange guarded landings for a larger travelling party; that route charges a higher fee and adds three days. The clerk holds out the wrapped bag for the woman to inspect.

The office clerk recognizes the receipt and thanks your party for bringing the packet through.

The Orchard by the Old Wall

## ev.road.02.body

The owner catches the water running down the wheel track. “My sister comes to pick apricots in the morning,” he tells the driver. “The lower trees need this water tonight.” His nephew wedges the gate shut and points to the public road beside an old boundary wall. “I’ll put a sign there before the next cart arrives.” A traveller remembers an old tale about a ruler who fooled young men with a garden. An escort offers the longer public road for a fee; it adds four days but keeps the carts away from the orchard. The owner checks the gate once more while the nephew looks for a board to paint.

## ev.road.03.title

The Boat at the Island Jetty

## ev.road.03.body

At an island jetty, a boatbuilder is repairing the steering oar of the vessel you have joined. The cook turns a water jar and finds a thin stream running from its base. “That one was for the rowers,” she says. “If we do not refill soon, they will have to share the little one.” A pilot draws two channels in wet sand. The short passage passes a shoal shifted by the last storm; sounding it will add two days to the journey. “Can the oar bear a scrape?” the cook asks. The boatbuilder fits a spare and makes her test the lashings herself. “It can, if you do not force it against the bottom.” The pilot knows a chain of sheltered harbours as well. His fare covers that longer passage, which adds four days. The cook puts the leaking jar where everyone can see it and asks the crew to fill the sound ones first.
