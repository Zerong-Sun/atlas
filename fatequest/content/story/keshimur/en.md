## city.keshimur.name

Keshimur

## ev.keshimur.entry.body

At a Kashmir lodging yard, a guide is helping his mother set bowls of rice and milk for travellers. His younger cousin is late bringing firewood. When the boy finally appears, he says mist hid the path near the upper orchard. The guide asks him which tree he used to find the turn. ‘The bent pine,’ the boy says, then admits he walked past it twice.

You ask whether that same path leads toward the market. The mother points to the lower lane and says people carrying saffron and mountain wool come that way. She asks her son to take the boy’s damp cloak to the fire before it chills him. The guide sets out another bowl for you and asks where you mean to travel. He knows several mountain approaches, but wants to hear your destination before naming a pass.

The cousin eats quickly, then tells you a funny tale about an old image at a hill shrine that supposedly spoke to a traveller. His mother smiles and asks what the traveller had eaten before hearing it. The boy says he will find out tomorrow.

## ev.keshimur.a.title

The Story at the Hill Shrine

## ev.keshimur.a.body

At the old hill shrine, a caretaker is replacing a small oil lamp while the guide’s cousin asks about the image inside. ‘Did it ever speak?’ the boy asks. The caretaker says he has heard the tale from many travellers, each with a different sentence. He has heard the boy’s own family tell it differently too.

You ask which part he knows for himself. ‘That the lamp goes out when this window is open,’ he says, and lets the boy feel the draught. The cousin closes the shutter and the flame steadies. A wool carrier resting nearby asks the caretaker how to reach the road toward Badakhshan. He points to a lower path but tells her to check the day’s mist with a guide.

The cousin wants you to stay and hear the older versions of the tale. The caretaker is finishing the lamp and can show you the hill path; he also knows travellers heading toward Kabul. The boy waits by the shutter, ready to tell you his own version first.

## ev.keshimur.b.title

The Guide’s Mist Notes

## ev.keshimur.b.body

The guide from the lodging yard has spread several weather notes beside the hearth. His cousin drew the bent pine on one page but put it on the wrong side of a stream. The guide asks him to walk through the route aloud. When the boy reaches the stream, he pauses and corrects his drawing.

You ask how the guide chooses a day to cross. He says the high path may be clear in the morning and hidden by afternoon cloud. His mother sets down food and asks whether the family can spend a week in the lower valley while the next party gathers. The guide says that would let the cousin learn the way properly. A stone merchant visiting the yard offers a merchant parcel of turquoise for 100 silver.

The guide can work with you for seven days for 6 silver and share the paths toward Delhi. His mother has room if you stay the same week to rest among the families. The cousin turns the page around and smiles: now the pine stands on the right bank.

## ev.ev_keshimur_a_followup.title

What the Caretaker Actually Heard

## ev.ev_keshimur_a_followup.body

The shrine caretaker has asked the guide’s cousin to bring his mother. She remembers hearing the story of the speaking image from a traveller when she was young, but cannot recall what the image supposedly said. The boy is disappointed. ‘I thought you knew the words,’ he tells her. She says the part she remembers clearly is the traveller getting lost in the mist and being found near the lamp.

You ask the caretaker what he has learned from the other visitors. He has put their names beside the versions they told, so no one has to pretend they all heard the same thing. A wool carrier arrives with news of travellers toward Kabul and Delhi. She says the roads divide after the lower valley; the caretaker asks her to draw that on the back of his page.

The boy watches the pencil move. A guide waiting outside offers to accompany you for 4 silver if you decide to take a mountain stage. The caretaker asks his young visitor to repeat his mother’s version once more before she goes home.

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.title

A Plank for the Footbridge

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.body

Near the lower valley, two sisters are fitting a new plank to a narrow footbridge. Their father carries wool across it each market day; last week he caught his sandal in a split board. The younger sister wants to nail the replacement at once, but the older asks her to set a loaded basket on it first.

You hold the basket while they test the span. The board rocks at one end. ‘I cut it to the old length,’ the younger says. Her sister points to the worn ledge where the old plank used to rest. Their father arrives with wool on his back and asks whether he may cross. ‘Wait for one more cut,’ both daughters say together. He sets the wool down and shows them the mark he made on the stone years ago.

They offer you 0.7 silver if you stay the day to help fit and test the bridge. If you have to leave sooner, their father can show you how the boards are supported above the stream.

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.choice.fit

Stay a day and help fit the bridge plank

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.result.fit

You spend the day testing the new board with baskets and then with the wool load. The sisters pay you 0.7 silver; their father crosses without catching his sandal and calls back to thank all three of you.

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.choice.learn

Ask how the boards rest on the stone ledge

## ev.keshimur.bridgeplank.result.learn

Their father shows you the ledge and the old mark he made. You record the small bridge design while the sisters fit the final board and test it with the wool basket.
