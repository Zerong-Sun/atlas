## city.chinginju.name

Chinginju

## ev.chinginju.entry.body

At a bridge in Chinginju, a tea seller is carrying cups from a tray to travellers waiting for the market to open. One cup rattles on the boards, and his little brother stops to look beneath the bridge. ‘Did it fall in?’ the boy asks. ‘Only the wooden spoon,’ the seller says, pointing to the tray. A cart driver offers to fetch it with a hooked pole. The boy wants to climb down himself, but the seller asks him to hold the cups while the driver works. You steady the tray. The spoon comes up wet and the seller rinses it before serving anyone. The driver asks which lane the cloth market uses this morning. ‘The same one as yesterday,’ the seller says, ‘unless the dyers have spread fabric there again.’ His brother runs ahead to check and comes back with a report: the lane is open, but a tailor has laid out samples at its entrance. The seller invites you to cross town now, or stay a day and meet the dyers and weavers who buy his morning tea.

## ev.chinginju.entry.choice.look

Stay a day among the cloth stalls

## ev.chinginju.entry.choice.pass

Cross Chinginju today

## ev.chinginju.entry.title

The Tea Seller’s Bridge

## ev.ev_chinginju_entry.choice_1_result

The seller rinses the recovered spoon, and his brother brings the first cups across the bridge. The cart driver points you toward the street through town. You pass the tailor’s samples and remember where to find the market if you return.

## ev.ev_chinginju_entry.choice_2_result

You stay another day. The seller’s brother introduces you to the tailor who set out the cloth samples, then walks you by the dyers’ sheds. They explain why some fabric stays out of the morning damp. By evening you know the people who use the bridge as well as the way across town.

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.title

The Blue Cloth under the Eaves

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.body

A dyer has hung two pieces of blue cloth beneath her eaves. One came out of the rinse water this morning; the other dried yesterday and looks noticeably lighter. Her apprentice thinks the older one has faded already. ‘Feel them both,’ she says. ‘The colour changes as the water leaves.’ You touch a corner of each. A customer arrives with white and yellow thread, unsure which to use for a border. The dyer pins both to the dry cloth and steps back so the customer can see. The apprentice asks why she does not decide for him. ‘He will wear it; let him look from the doorway,’ she replies. The customer walks out, compares the colours in daylight, and chooses one for his coat. He is travelling toward Chinghianfu soon and knows which road leaves by the western gate. The dyer asks whether you want his directions or will help her test a small sample before the rest of the blue cloth is washed.

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.choice.route

Ask the customer about Chinghianfu

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.choice.route_result

The customer marks the road toward Chinghianfu on your board, beginning at the western gate. The apprentice repeats the first turn as he folds the chosen thread into the coat order. The dyer leaves the wet blue cloth under the eaves to finish drying.

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.choice.scene

Help test the blue cloth and thread

## ev.ev_gap_chinginju.choice.scene_result

You hold a small cloth sample while the apprentice dips and rinses it. Once it dries, the customer compares his chosen thread against it again. The dyer asks the apprentice what changed; he names the shade correctly this time.

## ev.chinginju.printer.title

The Printer’s Two Labels

## ev.chinginju.printer.body

A small printer has made labels for two cloth shops, but his apprentice has laid the finished stacks together. The shops have similar names, and the ink is not yet dry enough to handle freely. ‘Which pile belongs to the dyer by the bridge?’ the apprentice asks. The printer points to a tiny mark at the bottom of the blocks. One has a short line; the other does not. You hold a clean board while they separate the stacks. A messenger from one shop arrives and says his master needs the labels before noon. The apprentice wants to hand him the first pile. ‘Read the name aloud,’ the printer says. He does, notices the wrong character, and puts it back. The messenger waits while they find the correct pile. He asks whether all the labels need to be counted, and the printer says yes: his shop has ordered enough for every bolt of cloth. You may help sort the names now, or return the next day to watch a new run and meet the two customers when they collect it.

## ev.chinginju.printer.choice.help

Help separate the two label orders

## ev.chinginju.printer.result.help

You hold each stack while the apprentice reads the shop name and counts the sheets. The messenger gets the correct order before noon. The printer shows you how the tiny block mark prevented a costly mistake in the cloth accounts.

## ev.chinginju.printer.choice.stay

Return a day later for the next print run

## ev.chinginju.printer.result.stay

You return the next day as the printer makes a fresh run. The apprentice keeps each shop’s labels on its own board and checks the names with both customers when they arrive. The printer introduces you as someone who knows the work, and the customers remember you.
