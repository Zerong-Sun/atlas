## city.cail.name

Cail

## ev.cail.entry.body

A groom is leading a horse through Cail's market when the animal stops at a patch of shade and refuses to move. “He thinks that stall still sells fodder,” the groom says. The stall now belongs to a cotton seller, who brings out a handful of leaves. “Your father fed him here last season. He remembers better than you.” The groom laughs and lets the horse take them. You ask where the road through town runs. The cotton seller points past the horse yards and the shrine, then asks whether you will stay to see the vessels unloading animals from across the sea. A porter passing with a bale of cloth says the landing is busy enough to last all day. The groom checks the horse's shoes before leading it on and tells you to watch your step near the water: men carrying wet planks seldom see who is standing behind them. The cotton seller gathers her cloth out of the horse’s reach and tells the groom to return after the market closes. The woman asks the carter to bring back news of his father when he returns. He promises to do so and leads the horse toward a place where the ground is dry.

## ev.cail.a.title

A Horse Comes Ashore

## ev.cail.a.body

At the landing, a groom waits while a horse is led down a plank from a western ship. The animal pauses halfway. “Don't pull against his neck,” the groom tells an impatient broker. “Give him time to see the ground.” They move a bundle of cloth away from the end of the plank. The horse steps down and shakes seawater from its mane. You ask the broker whether all horses arrive here by ship. “Many do, from harbours such as Ormus and Aden. Buyers meet them here, but a horse needs a look and a walk before anyone names a price.” The groom checks its legs and asks the broker to let it drink. A boatman nearby offers to name the ports farther along the coast; the groom promises to return after he has put the horse in shade. The broker holds the animal's tag for you to read and says he will introduce you to the stablekeeper when the next load is ashore. The broker tells the groom to write down which horse needed time on the gangplank. ‘Its next handler should know,’ the groom says, and notes the mark on its tag.

## ev.cail.b.title

The Cloth Seller’s Royal Tale

## ev.cail.b.body

In the square, a cloth seller is laying out a bolt for a visitor from the royal household. His niece asks why he has brought his finest cotton instead of the cheaper cloth people buy for work. “Because the steward asked to see this weave,” he says. She has heard stories of brothers from a ruling family who once divided their harbours and asks whether the steward belongs to one of them. He says the stories change with the teller and the year; today he only needs the right name on his receipt. A broker waiting beside them names the coastal markets toward Melibar and Coilum, where different buyers ask for different cloth. You ask whether the steward will come soon. “If he does, he'll want the dye to hold in water,” the seller replies. His niece brings a bowl, ready to show him. Other buyers are already looking at the cotton, and the seller asks her to keep their orders separate from the household's. The niece asks the broker whether buyers in those markets test cloth in water too. He says some do, and shows her a folded sample he carried back from his last visit.

## ev.ev_cail_a_followup.title

Cail: At the Stable after Landing

## ev.ev_cail_a_followup.body

The broker calls you to the stable after the landed horses have had water. A groom is showing a younger animal how to walk between the stalls. “He needs a little space,” the groom says when a buyer crowds its shoulder. The broker asks you to move the folded cloth that blocks the passage. Once the path is clear, he turns to a younger horse, recently landed and not yet trained for a long road and lets you inspect its lead rope and hooves. Another merchant is waiting with a route sheet. “If you're leaving without a horse,” he says, “I can still show you the roads toward Melibar and Coilum.” A sailor at the door joins in, naming Samara as a port farther across the water. The groom asks them all to lower their voices until the young animal finishes its turn; only then does the broker resume his offers. The sailor points to a worn place on the route sheet where many hands have traced the same crossing. The groom calls the broker over when the young horse finally stands still. Only then does the buyer approach to inspect its hooves, while the sailor folds the route paper away from the water trough.

## ev.cail.drum.title

The Drummer’s Missing Cloth

## ev.cail.drum.body

A drummer in a Cail courtyard is preparing to play for a household gathering. His daughter has brought the drum but cannot find the cloth that keeps dust from its skin. “You left it with the cotton seller,” she says. “I used that one to wrap the smaller drum,” he answers. She opens a basket and finds a piece of plain cloth the right size. Her father asks her to try it. She ties it too tightly, and the drum gives a dull sound. “Loosen this side,” he says. She does, strikes it again, and grins when the note carries across the yard. A neighbour comes to the gate to ask whether they have started without her. “Not yet,” the daughter says. “Father wanted to blame a missing cloth first.” The drummer laughs and offers you a place under the shade. He needs someone to carry the larger drum to the household, and his daughter would welcome another ear while she practises the new rhythm. The daughter asks whether the rhythm she learned will suit the family’s gathering. Her father taps the first beat for her, then lets her answer with the next three. The neighbour waits to hear the end.

## ev.cail.drum.choice.carry

Carry the drum to the gathering

## ev.cail.drum.result.carry

You carry the larger drum while father and daughter walk ahead. At the gathering the neighbour clears a place for them, and the daughter begins with the rhythm she practised. The household thanks you for bringing the instrument safely through the crowded lane.

## ev.cail.drum.choice.listen

Stay for the afternoon rehearsal

## ev.cail.drum.result.listen

You stay for the afternoon and listen as the daughter learns where to leave a pause for the singers. Her father brings water and a plate of rice when they stop. By the time the gathering begins, you have rested and the daughter can play the new rhythm without looking to him.
