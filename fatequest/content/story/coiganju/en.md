## city.coiganju.name

Coiganju

## ev.coiganju.entry.body

At Coiganju’s landing, a young deckhand has tied his boat to a post marked for incoming cargo. A larger boat is approaching, and its owner calls for the place to be cleared. ‘I thought the mark meant spare mooring,’ the deckhand says. His captain brings him to the post and traces the painted character with a finger. The boy admits he copied the wrong note from the harbour board. You help him ease the rope free while the large boat comes alongside. Its crew unloads crates for different streets in town, and a porter asks which pile belongs to the shop near the western gate. The captain checks the tags rather than guessing. You ask how to cross the town without getting caught among the carts. He points to a lane behind the warehouses. The deckhand has to carry a corrected harbour note there and offers to walk with you tomorrow if you stay. The large boat’s owner gives him time to finish the new knot before asking for the post.

## ev.coiganju.entry.choice.look

Stay a day to know the landing

## ev.coiganju.entry.choice.pass

Cross Coiganju by the warehouse lane

## ev.coiganju.entry.title

A Rope for the Wrong Boat

## ev.ev_coiganju_entry.choice_1_result

The deckhand frees the post and ties his boat at the right place. The captain points out the lane behind the warehouses. You take it across town while porters bring the larger boat’s crates ashore.

## ev.ev_coiganju_entry.choice_2_result

You stay another day. The deckhand carries his corrected note through the warehouses and shows you where each street branches from the quay. His captain asks him to read the mooring board again before they return, and he gets it right.

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.title

The Umbrella Rib at the Corner

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.body

An umbrella mender has taken a split bamboo rib from a customer’s old umbrella. His apprentice has cut a replacement, but the smooth side faces outward and catches the cloth when it opens. ‘Turn it with the grain,’ the mender tells her. She tries, and the frame moves more easily. The customer asks whether they must replace the whole umbrella. ‘The canopy is sound,’ he says, showing the dry seam above the joint. You hold the handle while he binds the new rib. A messenger waiting nearby asks the way toward Tiju; his parcel must go by the road after the next market. The apprentice has made that walk with her family and gives him the first turn. He asks what happens after the bridge, so she draws the second on a scrap of wrapping paper. The mender hands her the umbrella to test in a light shower and asks you to look at the repaired joint before they return it.

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.choice.route

Ask the messenger about the Tiju road

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.choice.route_result

The messenger marks the road toward Tiju on your board, using the apprentice’s two turns to begin. She checks his drawing before he folds the paper away. The umbrella customer tests the repaired frame by the doorway.

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.choice.scene

Help the apprentice mend the umbrella

## ev.ev_gap_coiganju.choice.scene_result

You hold the handle as the apprentice binds the rib along the grain. The mender asks her to open and close it three times. The cloth no longer catches, and the customer pays for the repair before taking it home.

## ev.coiganju.lantern.title

The Borrowed Lantern

## ev.coiganju.lantern.body

Two sisters at a courtyard gate are arguing over a borrowed lantern. The younger carried it to a neighbour’s evening meal and brought it back with a torn paper side. The elder has promised to return it before the owner’s son leaves for the road. ‘I can paste another sheet over it,’ the younger says. Her sister holds the lantern toward the light. ‘Then he will not see the painted bird his mother made.’ You ask whether the torn piece is still there. It is folded inside the younger sister’s sleeve. Their grandmother brings out a little paste and a narrow brush. She shows them how to join the edges without covering the picture. The owner’s son arrives while the repair dries; he says his mother will want to see that her bird is whole, not a brand new lantern. The sisters invite you to carry it with them once it is safe to hold, or return tomorrow to help their grandmother make a protective sleeve for the next journey.

## ev.coiganju.lantern.choice.return

Carry the repaired lantern back

## ev.coiganju.lantern.result.return

You carry the lantern by its upper ring while the sisters guard the still damp side. The owner’s mother recognizes the painted bird through the repair and thanks them for bringing back every piece. The sisters introduce you, and she remembers your help.

## ev.coiganju.lantern.choice.learn

Return a day later to make its sleeve

## ev.coiganju.lantern.result.learn

You return the next day. The grandmother measures a cloth sleeve around the dry lantern, lets the younger sister cut it, and has you hold the seam while she stitches. The owner’s son tries it on his road pack and promises to bring the lantern home safely.
