## city.yasdi.name

Yasdi

## ev.yasdi.entry.title

The Weavers of Yazd

## ev.yasdi.entry.body

In a Yazd workshop, a weaver named Roya cuts a loose thread from a length of silk. Her younger brother holds the cloth up to the light and points to a place he thinks she has missed.

‘That one belongs to the pattern,’ she says, turning the fabric over for him to see. A merchant waiting by the door will take this piece with him when it is ready. He tells you that Yazd silk travels far; for the road beyond the town, he is filling every water skin he has. Roya’s brother wants to know where you are headed so he can tell you which courtyard still has a well.

You ask the merchant why he is waiting instead of collecting the cloth. He says Roya promised to check the edge in daylight; last time a folded corner hid a loose stitch, and his buyer noticed first. Roya tells him that was another order and asks her brother to bring the whole length outside. The boy balances it across both arms. ‘This one is for your buyer with the blue border, yes?’ she asks. The merchant confirms it, then asks if the courtyard well is open. Her brother says it is, though the keeper has a queue of potters waiting. Roya promises to finish the edge while he fills a skin.

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.title

Roya’s Water Jar

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.body

Roya’s brother has promised to bring water for the workshop, but the shared jar at the well is cracked. Water keeps leaking onto his sandals. Roya needs the silk rinsed before the merchant returns.

The well keeper lends him a sound jar now and asks him to help repair the broken one when the potter arrives. He looks at the workshop, then at you. ‘I need to get water to Roya. Could you help with the old jar later?’ The keeper sets the sound jar between them and tells the boy to carry it to Roya first. The potter will look at the cracked one after she finishes a neighbour’s order.

You ask whether the cracked jar has served the well long. The keeper says her father left it there years ago; everyone knows its blue rim and brings it back after use. Roya’s brother asks if a repair will hold. The potter, passing with another customer’s bowl, says she needs to see the crack dry before promising anything. He reaches for the borrowed jar, then stops because his sandals are wet and his hands are slippery. The keeper wipes the handle with a cloth and asks him to hold it by both sides. He takes two careful steps, glances back at you, and says his sister will worry if the water is late again.

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.choice.repair

Spend a day helping mend the jar

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.choice.carry

Carry water back to Roya now

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.result.repair

The potter shows Roya’s brother how to seal the crack, then lets the repair dry. You spend the day helping him and carrying water in another jar. By evening the caretaker knows your name and thanks you for keeping the workshop supplied.

## ev.yasdi.threadwell.result.carry

You take a smaller jar back to the workshop. Roya rinses the silk and hangs it where the air can reach it. Her brother brings you a cup of cool water before going back for the next load. You feel refreshed.
