## city.zayton.name

Zayton

## city.zayton.wonder

Ships come and go through Zayton, and people at the quay recognize some of them by their sails. A-He sells hot soup for her mother beside the bridge and knows what the crews like to eat. Pepper arrives from India; empty holds wait for porcelain. She is watching for her uncle's ship. On his last departure he promised to bring back a cracked wooden bowl for her to mend.

## ev.zayton.entry.title

The Haven of Zayton

## ev.zayton.entry.body

By the time you enter Zayton, the harbour has been at work for hours. Porters queue at the gate. A young woman sits on a cargo sack, marking each load with charcoal. Her name is A-He; she is minding her uncle's cargo. His ship came in last night, its boards still crusted with salt from the south.

"These two go to different places," she tells a porter. "One belongs to our soup shop by the bridge. The other is for the apothecary in West Street." She turns over the wooden tag and shows him the matching mark on its back. Her uncle comes out of the customs house and tells her to put her own name in the register so they can trace the delivery.

You ask how long registration takes. He says several ships have come in this week, so the papers will take two days; if you would rather ask about ships first, he knows a pilot. A-He asks you to help explain the mistaken sack to the clerk. She has written its proper destination on the tag. Across the bridge, her mother lights the stove and calls to ask whether her brother has eaten anything warm yet.

## ev.zayton.ledger_consequence.title

The Road in the Ledger

## ev.zayton.ledger_consequence.body

The next morning A-He brings you a clean copy of the customs ledger. The sack she found yesterday now stands under the apothecary's name, with a clerk's signature beside it. Your cargo is listed too, and the land road to Kinsay is written in the margin.

Her uncle, drinking soup beside the ship, tells her to loosen the roll before the ink dries. A-He spreads it out and asks whether you want a stamped copy. "At the next gate you can show them this instead of telling the whole story again." She points to her name: this is the first full page she has copied alone.

You ask why the sack mattered so much. The apothecary helped her mother last year; this shipment contains medicine he asked the ship to carry. She did not want it waiting in a warehouse overnight. The clerk checks her figures and lends her a clean board to hold the paper while she finishes the final column.

## ev.zayton.watch_consequence.title

Lanterns of the Night Watch

## ev.zayton.watch_consequence.body

At night the harbour watch comes to your lodging with a lantern. A-He follows, carrying the day's ledger. No declaration mark appears beside your cargo. The watch can collect a late fee, or hold the goods in the warehouse for two days of checking.

Old Guo sets down the lantern and first asks where the cargo is. A-He gives the warehouse number. "I saw the sacks there today. Their seals were still on." Guo asks her to write only what she saw. He has known her father for years and often eats soup at the bridge, but tonight he is keeping the register.

You ask who will watch the goods if they stay. Guo says the warehouse has a guard, and he will give you a copy of the seal mark. A-He offers to copy the list again. Her mother brings the watchman a bowl of soup and tells him to let it cool; the lantern smoke has already filled the little room.

## ev.zayton.pilot_consequence.title

Two Roads from a Pilot

## ev.zayton.pilot_consequence.body

A-He's uncle leads you to an old table. Old Wu, a pilot, is repairing a wooden rule whose marks have faded in seawater. He asks A-He for a bowl of water, then draws two roads on the wet tabletop: south by ship to Chamba when the wind serves, and north overland by cart to Fuju.

"Ask me about one of them and I'll make that one clear," Wu says. A-He asks why he draws the roads afresh each time. A new crew copied an old mark badly last year and noticed only at a turning point. Her uncle remembers the crew: they nearly carried the apothecary's parcel to another port. A-He laughs and asks you which road to hear about first.

Wu will write down the places where you can rest and fill water. A-He offers to read back his handwriting so that even he does not mistake it later.

## city.zayton.market.name

The Quay at Zayton

## city.zayton.shrine.name

The Temple of the Sea

## city.zayton.shrine.desc

Above the anchorage, on a hill that catches the first light off the sea, stands
the temple of the sea-goddess. Her gilded image faces south over the water; at
her feet lie the offerings of shipmasters — paper burnt, rice set out, a cup of
wine. No master of this coast puts out without going up to her first, and none
of them calls it fortune-telling. They call it asking.

## city.zayton.market.desc

The quay of Zayton runs the length of the water and is never quiet. Pepper comes ashore
in sacks the height of a man; there are pearls weighed out in little brass scales, and
lignaloes and sandalwood stacked like cordwood. You must know that the merchant pays
dearly here: the Kaan takes a tamghā on everything, ten in the hundred, and the ship's
charge on top of it — thirty in the hundred on small wares, forty on bulky goods, and
four and forty on pepper. Between the freight and the Kaan's due a man pays out a good
half of what his cargo is worth. And yet on the other half he makes so great a profit
that he is always glad to come back with more.

## ev.zayton.fanfang.body

At the well in the foreign merchants' quarter, Farid, a Persian merchant, and A-Qing, a local weaver, wait for a length of silk to dry. A-Qing's younger brother mixed two kinds of thread, leaving the border a finger wider than the pattern. Farid has promised to explain it to the buyer, once A-Qing tells him whether it can still be sold at the old price.

"Feel this edge first," she says, holding out the cloth. "I took it out and wove it again here." Farid understands her local words but is looking for a clear phrase for a foreign buyer. He has lived on this street for years; his son sells spices at its entrance, and the family changes languages over dinner. She tells him he bargains too slowly. He says she weaves too quickly. Both laugh.

If you speak Persian, Farid can introduce you to a shipowner bound for Chamba. An interpreter nearby teaches Arabic over several weeks. A-Qing spreads the silk again, names the price of the whole piece, and asks you to wait for her brother's next work.

## ev.zayton.fanfang.title

The Foreign Quarter

## ev.zayton.harbour.body

Old Zheng is moving pepper sacks from a ship's plank to dry ground. His daughter has a small scale and is measuring half a sack for a neighbour. Zheng tells her to tie the mouth tight; damp pepper will bring complaints from the next buyer. Beside them a sailor repairs a sail with a needle as thick as a nail.

"Do you want a whole sack, or are you asking when the ships leave?" Zheng gives you the price and its port of origin. His daughter says this sack came from the south. Her uncle wrote her name on it so she could check it herself. Once the counting is done, she wants to take you to the outer embankment and show how the crews find a berth.

Zheng heard yesterday that ships for Chamba might wait for the wind, and asked the pilot again this morning. If you stay a day, he can help confirm the departure. His daughter hands the measured pepper to their neighbour and asks to see the newly mended sail tonight. He agrees, once she washes the pepper dust from her hands.

## ev.zayton.harbour.title

The Quay

## ev.zayton.mazu.body

Someone has come to give thanks at the sea goddess's shrine above the anchorage. Old Wu brings his daughter Xiao-Ling with rice and paper. A headwind kept them at sea several extra days last year. Xiao-Ling guarded the fresh-water jars and wrote down each day's use on a wooden slip. She has brought it to show the older woman by the shrine.

"Your writing is clearer than my sea chart," Wu says. Xiao-Ling points to a rain-blurred mark and asks how much water was left that day. He tells her to ask her uncle, who shared the watch. They argued over that count once and now check the jars together before every departure.

The woman asks Xiao-Ling to let the slip dry, then listen while she reads a lot. You can make an offering and sit with them, or try a lot yourself if you have learned the practice. The wind lifts a corner of the paper outside. Xiao-Ling runs to catch it and asks to keep the new water record on the next voyage.

## ev.zayton.mazu.title

The Temple of the Sea

## ev.zayton.mentor.body

The woman beside the shrine is called Granny Lin. Xiao-Ling asks her about the water record. Lin first has the girl strike through a wrong date and explain what happened aboard that day. "Write it carefully and you'll see where the count went astray," she says, handing back the pen.

Lin learned to read lots from her mother and now teaches children from the next street. When the shrine is quiet after noon, she reads the verses with her pupils and asks them to speak to sailors about their present concerns. Xiao-Ling asks whether she may learn only her favourite lot. Lin tells her to learn the numbers first; the favourite one will still be there tomorrow.

If you stay two weeks and pay her teaching fee, she will teach you to read lots. The shrine keeper teaches casting the wooden blocks in a shorter, three-day course. You may also sit and listen. Wu waits at the door and asks his daughter if she has finished with the slip. She has, and she has written down the three jars to check before their next departure.

## market.zayton.item.cinnabar

Cinnabar from the mountain mines of the south, sold in both the raw stone and the ground
powder — the latter in small stoppered bottles because the wind off the haven will carry
it away by the grain.

## market.zayton.item.dehua-porcelain

Blanc de Chine out of the Dehua kilns — unglazed on the surface, warm as ivory. The Arab
merchants call it 'the fat of the earth' and pay the forty-four in the hundred freight
without complaint.

## market.zayton.item.ginger

Preserved ginger in clay jars sealed with wax, bound for the galleys of Hormuz and Aden.
The spice merchants say a ship without ginger is a ship that will lose half its crew to
the flux.

## market.zayton.item.hangzhou-fan

Folding fans from Kinsay, their ribs of sandalwood, their leaves painted with
calligraphy. A merchant who buys a crate of these at Zayton can sell them in Damascus
for ten times the price.

## market.zayton.item.lacquerware

Black and cinnabar-red lacquer from the workshops of Fuju, cured in the damp heat of the
coastal summer. The Indian merchants prize it for the way candlelight moves across the
surface like oil on dark water.

## market.zayton.item.musk

The musk-pod from the deer of the Tibetan marches, brought down to Zayton by caravan
through the passes. A single pod, no bigger than a walnut, will scent a cargo of silk
for a month.

## market.zayton.item.paper-money

The Kaan's own currency, printed on mulberry-bark sheets with the imperial seal in
vermilion. The quay-side money-changers will swap silver for paper at a rate that
changes with the tide.

## market.zayton.item.porcelain

The white ware of Jingdezhen, packed in straw and sawdust, carried down the rivers on
flat-bottomed boats. A percentage of every crate is broken in transit, and the merchants
have already figured that cost into the price you pay.

## market.zayton.item.rhubarb

The dried root of the Tangut rhubarb — worth more pound for pound than cinnamon in the
apothecaries of Venice. The warehousemen weigh it on the same brass scales they use for
pearls.

## market.zayton.item.silk

Bales of raw Manzi silk, stacked eight-high in the customs shed, waiting to be weighed
and taxed. The Kaan's clerks nick a corner of every bale to check the grade; finer than
any the Persians spin.

## market.zayton.item.sugar

Cane sugar from the plantations of Fuju, shipped up the coast in cone-shaped loaves
wrapped in palm-leaf. A cone of sugar is the universal gift at the quay; no deal is
closed without one.

## market.zayton.item.tea

Tea pressed into bricks and stamped with the producer's chop. The quayside merchants
will trade a brick for a sack of pepper straight across, and both sides consider it a
bargain.

## ev.ev_zayton_harbour_followup.title

Zayton: The Freight Reckoning

## ev.ev_zayton_harbour_followup.body

A-Miao, Old Zheng's daughter, has delivered the half sack of pepper to their neighbour. You may buy the sack at the merchant price, ask when the next ship for Chamba will sail, or stay a day to watch A-Miao's uncle mend the sail. Zheng shuts the ledger and sends his daughter to return the neighbour's small scale.

## ev.ev_zayton_mentor_followup.title

Zayton: The Reader's Parting Counsel

## ev.ev_zayton_mentor_followup.body

At the end of two weeks of lessons, Granny Lin hands you the lot cylinder and asks you to read for Old Wu. He waits outside with a fresh list of water jars. Xiao-Ling peers in and says she wants to hear you first, then tell you the departure day the captain gave her.

You can take the slip and Xiao-Ling's reminder, ask Lin about the sea road to Chamba, or contribute oil for the shrine lamp. Xiao-Ling hands the jar list to her father and asks which line sounds most useful for this voyage.

Zayton: The Three Cups at Parting

## ev.ev_zayton_mentor_jiaobei_followup.body

The shrine keeper sets two wooden blocks on the table and asks you to cast them once on your own. During your three days of lessons, Xiao-Ling often watched; now she knows the faces they can show. The keeper asks what you want to know. You name your next journey, and he asks you to say the destination and departure day aloud.

The blocks fall. Xiao-Ling sees one wobble near the edge. "Has it stopped yet?" she asks. The keeper waits. Once it rests, he asks you to describe what you see before he reads anything. Xiao-Ling fetches her water-jar list and says that when you choose a ship, you should ask its captain about the water.

You may copy the reading into your book, ask about the ships for Java, or stay another day to practise. The keeper puts the blocks by your hand again while Xiao-Ling waits to hear your choice.

## codex.cx-zayton-watch.name

The Night Watch of Zayton

## codex.cx-zayton-watch.body

The night watch of Zayton keeps its own account of every shadow that slips past the Kaan's clerks. After dark the watch lanterns stop at your lodging and look for your mark in the quay ledger; when they cannot find it, their leader draws no weapon but sets a late-declaration tally on the table. Pay then, or leave the cargo in bond for inspection. The watch writes a name down twice before it is done, and their rounds over the quay are known to you by heart before your cargo is released.
