## city.siju.name

Siju

## ev.siju.entry.body

At the edge of Siju, miller Chen is waiting beside a cart with a broken side board. His sister brings a new plank, but it is a little longer than the cart bed. ‘You measured from the outside,’ he says. She answers that the old board was bent, so she measured the space it was meant to fill. They set the plank against the cart and find she is right; it only needs the corner trimmed.

You ask which way enters town. Chen points toward the grain market and says his sister can show you the families who keep their stores dry through the season. She asks whether you have time to stay a day, because they will be weighing the new harvest. A neighbour comes with water for the workers and asks if the little roadside shrine has its lamp lit yet. Chen’s sister says she will check it after the cart is ready.

The neighbour holds the plank while Chen trims the corner. His sister tries it in the cart and smiles when it sits flat.

## ev.siju.a.title

The Sugar Kettle and the Grain Road

## ev.siju.a.body

At a workshop beyond the grain market, sugar maker Luo is lowering the fire under a kettle. His nephew has placed a tray too close to the stove and asks why the syrup has darkened at one edge. Luo makes him move the tray before it burns, then tastes a drop from the cooler side. ‘The colour tells you where to stir,’ he says. The boy asks if he can take a turn. Luo gives him the long paddle and watches his first careful circle.

You ask where the finished sugar goes. A cart driver waiting outside names Paukin and Coigangiu; another clerk keeps notes for the road to Coiganju. Luo has a full trade bale of sugar for 108 silver. He asks the driver not to load it until the jars are sealed. The nephew points at a loose lid and says they would have lost half a batch if it had gone out that way.

A woman from the next yard comes for a small order. Luo lets the boy weigh it while he answers your questions about the roads.

## ev.siju.b.title

The Clerk’s Paper Bundle

## ev.siju.b.body

Near the market office, clerk Fen is sorting a bundle of paper notes brought for exchange. Her younger brother has counted the sheets but forgotten to separate the torn ones. ‘They still have the same writing,’ he argues. Fen asks him whether the receiving house will take a note with half its mark missing. He admits they should check each sheet under the light.

You ask what else she records. Fen keeps receipts for shopkeepers and a list of travellers who have asked about local customs. A woman waiting by the door wants to know when her family can bring a small offering to the shrine. Fen tells her the caretaker will be there at dusk. The brother offers to carry the woman’s receipt, but Fen makes him finish counting first.

A complete trade bundle of paper notes is priced at 135 silver. Fen sets it apart from the torn sheets and invites you to watch the careful sorting over the day. The brother turns each page so you can see the mark.

## ev.ev_siju_a_followup.title

The Second Sugar Load

## ev.ev_siju_a_followup.body

The next time you pass Luo’s workshop, his nephew is writing labels for a second load. He has marked one jar for Coigangiu and another for a customer who will collect it in Siju. Luo asks him to read both before sealing them. The boy gets the second customer’s name wrong and calls her over rather than guessing again. She tells him the right name and thanks him for asking.

You ask when the carts leave. One driver is bound for Paukin, and the clerk who knows Coiganju has brought her road notes. Luo has another full sugar trade bale for 108 silver; he points to it but keeps the local customer’s jar separate. The nephew asks which way to stack the bale so the seals will not be pressed. Luo lets the driver show him.

The local customer checks her corrected label. Luo asks you to help the boy compare it with the order before anyone carries the jar away.

‘Will she recognize it now?’ the nephew asks. The customer reads her name and points to the correct jar with a smile.

## ev.siju.millstone.title

The Millstone’s Small Wedge

## ev.siju.millstone.body

At Chen’s mill, the new grain is ready but the lower stone shifts slightly each time the wheel turns. Chen’s sister thinks the small wooden wedge beneath it has worn thin. Her nephew says he can cut another; she asks him to measure this one before he touches the knife. He takes the wedge out and discovers one side is much shorter than the other. Chen tells him it was trimmed after the stone settled last season.

You ask whether the mill can work today. Chen says they can test it with a small scoop of grain, but not a whole cart until the stone sits level. The neighbour who helped with the cart board brings a piece of hard wood. He asks the boy which side should face the wheel. The boy fits it by hand, then asks his aunt to turn the wheel slowly. The stone stays steady for one turn. Chen asks everyone to wait for a second before they call it fixed.

‘May I turn it once more?’ the boy asks. His aunt says yes and keeps one hand near the grain scoop, ready to stop if it shifts.

## ev.siju.millstone.result.test

You watch the stone through another turn and show the nephew where the wedge still moves. He adjusts it, and the next small scoop grinds evenly. Chen’s sister thanks you before the first cart is unloaded.

## ev.siju.millstone.result.day
