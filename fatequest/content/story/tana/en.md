## city.tana.name

Tana

## ev.tana.entry.body

At Tana's market, a cloth merchant is trying to fit a new latch to a cargo chest. His mother holds the lid up while he searches for a missing pin. “You laid it by the leather straps,” she tells him. He finds it exactly there. A younger carrier arriving from the shore asks which chest goes on the next boat. “The one with cotton,” the merchant says. “The leather stays until its owner comes.” You ask why he keeps two lists for them. His mother shows you that one is for things leaving by water and the other for goods due on the road toward Melibar. The carrier points out the street he uses after unloading and offers to show you the market if you stay. His mother lets the lid down carefully, then asks her son to test the latch before he puts anyone's name on the chest. He opens and shuts it twice, pleased that the pin holds. The younger carrier asks whether he may borrow the pin if another chest breaks; the merchant tells him to bring the chest first.

## ev.tana.entry.title

The Chest with the New Latch

## ev.ev_tana_entry.choice_1_result

The carrier points you along the street leading out of Tana. Behind you, the merchant's mother asks him to open the chest once more so she can see the latch herself. You continue with the town's turns clear in mind.

## ev.ev_tana_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day among the cloth and leather stalls. The chest's owner comes for his leather, and the merchant's mother checks the road list before it leaves. The carrier shows where goods are sent toward Melibar. You leave knowing the market's people and paths.

## ev.tana.site_gap.title

The Coconut Syrup Pan

## ev.tana.site_gap.body

Near the shore, a woman tends a broad pan of coconut syrup. Her son is holding a clean mould and wants to pour before the syrup has thickened. “Look at the spoon,” she tells him. He lifts it; the syrup still runs from the back too quickly. A neighbour arrives with a basket and asks whether the next cakes will be ready for her family gathering. The woman says the first batch has cooled and sends her son to fetch it. You ask why she keeps the fire so low. “The juice grows bitter if you hurry it,” she says, moving the wood a little farther from the pan. The boy returns with set cakes and shows you the darker piece from yesterday, when he let the heat rise. The neighbour tastes a corner and asks him to make today's batch like the first one. While they wait, she points out the road she takes south toward Melibar. The boy asks whether the darker cake can still be eaten; his mother keeps it for their own tea.

## ev.tana.site_gap.choice.route

## ev.ev_tana_site_gap.choice_1_result

The neighbour takes you to the lane leading along the coast toward Melibar and shows where carts leave the market. The boy calls after you to bring a clean spoon if you return; his mother tells him to mind the pan.

## ev.tana.site_gap.choice.interact

Help judge when the syrup is ready

## ev.ev_tana_site_gap.choice_2_result

You watch the syrup cling to the spoon and tell the boy when it begins to hold. His mother checks it herself, then lets him pour a little into the mould. He sets the pan aside before it darkens and offers you a piece from the earlier batch to compare.

## ev.tana.receipt.title

The Receipt in the Cloth Roll

## ev.tana.receipt.body

A woman bringing cotton to Tana's shore has tucked a receipt inside the wrong roll. Her daughter discovers it while helping a buyer unfold a length of cloth. “This names our neighbour's leather, not this cotton,” the daughter says. The mother remembers setting both papers aside when rain began. She asks the buyer to wait while she checks each bundle. The neighbour arrives carrying a strap and says he has been looking for the paper since morning. “Then help us count before you take it,” the mother replies. He sets down his strap and agrees. You ask whether the buyer will still have time to reach his boat. “If we do it together,” the daughter says, handing you a clean board for the correct marks. The neighbour smiles at her confidence. The mother offers a small payment for help with the count, while the buyer asks someone to carry the corrected receipt to the boat.

## ev.tana.receipt.choice.count

Help count the cloth and leather

## ev.tana.receipt.result.count

You and the daughter count each bundle while her mother writes the correct owner's name. The neighbour gets his leather receipt back, and the buyer takes the cotton with its own mark. The mother pays you for helping the sale finish before the boat leaves.

## ev.tana.receipt.choice.carry

Carry the corrected receipt to the boat

## ev.tana.receipt.result.carry

The daughter gives you the cotton receipt after the family has checked it. You bring it to the buyer at the boat, and he matches the mark to the roll already aboard. The neighbour later tells the market sellers how you kept the two households' goods from being mixed.
