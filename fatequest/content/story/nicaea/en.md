## city.nicaea.name

Nicaea

## ev.nicaea.entry.choice.pass

Thank Eleni and continue beside the lake

## ev.nicaea.entry.choice.rest

Stay a day at Eleni's house

## ev.nicaea.entry.body

Nicaea sits beside a lake, its old walls visible from the road. Eleni is washing a blanket at the shore. Her grandson Leon sits on a stone, holding the wooden pegs she uses to dry it. Each time she asks for one, he gives her two.

“One for the blanket, one for the next blanket,” he explains. Eleni tells you that when he was smaller he tried to dry a fish with a peg. The fish escaped into the lake, taking the peg with it. Leon says he no longer does that. He is watching the water carefully now.

Eleni asks whether you have come from the east or the west. She shows you the lane that meets the road beyond the walls and offers a place to rest. Leon points out the easiest place to walk down to the water, then checks whether his grandmother has enough pegs left for the blanket.

You ask Leon how he knew the fish would take the clip with it. ‘I did not,’ he says. ‘I only found out when Grandmother counted the clips and one was gone.’ Eleni says a neighbour returned it a week later, caught in a reed beside his boat. Leon asks if she still has it. She points to the slightly bent one in his hand. He tries it on the thick edge of the blanket; it holds, though he has to press harder than with the others. Eleni asks him to leave that one for the last corner.

## ev.nicaea.entry.title

By the Lake at Nicaea

## ev.ev_nicaea_entry.choice_1_result

You thank Eleni and continue beside the lake. Leon runs after you to show the firmest part of the shore path. You mark Nicaea and the lake on your map before the road turns away.

## ev.ev_nicaea_entry.choice_2_result

You stay with Eleni's family for the day. Leon helps hang the blanket and then spends the afternoon telling you what he has seen from the shore. At supper Eleni finds the old peg he once lost, washed back among the reeds months later. Leon keeps it in a box now. They give you a place to sleep; you depart the next morning rested and mark Nicaea on your map.

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.title

The Potter's Family Bowl

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.body

A potter named Theodoros sets a cooled, plain glazed bowl in the shade. The glaze has gathered in one thick line near the rim. His daughter Maria holds it up to the light, looking for the place where she first touched the wet clay.

“I thought I spoiled it,” she says. Theodoros pours water into it and sets it on the table. “Your grandmother asked for a bowl that would hold soup. It does.” Maria asks if she can give it to her grandmother herself. He says she should, once they have dried it.

Theodoros asks Maria to show you where the glaze thickened. His brother has left a list of stops on the way to Constantinople; Maria puts it beside the dry bowl so neither is forgotten.

You ask whether Grandmother knows the bowl is ready. Maria says she promised to bring it after the afternoon meal. Theodoros wants her to carry it in a basket, with cloth around the rim so it will not knock against the other dishes. Maria asks if the thick glaze will show when soup is inside. ‘Only if she looks closely,’ he says. She turns the bowl in her hands and says Grandmother always looks closely at anything her family makes.

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.choice.talk

Stay while Maria prepares the bowl

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.choice.route

Read the potter's list of stopping places

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.result.talk

Maria tests the bowl with water once more, dries it, then wraps it in cloth for her grandmother. Theodoros takes you around the potter's yard and shows you where the lane reaches the lake. You add this part of Nicaea to your map. Maria leaves with the bowl held in both hands.

## ev.gap.nicaea.scene.result.route

Theodoros finds the travel list under a stack of bowl moulds. His brother went west toward the Marmara shore and continued to Constantinople; the list records the places where he changed carriers. You copy the names and mark Nicaea, Constantinople, and the route between them. Maria asks you to leave the list where her uncle will find it on his return.

## ev.nicaea.figpath.title

The Figs for Aunt Zoe

## ev.nicaea.figpath.body

Along the lane above the lake, Irem is packing figs into a shallow basket. Her mother has written a message for Aunt Zoe, who lives across the hills on the road toward Smyrna. Irem folds it three times and tucks it beneath the fruit, then takes it out again because juice has begun to leak through the cloth.

Aunt Zoe taught Irem to graft the tree when she was young. This is the first year it has borne enough fruit to send any away. Irem has learned the southward road from her aunt's letters, including the way toward Smyrna. Her mother is walking with her as far as the caravan stop tomorrow and invites you along. They also have lunch ready if you would rather sit with them and hear why Irem kept the tree alive through its first dry summer.

You ask whether the fruit will last until tomorrow. Irem’s mother presses one gently and says the ripest should stay at home; her sister can wait for the firmer ones. ‘Will Aunt Zoe mind if I choose them?’ Irem asks. ‘She will be pleased you grew them,’ her mother replies. Irem moves the damp letter into a dry cloth and asks you to check that the address is still legible. You can read it, though a corner has curled. She straightens it beneath the basket handle and tells you her aunt always writes back with news of the road and the family’s old garden.

## ev.nicaea.figpath.choice.walk

Walk with Irem to the caravan stop tomorrow

## ev.nicaea.figpath.choice.meal

Stay for lunch and hear about the fig tree

## ev.nicaea.figpath.result.walk

You spend the day walking with Irem and her mother. At each turn Irem recalls something Aunt Zoe wrote about the southward road. At the caravan stop they find a traveller bound toward Smyrna and give him the basket, the letter now wrapped in dry cloth. You mark Nicaea, Smyrna, and the route between them on your map. Irem asks the traveller to bring back word when her aunt tastes the fruit.

## ev.nicaea.figpath.result.meal

At lunch, Irem tells you how she carried water to the young tree during its first dry summer. Her mother remembers that Irem forgot her own sandals twice but never forgot the tree. They cut open one ripe fig to share, then pack the rest with fresh cloth. You eat with them and set out feeling stronger.
