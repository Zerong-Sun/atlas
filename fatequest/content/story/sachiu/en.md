## city.sachiu.name

Sachiu

## ev.sachiu.entry.body

After you have travelled thirty days through the Great Desert, you come to a city called
SACHIU, lying between north-east and east. It belongs to the Great Kaan and lies in a
province called Tangut. The people are for the most part Idolaters, though there are
some Nestorian Christians and some Saracens. Those who are Idolaters have a peculiar
language and are no traders, but live by the fruits of the earth. They have many abbeys
and minsters, all full of idols of divers fashions, to which they pay great honour and
show reverence. The city lies on the edge of the Desert, where caravans restock after
the terrible crossing; the farmers grow excellent melons and wheat in the irrigated
fields.

## codex.cx-tangut-rites.body

After thirty days of desert you come to Sachiu, in the province of Tangut, a city lying on the edge of the Great Desert where the caravans restock after the terrible crossing. The people are for the most part Idolaters, with a peculiar language of their own; they are no traders, but live by the fruits of the earth. They have many abbeys and minsters, all full of idols of divers fashions, to which they pay great honour and show reverence. Their dead are burned on pyres, and the ashes are gathered and kept — you watched a burial and set it down in your book.

## ev.ev_sachiu_a.choice_1_result

## ev.sachiu.a.title

Sachiu: The Oasis Gate and the Cliff Shrines

## ev.sachiu.a.body

At the oasis where the desert roads meet, the water-keepers of Sachiu tend the spring that makes the town possible, and the shrine-keepers watch the cliff-shrines cut into the rock. A water-keeper who has measured the spring for forty years says the roads to Camul, to Etzina, and to Campichu all pass by this water, and that a traveller who drinks here without honouring the shrines drinks the desert's hospitality in vain.

## ev.sachiu.a.choice.1

## ev.sachiu.a.choice.2

Ask the water-keeper the roads toward Camul

## ev.sachiu.a.choice.3

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.title

Sachiu: The Water-Keeper's Second Word

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.body

The water-keeper takes you to the spring's head and speaks of the desert as a debtor. He says the water here is owed to every road that crosses the sand — to Camul, to Etzina, to Campichu — and that the shrine-keepers count the travellers who honour it, so that the desert may know the water was not drunk in vain. He asks whether you will be counted among those who repay the oasis.

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_1

Offer a small gift at the spring for the road's sake

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_1_result

Your gift is accepted without ceremony. The water-keeper speaks your name to the spring, and a little favour goes with you on the sand.

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_2

Ask him to name the road toward Campichu

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_2_result

Campichu is marked on your map with the wells between. The Tangut road is open.

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_3

Wait a day at the spring and learn the desert's measure

## ev.ev_sachiu_a_followup.choice_3_result

You wait a day at the spring. The desert's measure is learned, and in Sachiu your name now carries a little more weight.
