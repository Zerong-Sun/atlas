## city.sachiu.name

Sachiu

## ev.sachiu.entry.body

Water runs beside the fields outside Sachiu. A farmer is dividing a melon for travellers while his younger sister carries a covered bowl toward the nearby monastery. You ask whether the bowl is food. She says it holds a little of their grandmother’s ashes; the family is meeting there today to remember her. Her brother wraps a slice of melon for her before she leaves.

A monk coming down the path asks if the sister wants someone to carry the bowl. ‘I can manage,’ she says, then asks whether her mother has arrived. The monk has seen her lighting a lamp inside. The farmer tells you his grandmother used to argue with him about when to open the water channel. ‘She was usually right,’ he says. The sister turns around long enough to add, ‘Especially when you forgot to close it.’

You walk past the monastery as the family gathers. A merchant beside you asks for water before going on, and the farmer points him toward the same channel his grandmother tended.

## codex.cx-tangut-rites.body

After thirty days of desert you come to Sachiu, in the province of Tangut, a city lying on the edge of the Great Desert where the caravans restock after the terrible crossing. The people are for the most part Idolaters, with a peculiar language of their own; they are no traders, but live by the fruits of the earth. They have many abbeys and minsters, all full of idols of divers fashions, to which they pay great honour and show reverence. Their dead are burned on pyres, and the ashes are gathered and kept — you watched a burial and set it down in your book.

## ev.sachiu.site_gap.title

Feed at the Posting Station

## ev.sachiu.site_gap.body

At the posting station, Yulduz is spreading dry grass and beans across a mat. Her son picks out thorns, then asks why the horse at the far stall has less feed. ‘That mare leaves at sunrise,’ Yulduz says. ‘Her rider will feed her again at the next stop.’ The boy looks unconvinced until the rider comes over and confirms it.

You ask how Yulduz knows what each horse needs. She names the distances on the station keeper’s board and shows you where the older animals rest. Her brother arrives with damp grass he has just cut. She asks him to spread it in the sun before bagging it; packed wet, it will spoil on the road. He grumbles that the sky might cloud over, but helps the boy clear a wider patch of ground.

The rider checks his girth beside the mare. Yulduz asks him to tell the next station that she has sent two sacks, one marked for the young horses. The boy sets a clean strip of cloth beside the right sack so the rider will recognise it.

Ask Yulduz about the stations toward Etzina

## ev.ev_sachiu_site_gap.choice_1_result

Yulduz and the rider name the stopping places toward Etzina. Her son notices one board is written with an older station name, so she gives you the name travellers use now. You mark the route on your map.

## ev.sachiu.site_gap.choice.interact

Help her son pick thorns from the grass

## ev.ev_sachiu_site_gap.choice_2_result

You and the boy clear the hard thorns while Yulduz measures the feed. The young horses eat safely, and she explains why she dries each sack before tying it. You remember the station’s way of caring for animals.

## ev.sachiu.trough.title

The Quiet Horse at the Trough

## ev.sachiu.trough.body

Before dawn, a station mare stands by the water trough with her head low. The boy who tends her says she refused the hard stalks last night. His aunt asks him to open her mouth. He hesitates, and she shows him how to lift the lip gently; a thorn has scratched the gum. ‘I picked the feed,’ he says. His aunt answers that finding the thorn now is part of caring for her.

You ask whether the mare can still travel. The aunt says she will wait for the next rider and give this one a softer meal today. the boy brings a bowl of warm water to soak beans, then asks if his father will be angry. ‘He will ask what you did when you noticed,’ she says. The father arrives with a saddle and, seeing the mare, sets it down without scolding.

There is enough work to keep the family busy until evening. the boy holds the bowl while the mare tries a mouthful; his father checks whether she chews comfortably. His aunt waits by the trough with the next sack of feed.

## ev.sachiu.trough.choice.stay

Stay a day and help prepare soft feed

## ev.sachiu.trough.result.stay

You spend the day soaking beans and sorting the soft grass with the boy. By evening the mare eats without flinching. His father thanks you, and you learn a little more about travelling at an animal’s pace.

## ev.sachiu.trough.choice.tell

Tell the next rider why the mare must rest

## ev.sachiu.trough.result.tell

You explain the scratched gum when the next rider arrives. He changes to another mount without argument and brings a cleaner bundle of grass back for the boy. The family thanks you for helping them speak plainly to the travellers.
