## city.bochara.name

Bochara

## ev.bochara.entry.body

At the edge of a Bukhara cloth yard, a dyer is helping his daughter count the rolls that will leave with the next caravan. The girl lays out six, then calls her father back: one has a loose outer cord, and she will not put it on the cart until he checks the cloth. He laughs and unfastens the cord. ‘You caught it before I did.’

A neighbour arrives with a cup of water and asks whether they have room for her bundle too. ‘If your son brings it before the drivers finish loading,’ the dyer says. She asks you where you came from. When you name a road, she points to a lane with places to sleep and says people who have crossed that way often gather there at supper.

The daughter finds the cloth uncut but asks for a fresh wrapping. ‘This roll is yours to watch,’ her father tells her. ‘Count it once more tomorrow morning.’ She asks whether you intend to go on today. You can leave after marking Bukhara on your map, or stay a day by the yard, rest, and listen while the drivers arrange the next load.

## ev.bochara.entry.title

The Cloth Yard in Bukhara

## ev.ev_bochara_entry.choice_1_result

The dyer’s daughter steps aside to let you pass and calls after you to check your water before the next stretch. You mark Bukhara on your map and carry on.

## ev.ev_bochara_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day resting near the yard. By supper, the daughter has retied all six rolls, and the neighbour’s son has brought his family’s bundle. You regain your strength and mark Bukhara on your map.


## ev.bochara.recipe.title

The Baker’s Shared Oven

## ev.bochara.recipe.body

A baker named Hamid has room for one more tray in his oven. His sister has brought dough for her household, but a caravan driver asks him to bake his bread before the wagons leave. Hamid looks at the heat and says both batches will fit if he moves the coals. His sister wants him to eat something first; he has worked since dawn.

‘You promised me the first tray,’ she reminds him. ‘I did,’ he says. ‘You also told me not to send a traveller away hungry.’ The driver puts down his sack. He can wait while Hamid’s bread finishes, but he must return to his wagon before dusk. You ask who will watch the oven when Hamid goes for water. His young nephew raises a hand, then admits he cannot yet tell when the crust is done.

Hamid gives the boy a small piece of dough to practice with. You can stay for the day and help the nephew turn the trays, earning a little pay from Hamid, or buy a warm piece of bread now and eat while his sister takes the next turn at the oven.

## ev.bochara.recipe.choice.work

Stay the day and help with the trays

## ev.bochara.recipe.result.work

You show the boy how the edge of the bread changes colour. When his aunt’s tray is ready, he calls Hamid over before lifting it. Hamid pays you 1 silver for the day and lets the boy take the first finished piece home.

## ev.bochara.recipe.choice.eat

Buy a warm piece for 0.4 silver

## ev.bochara.recipe.result.eat

You pay 0.4 silver, eat the warm bread, and feel better for the road. Hamid’s sister takes the oven fork while he drinks his water; the driver settles down to wait for his batch.
