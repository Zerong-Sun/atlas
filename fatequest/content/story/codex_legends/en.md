## codex.leg-prester-john.name

Prester John

## codex.leg-prester-john.body

Beyond India, say the wonder-books, reigns a Christian prince called Prester
John, whose rivers run with gems and whose salamanders live in fire. Mandeville
gathers the tale as itinerary; this codex keeps it as legend — origin marked
authored, never as a sourced city gate.

## codex.leg-earthly-paradise.name

The Earthly Paradise

## codex.leg-earthly-paradise.body

Some travellers' books place Paradise beyond the deserts of India, guarded so
that no living man may enter. The story is older than any one author. Here it
stands among legends, not among charts.

## codex.chr-daoyi-gulong.name

Dao Yi Zhi Lue · Calicut

## codex.chr-daoyi-gulong.body

Wang Dayuan (1349) notes Guli on the western ocean: a meeting-place of foreign
ships, a king who honours Buddha, elephant and ox, and brokers who fix a price
once and do not change it. Pepper, coconut and western cloth are its staples.

## codex.chr-zhenla-city.name

Zhenla Fengtu Ji · The Walled City

## codex.chr-zhenla-city.body

Zhou Daguan, following a Yuan embassy to Zhenla, describes a square city of
stone towers and five gates, each watched by stone Buddhas. Trade, he says, is
often done by women; a Tang man who settles there may take a local wife who
knows the market.

## codex.chr-xingcha-malacca.name

Xingcha Shenglan · Malacca

## codex.chr-xingcha-malacca.body

Fei Xin, sailing with Zheng He, records that Malacca was raised from a Siamese
dependency to a kingdom by Yongle patent. Treasure ships raised stockades and
warehouses here. The people follow Islam; the harbour faces the open sea.

## codex.chr-changchun-audience.name

Changchun's Audience

## codex.chr-changchun-audience.body

Qiu Chuji, summoned west by Chinggis Khan, answered that there is a way to
guard life, but no medicine of immortality. Li Zhichang wrote the journey down;
the frank reply became a touchstone of the steppe road.

## codex.chr-yelu-xiyou.name

Yelü Chucai · Record of a Journey West

## codex.chr-yelu-xiyou.body

Yelü Chucai followed the Khan into the lands beyond the Altai and wrote a short
geography of cities and products, fearing later ages would forget what the
campaign had seen. His prose is spare; it pairs with Changchun's longer road.
