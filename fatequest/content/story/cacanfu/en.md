## city.cacanfu.name

Cacanfu

## ev.cacanfu.entry.body

A boat has come in with silk and wet grain sacks stacked too close together. The boat owner asks his daughter to move the silk onto the raised shelf before the evening damp reaches it. She tries to lift a roll alone; it catches against a nail. ‘Wait,’ her father says. He puts a piece of cloth over the nail and shows her where to take the weight. You steady the roll while they shift it. At the quay, a broker is trying to find the family that owns the grain. He recognizes the mark on one sack but not the boatman’s handwriting. The daughter reads the mark aloud and points him to the right household across town. ‘Do northbound boats use this channel?’ you ask. The owner draws the turn on his board. ‘We leave the landing here; ask again at the larger waterway.’ His daughter points to the dry shelf. ‘Ask about the silk too. I can show you the market tomorrow,’ she says. She wants you to see how merchants keep their dry goods. He says you can also follow the street past the quay today if you need to leave at once.

## ev.cacanfu.entry.title

The Canal Boat’s Dry Shelf

## ev.ev_cacanfu_entry.choice_1_result

The daughter finishes moving the silk to the shelf while her father sends the broker to the grain owner. She points out the street beyond the quay. You follow it across town, knowing where to return if you need the canal landing.

## ev.ev_cacanfu_entry.choice_2_result

You stay another day. The daughter brings you through the market, asks two sellers to show how they store silk clear of damp sacks, and takes you back by a different lane. Her father shows you the canal branch on his route board before you leave.

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.title

The Bean Sack on the Scale

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.body

At the canal quay, a weigher is trying to settle the weight of a bean sack. The seller has brought an old heavy sack; the buyer has only counted the beans. ‘I used this cloth because the thin bags split,’ the seller says. The weigher asks them to empty one sack and weigh the cloth separately. The seller’s son says that will spill the beans onto the boards. You offer a clean tray, and the buyer helps hold its edge. They tip the beans out carefully. The empty cloth is heavier than anyone expected. The weigher writes its weight on a separate line and asks the son to read the figure back. The boy has been watching boats on the canal and asks where the one bound toward Linju turns. The buyer comes by road from Linju. ‘The cart track turns past the last farm,’ he says, drawing the bend in the dust while the weigher puts the beans back. The son repeats both the weight and the turn, then asks his father to keep the two figures on the next sales note.

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.choice.route

Ask the buyer about the Linju route

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.choice.route_result

The buyer marks the way toward Linju on your board, including the bend he had drawn for the boy. The weigher checks that the bean sack is closed again before the buyer loads it onto his cart. You carry away a clear route while they finish the sale.

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.choice.scene

Help weigh the empty sack

## ev.ev_gap_cacanfu.choice.scene_result

You hold the tray while the seller and buyer lift the empty cloth onto the scale. The weigher writes down the sack’s weight, and the son reads it aloud without losing his place. They agree on the bean weight and load the sack onto the buyer’s cart together.

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.title

The Bowl at the Canal Stall

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.body

A food seller near the canal has made more noodles than her one table can hold. Porters come in from the boats, and her son is washing bowls as quickly as they return. One porter puts down a coin and asks for his sister’s portion to be kept warm; she is still counting cargo at the quay. The seller covers a bowl, but her son sets it beside the bowls waiting to be washed. ‘Shall I rinse this one too?’ he asks. ‘That one is for someone who has not eaten yet,’ she reminds him. ‘Then I will mark it,’ he says, fetching a red thread. He moves it to the stove and ties a red thread around its handle. You ask whether there is room to sit. She makes space beside an old boatman who has been waiting for his daughter. He tells the boy how she once worked the same shift and often arrived after the broth cooled. When the sister finally comes, she sees the thread and laughs at her brother’s careful system. The seller asks you to help carry bowls during the rush, or stay until tomorrow when the family will have time to make a proper meal for you.

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.choice.serve

Help serve the late porters

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.result.serve

You carry the covered bowl to the porter’s sister and bring the empty ones back to the boy. He keeps the red thread for the next late arrival. The seller thanks you in front of her regular customers and saves you a seat whenever you return.

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.choice.rest

Stay a day and eat with the family

## ev.cacanfu.noodle.result.rest

You stay another day, helping the son wash bowls after the morning rush. At noon the seller serves you a hot meal at her own table, and the old boatman’s daughter joins you after work. You leave rested, having had time to hear how the family feeds the quay each day.
