## city.novogardia.name

Novogardia

## ev.novogardia.entry.title

The River Market

## ev.novogardia.entry.body

At Novgorod’s river market, a woman named Anfisa counts bundles of wax while her father argues with a boatman over space. Her daughter carries a birchbark note from one stall to another: a neighbour has borrowed their scales again.

‘Tell him to bring them back before noon,’ Anfisa calls. Then she turns to you and explains where travellers gather for the road south. The city’s churches and trading yards stand close to the water; the river makes it easy for news to arrive with the cargo.

Her father asks whether they can use another trader’s scales. Anfisa shakes her head: ‘I promised the buyer I would weigh it on ours, so he can check the mark himself.’ The girl returns without the scales and says the neighbour has found one pan bent and is coming to apologize. Anfisa asks her to help lay out the bundles in order. You ask where to find a bed after the market closes; she points to a house behind the landing, then reminds her daughter that the small bundles go first.

## ev.gap.novogardia.scene.title

Anfisa’s Borrowed Scales

## ev.gap.novogardia.scene.body

The scales come back with one pan bent. Anfisa’s neighbour says it slipped from his cart, and he offers to repair it. Her father is ready to weigh the wax by hand, but Anfisa wants the pan straight before she sells any.

Her daughter asks you to hold the stand while the neighbour works. ‘He borrowed it to weigh flour for his sister,’ she explains. ‘She was making bread for the boatmen.’ Anfisa knows the southbound road toward Moscow and can tell you where it starts.

The neighbour tries the pan against the edge of his cart and asks Anfisa to look. ‘A little more,’ she says. Her father offers a wooden wedge, but the neighbour says he can straighten it without one. You ask why she is so particular about a small bend. Anfisa sets a piece of wax in the pan: ‘The buyer will put his own weight on the other side. If this pan hangs low, we argue all afternoon.’ Her daughter holds the stand steady as he makes one last adjustment.

## ev.gap.novogardia.scene.result.talk

You steady the stand while the neighbour bends the pan back into shape. Anfisa weighs one small bundle to test it. It balances, and you mark her trading yard in Novgorod on your map.

## ev.gap.novogardia.scene.result.route

As Anfisa tells you which yard the southbound carts leave from, the neighbour straightens the pan. She tests it with a small wax bundle, then explains how the road reaches Moscow. You mark the route and city before the next boat unloads.

## ev.novogardia.barknote.title

The Note in the Wax Bundle

## ev.novogardia.barknote.body

A boatman finds a birchbark note tucked into Anfisa’s wax bundle. It is a reminder from her sister: bring a little wax home for candles and stop working after the evening bell. Anfisa laughs at the second part but folds the note carefully.

Her daughter offers to carry a small parcel to her aunt. You can walk with her and see the town beyond the market, or stay to help Anfisa finish weighing the last bundles before dusk. Anfisa will pay for the work.

‘Did she really write that I should stop at the bell?’ Anfisa asks her daughter. ‘Twice,’ the girl says, showing her the second line. Anfisa tucks a little wax into the parcel and asks you whether it will survive the walk without breaking. Her daughter suggests wrapping it in old cloth. Her father calls that the last bundles are ready for the scales. Anfisa hands the parcel to the girl and says she may go as soon as someone helps finish the weighing.

You help Anfisa weigh the last bundles, then spend the night nearby. At dawn she pays you 0.5 silver for the day’s work. Her daughter returns with a candle her aunt made from last year’s wax.
