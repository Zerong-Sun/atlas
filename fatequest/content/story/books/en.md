## book.polo.title

The Travels of Marco Polo

## book.polo.subtitle

Yule–Cordier · Venice to Cathay

## book.polo.blurb

The spine of this game's first chapter. Polo's book is cut into places and
stories that feed city entries, road encounters, and the codex. What you read
on the map can usually be traced back to a chapter here.

## book.polo.passage

When you leave the city of Tauris (Tabriz) and ride east, you enter a land of many
cities and of merchants who know every road from the Black Sea to Cathay. Of
Cambaluc and of Kinsay the book speaks at length — of courts, of canals, of
harbours thick with masts. This desk keeps only a leaf; the journey keeps the
rest.

## book.battuta.title

The Travels of Ibn Battuta

## book.battuta.subtitle

The Rihla · Tangier to the ends of the earth

## book.battuta.blurb

Battuta's voice opens the Islamic cities Polo scarcely names — Cairo, Mecca,
Damascus, Delhi, the Maldives. Our bindings use passage-level extracts, not
whole abridged chapters, so each city citation can be checked in one glance.

## book.battuta.passage

I left Tangier, my birthplace, on Thursday the second of Rajab 725, with the
intention of making the Pilgrimage to the Holy House and visiting the tomb of
the Prophet. I swore that I would never travel a second time the same road, if
I could help it — and for years the roads kept that oath for me.

## book.rubruck.title

The Journey of William of Rubruck

## book.rubruck.subtitle

Rockhill 1900 · to the court of Mangu Khan

## book.rubruck.blurb

A Franciscan's report from 1253–55: felt idols, kumiss, and a debate before
the Khan at Caracarum. Public-domain Rockhill translation. Excerpts feed the
steppe desk and legend of the religious disputation.

## book.rubruck.passage

Of the city of Caracarum you must know that, exclusive of the palace of the
Khan, it is not as large as the village of Saint Denis. There are two
quarters in it: one of the Saracens, where the market is; the other of the
Cathayans, who are all craftsmen. There are twelve temples of idols, two
mosques, and one Christian church at the far end of the town.

## book.odoric.title

The Travels of Friar Odoric

## book.odoric.subtitle

Yule 1866 · Cathay and the Way Thither

## book.odoric.blurb

Odoric saw Zayton's harbour and the bridges of Cansay a generation after Polo.
Yule's edition is public domain. Desk passages sit beside Polo's China without
displacing his chapter bindings.

## book.odoric.passage

I came to a certain city called Zayton, which hath a port twice as great as
any in the world. In this city friars dwell, and porcelain is made in great
quantity. From Zayton one may sail to many isles of India.

## book.conti.title

The Travels of Niccolò de' Conti

## book.conti.subtitle

Hakluyt Society 1857 · India in the Fifteenth Century

## book.conti.blurb

A Venetian merchant's years in Calicut, Pegu and Malabar, written down by
Poggio after Conti's confession in Rome. Public-domain Major translation.
Useful for the Indian Ocean desk and monsoon notes.

## book.conti.passage

Calicut is a maritime city, noble and noted for its harbour. Pepper grows
here in great abundance. Ships come from all parts of India, from Ethiopia,
Arabia and China. Conti lived here long enough to learn the tongue of the
merchants.

## book.tafur.title

Travels and Adventures of Pero Tafur

## book.tafur.subtitle

Letts 1926 · Mediterranean and the Near East

## book.tafur.blurb

A Castilian gentleman's tour of 1435–39: Constantinople, Cairo, Cyprus. The
Letts English version (1926) is public domain in the United States. Desk
excerpts only — bindings stay conservative.

## book.tafur.passage

I came to Constantinople, which is a great city, though much of it is in
ruins. Saint Sophia still stands in majesty, and the walls that have held so
many sieges yet enclose the city. Across the Horn the Genoese keep Pera.

## book.zhenghe.title

Yingya Shenglan · The Overall Survey of the Ocean's Shores

## book.zhenghe.subtitle

Ma Huan / Zhang Sheng · Zheng He's voyages

## book.zhenghe.blurb

Chinese harbour chapters for Champa, Java, Malacca, Calicut, Aden and Hormuz.
The desk reads from the classical Chinese abridgement; English glosses are
authored for play. City map bindings remain with Polo where he already
speaks; Zheng He's voice lives here and in the maritime codex.

## book.zhenghe.passage

自闽之长乐县五虎门发舟，西南行顺风约十日可抵占城。港岸立石塔为标，舶至是系焉。王尚释教，跨象或乘小车。市交易以金，极宝爱中国青磁器暨纻丝。

## book.pinto.title

Peregrinação · Mendes Pinto

## book.pinto.subtitle

Desk excerpt · not used as city evidence

## book.pinto.blurb

Fernão Mendes Pinto's sprawling romance of captivity and coasts. Our English
excerpt is rough machine prose — honest enough for a desk leaf, never strong
enough to bind a city. Marked authored / desk-only.

## book.pinto.passage

I spent one-and-twenty years in the parts of India, Ethiopia, Arabia, China
and the eastern archipelago — thirteen times a captive, seventeen times sold.
I leave this rude writing so that my children may see the labours of that
road.
