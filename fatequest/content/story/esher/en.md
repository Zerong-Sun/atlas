## city.esher.name

Esher

## ev.esher.entry.body

At Esher, a date seller has left a basket beside the harbour clerk's table. Her younger brother has used the basket lid as a tray for his lunch. “When I need that lid, you are always eating,” she tells him. He puts it back and asks which ship she is waiting for. “The one carrying our uncle's letter,” she says. The clerk finds the ship on his list and promises to call when its cargo is counted. You ask how the road out of town runs. The date seller points past the incense sheds and offers to walk you there after she has read the letter. Her brother says he can show you now, but he first has to return the neighbour's water jar. Nearby, merchants are taking frankincense from the storehouse and carrying food toward the boats. At the mosque door a visitor finishes washing and makes room for another traveller. The date seller shades her eyes, looking for the incoming sail.

## ev.esher.a.title

A Horse Waiting for the Gangplank

## ev.esher.a.body

At Esher's harbour, a groom is giving a horse water before it boards a ship. The animal watches men carry crates across a gangplank and refuses to come near it. “He heard the wood move under the last crate,” the groom tells his daughter. She asks the crew to secure the plank and walks over it herself. “Now let him see me come back,” she says. The horse follows her as far as the shade. A customs clerk meanwhile checks the horse's tag against the ship's list. You ask where the arriving vessels came from; he names the coast toward Aden and the boats that call at Dufar. The groom asks whether you are looking for passage or a horse of your own. His daughter says the horse will be ready to move when the loading noises settle. The clerk invites you to stay and watch how he marks both incoming cargo and the animals going out.

## ev.esher.b.title

A Frankincense Seller’s Ledger

## ev.esher.b.body

In a shaded store at Esher, an incense seller is dividing a large lot of frankincense. His sister has written the buyer's mark on every sack, but one tag is upside down. “That is yours,” she says, handing it to him. He turns the sack and recognizes his own crooked letters. A customer asks why the resin is kept in a dry room while dates are sold outside. The sister breaks a pale piece and lets him smell it. “If the sacks grow damp, you won't want to pay for this scent.” Their niece brings in a tray of dates from the neighbouring stall and asks whether they may take a short break before weighing the last sack. The seller agrees, then asks you whether you want a full trade sack, a little food for the road, or directions to the ships toward Aden and across to Axuma. He waits for his sister to finish the account before anyone carries a sack away.

You spend a day at the harbour stalls. The clerk finally reads out the ship's name, and the date seller opens her uncle's letter beside you. She tells you which families carry food to the boats and which people keep the incense sheds. By evening you know the town's streets and its trade.

## ev.ev_esher_entry.choice_3_result

You leave your shoes at the mosque and wait while the attendant shows a visiting family where to sit. Afterwards he points out the lane to the harbour. The date seller sees you returning and thanks you for making room for the family.

## ev.ev_esher_a.choice_2_result

A pilot names the boats that call at Dufar and shows you how he waits for a safe tide at the harbour mouth. The groom's daughter listens and asks which vessel will take their horse. You leave with the coastal departure clearer in mind; the clerk invites you back to hear about the next ships.

## ev.ev_esher_a.choice_3_result

You pay the groom for a horse after he leads it on level ground for you. His daughter checks the lead rope, and the clerk confirms the tag. The groom tells you how this town prepares horses for sea voyages and asks you to give the animal a drink before you leave.

## ev.ev_esher_b.choice_1_result

You pay for a full trade sack of frankincense. The seller's sister checks its mark against the ledger and has him retie the label before handing it over. She tells you how they keep the resin dry, and you leave with the sack properly packed.

## ev.ev_esher_b.choice_2_result

You buy a small serving of dates from the niece's tray and eat it in the shade. Her uncle finishes weighing the last sack while you rest. She offers you water before you leave; the food restores some of your strength for the road.

## ev.ev_esher_b.choice_3_result

The seller's sister opens her route sheet and points along the coast toward Aden, then across the water toward Axuma. She asks the niece to repeat the names, and the girl adds the carrier who currently takes letters on each passage. You leave with both destinations in mind.

## ev.ev_esher_a_followup.title

Esher: Another Harbour List

## ev.ev_esher_a_followup.body

The harbour clerk waves you over when the next vessel comes in. His list shows cargo due from the coast, but the porter beside him has put a sack of frankincense under the wrong merchant's mark. “Read the tag again,” the clerk tells him. The porter turns it toward the light and moves the sack to the right place. A trader waiting nearby says he has another full sack for sale if you want one. You ask the clerk which ship will sail next. “One is bound toward Aden,” he says, “and a pilot has just come from Dufar.” The pilot joins them and spreads his route sheet over a crate. A woman carrying letters asks about the crossing to Axuma, so he marks that departure too. The clerk gives the porter his corrected list and asks him to read it aloud before the cargo leaves the shed.

## ev.esher.cat.title

The Cat under the Date Baskets

## ev.esher.cat.body

A harbour scribe is looking for his mother's grey cat among the date baskets outside her house. His little sister insists the cat came back from the quay before lunch. “I fed him,” she says. “Then you must know where he went,” he answers. She crouches beside the baskets and finds only a strip of torn awning cloth. Their mother opens the door and asks why both children are under her stall. The scribe shows her the strip; wind has pulled the awning loose, leaving a gap that the cat likes to climb through. She says he will come home when the yard grows quiet, but the awning needs fixing before the next rain. The sister hears a scrape behind a basket and points. You can help her bring the cat out carefully, or take the tools her mother offers and mend the shade while the children look for him. Her mother puts the ladder by the door, where it will not block the neighbours.

## ev.esher.cat.choice.cat

Help the children bring out the cat

## ev.esher.cat.result.cat

You move the basket slowly while the sister holds out a bit of food. The cat emerges and lets the scribe carry him indoors. Their mother thanks you for taking care around the stall, and the neighbouring sellers call to see whether the cat is home.

## ev.esher.cat.choice.awning

Repair the awning for the mother

## ev.esher.cat.result.awning

You fasten the torn awning while the mother holds the ladder. The children coax the cat from behind the baskets before you finish. She tests the shade, pays you for the repair, and tells them to give the cat a place inside before the wind rises again.
