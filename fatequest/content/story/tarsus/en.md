## city.tarsus.name

Tarsus

## ev.tarsus.entry.choice.pass

Thank Hadiya and continue along the river road

## ev.tarsus.entry.choice.rest

Spend a day at Hadiya's awning

## ev.tarsus.entry.body

A river runs beside the road into Tarsus. Under an awning, Hadiya is tying a roll of cloth to a cart. Her son Yusuf holds the rope while she pulls it tight. He has put the knot on the wrong side, and each time the cart moves the loose end brushes his ear.

“Let me try once more,” he says. Hadiya lets him undo it. She says that when she was young, her mother made her pack the cart for a journey into the hills. She tied everything so tightly that nobody could get the food out at supper. Yusuf asks whether his grandmother still remembers. “She tells that story every time she sees me with a rope.”

Hadiya offers you a place in the shade and points out where the road crosses the water. Yusuf has finally moved the knot to the right side. He asks you to watch the cart while he rolls it a few steps to test his work.

You ask whether the load is meant for the market today. Hadiya says a neighbour is waiting for two rolls, while the rest will leave with a caravan tomorrow. Yusuf pushes the cart a short distance; the knot stays clear of the wheel. ‘Now it works,’ he says. Hadiya checks the lower roll, then asks him to tell the neighbour exactly which cloth is hers. He reads the mark on the wrapping aloud. A ferryman calls from the bank that the crossing is open, and Hadiya points out where you can reach it without bringing the cart through the narrow lane.

## ev.tarsus.entry.title

The River Road at Tarsus

## ev.ev_tarsus_entry.choice_1_result

The cart rolls without losing its load. Yusuf checks the knot and waves you onward. You thank Hadiya, cross the water, and mark Tarsus on your map.

## ev.ev_tarsus_entry.choice_2_result

You stay beneath the awning for the day. Hadiya lets you rest while she and Yusuf finish loading. At supper Yusuf asks his grandmother about the cart story; she tells it with several new details, and Hadiya protests that half of them never happened. They make room for you to sleep. You leave the next morning stronger and mark Tarsus on your map.

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.title

Meryem's Spindle

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.body

Meryem spins cotton thread in a small courtyard. Her nephew Cem has been asked to wind the finished thread into a ball. Every few turns he stops to straighten a tangle, then winds so quickly that another one forms.

“The cloth is for your mother,” Meryem reminds him. Cem says he knows; he wants her to have it before his father leaves with a caravan. He has never been to Antioch himself, though his father has carried bundles there and told him about the road.

Meryem passes you the spindle so she can free the thread caught beneath Cem's foot. She can show you the lanes around this courtyard. Cem offers to fetch his father's travel notes if you are going toward Antioch.

You ask Jem whether his mother knows about the new cloth. ‘She knows Aunt Meryem has wool,’ he says. ‘She does not know we chose the blue thread.’ Meryem tells him to leave the surprise alone until the weaving is done. He asks how many more bundles need winding. She holds up three fingers; he looks at the tangle by his shoe and admits it may take longer than he hoped. A neighbour knocks at the gate to collect a spindle, and Meryem asks Jem to show her where he put it before he starts another ball.

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.choice.talk

Help Meryem untangle the thread

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.choice.route

Look at Cem's father's road notes

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.result.talk

You lift the spindle while Meryem frees the strand beneath Cem's foot. Cem starts winding again, more slowly this time. Meryem takes you around the nearby lanes as the thread dries, pointing out the courtyard where travellers can ask for clean water. You add this part of Tarsus to your map.

## ev.gap.tarsus.scene.result.route

Cem finds his father's notes beneath a folded cloth. He reads out the stopping places on the way southeast toward Antioch, and Meryem corrects the name of one village from her own memory. You mark Tarsus, Antioch, and the road between them. Cem puts the notes back carefully; his father will want them when he leaves.

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.title

The Basket for the Journey

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.body

Near the river, Hiba is weaving a basket from reeds. Her grandson Aman has made the handle. It is wide enough for his hand but too short for the basket, and he keeps trying to force it into place.

The basket is for his aunt, who carries bread to relatives outside Tarsus. She lost her old one when a donkey stepped through it. Aman wants to give her a new basket before her next trip. Hiba tells him to soak another reed and make the handle longer. “You can tell her the donkey helped with the design,” she says.

Hiba has several baskets to finish for neighbours and offers a day's pay if you stay to weave with them. You can also sit for a while, share the bread Aman's aunt brought, and hear how the family learned to work by the river.

Aman asks whether the donkey will step on the new basket too. Hiba says his aunt has moved the feeding place since last time, and the animal now stands farther from the door. You ask how they know the handle is long enough. Hiba puts two loaves inside and tells Aman to lift it without squeezing the rim. He tries, then takes out one reed and weaves it back through the handle. ‘Now Aunt can carry it with her arm,’ he says. Hiba checks the knot and asks him to set the basket in the shade until it dries.

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.choice.work

Stay a day and weave baskets for a wage

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.choice.meal

Sit with Hiba and share the bread

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.result.work

You spend the day soaking reeds and holding the basket frames while Hiba and Aman work. Aman makes a new handle. His aunt arrives before dusk, puts six loaves inside, and lifts the basket without straining it. Hiba pays you the promised wage. You leave the next morning as Aman is showing the neighbours how he made the longer handle.

## ev.tarsus.reedbasket.result.meal

You eat bread beside the river. Hiba says her daughter learned to weave baskets as a child, though she now spends more time carrying bread than making them. Aman listens, then quietly removes his short handle and starts a longer one. His aunt arrives while you are still there and promises to return for it tomorrow. The meal leaves you ready for the road.
