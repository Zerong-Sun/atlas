## ev.hierusalem.entry.title

The Soap Basket

## ev.hierusalem.entry.body

As you enter Jerusalem, workers in a soap shop are cutting hardened soap into blocks. Nadia, the apprentice, counts them against a list of names. She needs six for an inn downhill. On the fifth, the master takes a block with a chipped corner away.

"It still washes," Nadia says. The master turns the chip towards her. "Last time the innkeeper said the children thought a chipped block had already been used. Give him a whole one."

The shop smells of olive oil. People with different accents pass the door, some going to worship, others looking for a room. Nadia shows you her list. "I have a delivery to make. Tell me which way you're leaving town. I know these lanes."

## ev.hierusalem.entry.choice.pass

Ask Nadia for the gate and continue

## ev.hierusalem.entry.choice.look

Help Nadia deliver the soap and stay in town for a night

## ev.ev_hierusalem_entry.choice_1_result

Nadia carries the basket as far as the corner and points to the gate. "Follow the stone road, then turn right at the cloth seller." She stops a messenger and asks him to walk with you as far as that shop.

When you look back, Nadia has turned into the narrow lane towards the inn. All six blocks are wrapped neatly in her basket.

## ev.ev_hierusalem_entry.choice_2_result

You help Nadia deliver the six blocks. The innkeeper counts them and offers you both a drink while he finds his coins. Nadia asks where tomorrow's caravan for Petra will gather. You spend the night in town, and in the morning she shows you the yard.

A driver is tying a bell to his mule. He tells you to leave by the eastern gate and join his group there. "Fill your water today." Nadia remembers that advice and fills the inn's water bucket on her way back to the shop.

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.title

What Kind of Incense Grandmother Likes

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.body

At the incense stall, a boy holds a few coins and points to two packets of frankincense. "Which one does my grandmother like?" The seller looks up. "Which grandmother?"

The boy says she lives south of the city and always tells him to leave the window open a little when she burns incense. The seller opens both packets for him to smell. He bends close, hesitates, and is distracted by a neighbour grinding cumin. His grandmother sent him out after breakfast with an old cloth pouch and enough money for a small packet. She was baking bread when he left and promised to tell him a story while the incense burned. He would rather bring home the right scent than return with the coins.

The seller puts a grain on a charcoal chip and covers it with a little copper hood. A woman waiting for spices recognises the grandmother's name and says she buys incense here before winter, but cannot remember which packet she chose last time. The boy fans the smoke towards her; she laughs and asks him to let it settle first. He turns to you. "Could you smell them with me? She'll be waiting for me at home."

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.choice.talk

Help the boy smell the two kinds of incense

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.choice.route

Help him choose, then ask for the road to Acre

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.result.talk

The seller lifts the hood from each sample in turn. The first scent fades quickly; the second stays on the boy's sleeve. "Grandmother asks me to open the window because I make the charcoal too hot," he says. "She likes the second one."

The seller weighs a small packet and adds a grain for the boy to try at home. He holds it carefully on the way out. The seller tells him to let his grandmother tend the fire; the boy says he has learned something this time too.

## ev.gap.hierusalem.scene.result.route

The boy chooses the longer-lasting incense. The seller wraps it and turns to you. "Acre is north. Leave by the northern gate and find a caravan going to the coast. They'll know the wells and villages."

The boy says his grandmother went to Acre when she was young. She told him the sea wind filled her clothes, and he has always wanted to see whether it does. The seller laughs and sends him home before she has to wait any longer.

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.title

Two Pairs of Sandals at the Inn

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.body

At the inn, Maria the cobbler sets two pairs of sandals on the steps. Two travellers brought them in together yesterday; today each claims the pair on the left. One says its sole has red stitching. The other says a friend stitched that red line for him.

Maria turns both pairs over. "No need to grab. Which of you had a pebble caught in the heel? I kept it in a box while I worked." One man touches his heel and admits he walked with it half the day.

The other is travelling to Bethlehem and worries his companions will leave at noon. Maria asks you to hold the little box. "I remember what was wrong with the other pair too. Let me tell them."

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.choice.sort

Help Maria return both pairs to their owners

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.choice.road

Ask the traveller where the Bethlehem group leaves from

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.result.sort

Maria turns back a strap on the second pair and shows you a fresh mend. "I caught it on a doorstep yesterday," the other traveller says. They put on their own shoes and walk a few steps along the street, laughing at the mix-up.

Maria tips the pebble into the dirt beside the steps. The innkeeper brings water and remembers that you helped her.

## ev.jerusalem.sandals.result.road

The traveller points south. "Leave by the south gate for Bethlehem. We're going soon; you can meet us there." He reaches for a pair and then stops to ask Maria which is his.

She shows the old tear in each shoe. He puts his pair on, runs to the corner and back, and says it no longer rubs his heel. Then he and his companion head for the southern gate.
