## ev.babylonia-cairus.entry.body

Cairo, which the Latins call Babylonia — the greatest city of the Mamluk realm. A
hundred minarets rise above the Nile; the citadel of Salah al-Din crowns the hill. The
spice markets of the Khan al-Khalili never sleep; merchants from India, Yemen, and the
Maghreb crowd the streets. The Caliph's ancient palace is gone, but the Sultan's Mamluk
cavalry — ten thousand Circassian and Turkic slave-soldiers — patrol the walls. All
goods that pass from the Indian Ocean to the Mediterranean must pass through the
Babylonia customs, and the Sultan takes a handsome share.
