## ev.babylonia-cairus.entry.title

The Lunch Basket by the River

## ev.babylonia-cairus.entry.body

At the Nile, a water carrier and his daughter are arguing over a lunch basket. She wants to take it to her uncle on Rawda Island, where he records the river's level. The carrier has just filled two heavy jars and does not want her to board the ferry alone.

"Leave it here. I'll go when I've delivered the water," he says. The girl holds the basket tighter. "You said that last time. Uncle waited until dark and his food went cold."

Seeing you search for the road, the carrier sets down a jar and points to the landing. "Go through Fustat by the river, then on into Cairo. If you want the island, wait for that small boat to come back." The girl asks the boatman when he is leaving. He promises to wait a little longer.

The boatman asks the girl whose hands will carry the basket once the boat lands. "My uncle's," she says. "He always waits by the water marks." Her father adjusts the cloth over the food and tells her to sit where he can see her until the boat leaves. A second water carrier comes for his jars and offers to take one of the father's deliveries. The father hesitates, checks the address on the jar, and asks the girl to tell the boatman her uncle's name once more.

## ev.ev_babylonia_cairus_entry.choice_1_result

The carrier entrusts the basket to the boatman and takes up his jars for the next house. He shows you the way to the city gate. "Follow the stalls with awnings. Don't turn into the grain yard."

The girl waves from the boat. It has not left yet, but she is already checking whether the bread in the basket has been crushed.

## ev.ev_babylonia_cairus_entry.choice_2_result

You help the carrier deliver his water and stay by the river that night. The boatman carries the first basket across for the girl. In the morning she prepares another basket of bread, and her father takes her to the island. Her uncle eats the bread she brought and shows you the marked column inside the measuring well. He records where the water reaches each day. People who tend the canals and fields come to ask him.

On the way back, the carrier points out the yard where caravans for Petra gather. He says his daughter has won this time: she will set the hour for future lunch deliveries.

## ev.gap.babylonia-cairus.scene.title

The Second Loaf at the Canal

## ev.gap.babylonia-cairus.scene.body

At a bread stall by the canal, a young woman who carries grain holds two loaves and hesitates. Amin, the baker, looks at his board. "I only took payment for one, didn't I?"

She tells him the other is the loaf he promised her little brother this morning. Amin remembers. "He came early. He said you had to cross the river and might miss lunch." She asks whether the second loaf can wait in the oven until the boy returns from school.

Amin puts the loaves back on his board and draws a circle in charcoal at the corner. "I'll know your mark when he comes." Then he turns to you. "Where are you going? Someone passing through here will know the road."

A customer asks whether the fresh bread will be ready before the crossing boat leaves. Amin lifts one edge to check the colour and tells her to wait a little. The young woman watches the canal; her brother's school is on the far bank, and she worries that he will run here before she has finished carrying grain. Amin says he can keep one bread warm under a cloth. He asks her to write the boy's name on the board so he does not hand it to the wrong child.

## ev.gap.babylonia-cairus.scene.result.talk

The young woman says her brother is named Musa. You write it on the back of Amin's board so he will remember during the rush. She asks if he can give the boy a spoonful of mashed beans with his bread. Amin says yes; he will keep a loaf for himself when the oven closes.

When you pass the canal at dusk, Musa is sitting by the stall with his bread rolled around the beans. His sister has not returned from carrying grain, so he has saved half for her.

## ev.gap.babylonia-cairus.scene.result.route

Amin points to the wagons by the canal. "They leave for Alexandria tomorrow by the northern road. The drivers buy bread here before each journey. I'll call one over."

The young woman wraps her loaf. She is going to the harbour herself in a few days to see her uncle. She writes down the driver's name and says she will ask Musa if he has a message for him.

## ev.cairo.caravan.title

Date Baskets in the Caravan Yard

## ev.cairo.caravan.body

In the eastern caravan yard, two sisters sort dates into small baskets. The older one picks out the softest fruit; her sister slips a few back in. The older girl sees her and rolls up her sleeve. "You have your own share for the road."

Their mother is travelling to Petra to visit her sister, whom she has not seen since the last flood. The younger girl wants good dates in every basket so her aunt's children can taste them too. Their mother is indoors sewing a waterskin. She comes out to test the seam, blows air into it, and asks why the girls have filled three baskets when she meant to carry two. The younger one admits she packed a third for the relatives. Mother smiles and asks them to make it small enough for the mule.

Another group in the yard is preparing for Bethlehem. Both groups draw from the same well, their buckets lined up beside it. A driver walks past with a bundle of blankets and nearly takes the wrong water jar. The younger sister calls him back, then looks at her unmarked baskets. "What if they take ours by mistake?" she asks you. The older one has a piece of charcoal but her hands are sticky from the dates. Their mother is still holding the waterskin open, waiting to know which basket to tie to the mule.

## ev.cairo.caravan.choice.baskets

Help the sisters mark their baskets

## ev.cairo.caravan.choice.bethlehem

Carry the baskets inside, then ask when the Bethlehem caravan leaves

## ev.cairo.caravan.result.baskets

You find a piece of charcoal. The younger sister draws a little star under each basket while the older writes their mother's name. Their mother comes out with the sewn waterskin and sees the marks at once. "Who taught you that?" The girl says she thought of it herself; even far from home, they will recognise their baskets.

The older sister puts the softest dates in her mother's small bag. They thank you, and the keeper of the yard remembers your help.

## ev.cairo.caravan.result.bethlehem

You carry the baskets to the door. Their mother is testing the waterskin seam and asks the girls to move the fruit into the shade. Outside, a driver bound for Bethlehem says his group leaves after dawn through the east gate and will wait for the rear wagons at the crossing.

The younger sister hears you asking directions and presses a date into your hand. "For the road. If you see a basket with a little star, that's ours."
