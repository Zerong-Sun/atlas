## city.pentam.name

Pentam

## ev.pentam.entry.body

At Pentam’s landing, a girl is chalking the names of boats onto freshwater jars. Her grandfather carries the filled jars down from the well, and she tells him which boat has already paid. A sailor from a larger vessel points to one marked for another crew. ‘We ordered two,’ he says. She checks the cord on his receipt and finds that his mate wrote down tomorrow’s delivery. The grandfather sets his load beneath the shade while they sort it out. ‘If we give you that jar now, whose boat waits thirsty?’ he asks. The sailor goes to bring his mate and returns with an empty jar of his own. The girl promises to fill it after the next walk to the well. You ask how the larger ship will leave through the shallow water. Her grandfather points to a pilot’s little boat moored beyond the jars. ‘Ask him when the tide turns,’ he says. The girl gives the sailor his corrected receipt and asks whether you are going on through the town or staying to watch the harbour for a day.

## ev.pentam.entry.choice.look

Stay a day to learn the harbour

## ev.pentam.entry.choice.pass

Continue across the island town

## ev.pentam.entry.title

The Freshwater Jars at Pentam

## ev.ev_pentam_entry.choice_1_result

The girl finishes marking the new jar while her grandfather takes the empty one back to the well. She tells you which lane reaches the far side of town. You pass the boatyard and learn how the landing connects to the streets inland.

## ev.ev_pentam_entry.choice_2_result

You stay another day. The girl checks each receipt as the jars come down from the well; the sailor returns with his mate and collects the water at the promised time. Her grandfather introduces you to the pilot, who shows you where the deep water gives way to a shoal.

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.title

A Cracked Coconut Ladle

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.body

A boat worker is using half a coconut shell to empty rainwater from a small craft drawn up beside the landing. Water drips through a crack in the shell. His nephew has brought a fresh one, but it is still full of flesh. ‘We cannot scrape it by smashing it,’ the man says. ‘I need the rim whole.’ You ask why he does not fetch a wooden scoop. He points to the shallow place where the boat will go: a heavy scoop is awkward when he has to reach beneath the boards. The nephew sits down to hollow the new shell with a blunt knife. He hears a pilot call from the quay and asks whether the craft is leaving at once. ‘Once the tide covers that pale stone,’ his uncle answers. The boy checks the stone, still dry above the water, and continues scraping. The worker tells you the same pilot can describe the route toward Java Major and the safer way through these local shallows.

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.choice.route

Ask how to travel toward Java Major

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.choice.route_result

The worker calls over the pilot, who marks the route toward Java Major on your board. His nephew points to the pale stone at the quay and repeats when their own small craft can pass it. You leave with the outward route while they finish the new ladle.

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.choice.scene

Ask the pilot about the shoal

## ev.ev_gap_pentam.choice.scene_result

The pilot shows you how he reads the dark and pale patches of water beside the landing. He lets the nephew describe where the little craft must turn, then corrects one spot where the boy guessed too early. The nephew checks the stone again and smiles when the tide begins to cover it.

## ev.pentam.well.title

The Well Keeper’s Spare Jar

## ev.pentam.well.body

On a lane above the landing, the well keeper has found a crack in the jar reserved for an older neighbour. Her son wants to fill another jar at once, but all the spare ones have been promised to boats coming in that afternoon. ‘We can carry the water in two smaller pots,’ he says. His mother reminds him that the neighbour cannot lift them from the ground. The neighbour comes to the gate while they are still arguing and explains that her granddaughter will be home before dusk. ‘Put it on my low shelf,’ she says, ‘and she can pour it when she returns.’ The keeper asks whether the granddaughter knows the jar is cracked. The neighbour says she will leave a note beside it. You find a clean basket large enough to hold the two pots without tipping them. The son tests its handles and decides he can carry it alone, though his mother asks him to take the path without the broken stones. They invite you to walk with him now, or stay the next day to learn how the well supplies both the lane and the visiting crews.

## ev.pentam.well.choice.carry

Help carry water to the neighbour

## ev.pentam.well.result.carry

You steady the basket on the rough part of the lane while the son holds both handles. At the neighbour’s house, he sets the pots on the low shelf as she requested. She asks his mother to keep the cracked jar for seedlings, and the family thanks you on your way back.

## ev.pentam.well.choice.rest

Stay a day and learn the well’s work

## ev.pentam.well.result.rest

You spend another day beside the keeper as her son fills jars for the lane and the arriving boats. The neighbour’s granddaughter comes to return the two small pots and helps mark which boats still need water. The keeper tells you where the next well can be found, and her son asks you to read the marks back to him.
