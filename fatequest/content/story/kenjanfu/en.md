## city.kenjanfu.name

Kenjanfu

## ev.kenjanfu.entry.body

At a gate in Kenjanfu, a woman who keeps mulberry trees is carrying an empty basket with a split handle. Her son has brought a new strip to bind it. ‘The basket need not carry leaves today,’ he says, ‘so why mend it now?’ She shows him the old knot, which came loose when it was last full. ‘Because I would rather find the fault while it is empty.’ You hold the rim while he threads the new strip. A cloth merchant waiting to enter asks where the family stores their silk during damp weather. The keeper points to a raised room beyond the market and says the cloth stays there until a buyer checks it. You ask how to find the town’s main streets. Her son knows the market lane and offers to walk it with you tomorrow. A temple visitor at the gate says the shrine has drinking water for travellers today. The keeper tests the basket handle twice, then asks her son to carry it home before it is used again.

## ev.kenjanfu.a.title

The Mulberry Garden’s Repair Table

## ev.kenjanfu.a.body

A mulberry keeper has laid out bamboo trays beneath a roof. Some carry leaves when the trees are ready; today she is mending their loose rims. Her niece wants to stack them before the ties are dry. ‘If the edge bends, we will lose the shape,’ the keeper says. She asks the girl to compare a repaired tray with one that still has a gap. You help turn them over. A silk seller arrives from the market with a wrapped full load ready for inspection, and asks whether the keeper knows a dry place to show it. She clears a bench. You ask about the road toward Cachanfu. A carter who brings supplies to the garden knows it and is expected later. The niece asks how the garden’s work changes when there are no leaves to carry. ‘Look at what we have repaired today,’ the keeper says. ‘The records will show the rest if you stay another day.’ She opens an older page and lets her niece read the date. She asks the silk seller to wait until the bench is completely clean before opening his pack.

## ev.kenjanfu.b.title

The Workshop’s Lacquered Box

## ev.kenjanfu.b.body

In a Kenjanfu workshop, a lacquer worker has set a newly coated box under a roof to dry. His apprentice wants to fit its lid before the surface is ready. ‘Touch the spare board first,’ he says. She finds that the same coat still marks her fingertip. A silk weaver from the next room comes to ask whether the box can carry cloth samples on the road. The worker says the lid will fit when the lacquer has hardened, and brings out an older finished one to show her. You ask how the two workshops share the yard. The weaver says she uses the dry room for cloth and keeps clear of the lacquer table; the worker lends her clean boxes when buyers arrive. A carter at the gate mentions a road toward Saianfu and waits while they measure the sample cloth. The apprentice asks who manages the large compound beyond the workshops. ‘Ask its gatekeeper,’ the weaver says. ‘He sees who goes in and what each room is used for.’ The apprentice asks if she may go with you, and the weaver says she can after the samples are measured.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_entry.choice_3_result

You visit the temple by the gate and help a caretaker move water jars into the shade. She offers you a drink and shows you where travellers leave their shoes before entering. When the mulberry keeper passes, the caretaker thanks her son for bringing the repaired basket back.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_a.choice_1_result

You stay another day, helping the niece repair the last trays while the keeper explains when leaves are picked and how the empty trays are stored. The supply carter arrives and points out Cachanfu on your map. The keeper asks him to speak with you before he leaves.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_a.choice_2_result

The silk seller opens the dry wrapper for inspection. You pay for the full load, and the niece helps tie it again above the wet ground. A carter from the northern plain names Egrigaia while waiting for the keeper to finish a note.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_a.choice_3_result

The supply carters show how they bring materials to the garden and where the road bends beyond the town. One names the route he has taken across the plain; the niece draws the turn beside her notes on the repaired trays.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_b.choice_1_result

You spend two days between the lacquer table and the weaving room. The apprentice learns to test a spare board before touching a new box, and the weaver shows how she packs samples. The carter tells you where the road toward Saianfu begins before he takes his own load away.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_b.choice_2_result

You choose finished lacquerware, inspect its dry surface, and pay for the full load. The apprentice wraps each piece so it will not rub against the others. The worker calls you by name when the carter asks whose package is ready.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_b.choice_3_result

The gatekeeper tells you who works in the compound and where visitors may walk. He remembers the weaver’s father bringing samples there and points out the room they used. You leave with a clearer sense of the neighbourhood and its people.

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_a_followup.title

The Supply Carter’s Garden Note

## ev.ev_kenjanfu_a_followup.body

The supply carter has brought a list of trays and cloth wrappers to the mulberry garden. The keeper’s niece compares it with the repairs she finished. ‘We need fewer new trays than I thought,’ she says, showing the sound rims. The carter crosses out the extra line and asks what to do with the spare space in his cart. A silk seller nearby offers another wrapped full load for anyone ready to buy. You ask which route the carter takes after leaving Kenjanfu. He names the road toward Saianfu and the branch where another team goes toward Cachanfu. The keeper wants the niece to repeat both names before writing them on the garden board. The carter waits until she has marked them clearly. He can show you the silk seller’s load, give directions, or let you stay another day to watch the family prepare the trays for their next use.

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.title

The Cover That Was Too Short

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.body

A maker of oilcloth covers has finished one for a merchant’s cart, but it falls short of the back rail. His daughter measured the empty cart; now the merchant has added a tall chest. ‘The rain will reach its lid,’ she says. Her father asks the merchant whether the chest can be laid flat. It holds pottery packed upright, so it cannot. You help spread a spare strip of treated cloth beside the cover. The daughter measures the missing length and proposes a seam above the rail, where water will run away from the goods. Her father lets her mark it with charcoal before cutting. The merchant asks whether the stitch will hold when the cart turns. ‘We can test it here,’ she says, pointing to a bucket and the yard’s sloping path. She asks you to hold the cloth for the seam; her father offers to let you return tomorrow and watch the loaded cart tested in rainwater.

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.choice.sew

Help them add the missing strip

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.result.sew

You hold the treated cloth while the daughter stitches the extra strip above the cart rail. Her father pours a little water over the seam; it runs clear of the pottery chest. The merchant thanks you for helping them protect the load before he leaves.

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.choice.stay

Return a day later for the loaded test

## ev.kenjanfu.oilcloth.result.stay

You return the next day. The merchant has loaded the cart, and the daughter helps draw the cover across its taller chest. They pull the cart down the yard’s slope while her father pours water over the cloth. The seam stays dry beneath, and you see how a cover must fit the load that is actually carried.
