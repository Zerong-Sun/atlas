## ev.ev_unique_caiju.title

The Warehouse Tally Board

## ev.ev_unique_caiju.body

At a grain warehouse near Caiju’s waterway, a clerk has found two sacks with the same painted mark. The boat owner insists they came from different farms. His older son knows which sack he carried first, but the mark has smeared where rain touched it. ‘We can weigh them and ask the farm hands,’ the clerk says. The son asks whether that will delay the boat. The owner tells him to finish the count before promising a departure. You help turn the sacks so the seams can be checked. One is stitched with black thread, the other with pale hemp. The clerk writes the difference beside each entry and asks the owner to read the names aloud. A ferryman waiting at the door has a list of crossings toward Chinghianfu. He offers to copy his next departure and landing for a small fee while the warehouse family completes its count. The son wants to keep learning the tally work; he asks whether you will stay a day to help him compare the remaining sacks.

## ev.ev_unique_caiju.choice_a

Stay a day and check the grain tally

## ev.ev_unique_caiju.choice_a_result

You spend another day with the clerk and the son, turning sacks to check their stitching before each mark is read. By the final load the son can tell the two farms apart. The clerk thanks you in front of the waiting owners, and you understand how the warehouse keeps its accounts.

## ev.ev_unique_caiju.choice_b

Pay for the ferryman’s crossing list

## ev.ev_unique_caiju.choice_b_result

You pay the ferryman for a copy of his crossing list. He marks the ferry toward Chinghianfu and the landing on the far bank. The son brings the corrected grain list to the door as you leave.
