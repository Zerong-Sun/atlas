## city.tanpiju.name

Tanpiju

## ev.tanpiju.entry.body

At a garden gate in Tanpiju, grower Lin is trying to move a basket of mulberry leaves without dropping the tea parcels stacked beside it. His daughter says the parcels can wait on the bench. He asks who will keep the morning dew off them. She spreads a dry mat, moves the tea, and then helps lift the leaves. ‘You could have told me the mat was there,’ he says. She replies that he could have looked behind him.

You ask where the town market lies. Lin points along the street between gardens and houses. His aunt sells fans and lacquerware there and knows people travelling toward Kinsay. The daughter wants to see a little shrine by the lane first; a neighbour asked her to bring fresh flowers. Lin says she can go once the tea is secure.

A cart arrives for the mulberry leaves. The driver asks which basket belongs to him, and Lin’s daughter checks the name tied to its handle before anyone loads it.

‘Can I take the flowers now?’ the daughter asks. Lin checks the tea mat and says she may, if the neighbour is waiting.

## ev.tanpiju.a.title

The Tea Parcels among the Mulberries

## ev.tanpiju.a.body

Lin’s aunt has come to the garden with a ledger of tea orders. One parcel is marked for Kinsay, another for a buyer whose family has moved toward Fuju. Lin’s daughter asks whether the old address should remain on the tag. Her aunt says it should stay in the ledger for reference, while the new address goes on the parcel. The girl copies both and asks you to check which one the cart driver needs.

You ask how the tea is packed for a longer journey. Lin shows you the dry mat used earlier and a full tea trade bale priced at 108 silver. A neighbour who tends mulberry trees says she knows the road toward Chinginju and offers to compare her route notes with the aunt’s. The aunt asks her to wait until the Kinsay parcel is tied, so nobody mixes the two tags.

The daughter puts down her brush and reads the new address aloud. Lin checks the basket handle before the driver lifts it.

‘Which address goes on the cart?’ the driver asks. The girl shows him the new tag; her aunt keeps the old one in the ledger.

## ev.tanpiju.b.title

The Fan Maker and the Lacquer Box

## ev.tanpiju.b.body

At the market, Lin’s aunt is setting painted fans beside a lacquer box made by her friend Mei. A family has asked Mei to make a box for letters left by someone they miss. Her apprentice wants to paint a bright flower on the lid. Mei asks whether the family asked for it; the apprentice says they asked only for a place to keep the letters dry. Lin’s aunt suggests a plain lid and offers to show them a small pattern inside.

You ask how long the fan trade has been here. Lin’s aunt says she learned from neighbours who carry their work toward Kinsay. A full fan trade bundle is priced at 135 silver and takes a day to inspect and pack. Mei has a full lacquerware trade bundle for 108 silver. The family waiting for the letter box comes in; they want to speak about the memorial before any design is chosen.

The apprentice puts down the paintbrush. Mei sets the undecorated lid between them and waits for their wishes.

## ev.ev_tanpiju_a_followup.title

The Corrected Tea Address

## ev.ev_tanpiju_a_followup.body

Lin’s daughter has received a reply from the family that moved. They confirm the new address and thank her for keeping the old one in the ledger. She asks whether she should erase the first address now. Lin’s aunt says the old entry explains how the change was made; the parcel itself has only the new address. The cart driver reads the tag aloud and is satisfied.

You ask about the next tea load. A second full trade bale costs 108 silver. The aunt’s route notes include Fuju and Chinginju, while Lin wants to spend a day with you checking how the garden paths meet the road. His daughter asks the driver whether the town name on her tag is clear from where he stands. He steps closer and reads it without help.

Lin takes the dry mat from the bench and asks his daughter to bring the next bale into the shade.

‘You heard it from there?’ the driver asks. She nods and shows him the dated reply before he takes the parcel.

Mei asks her to choose from two lighter pigments and does not hurry her. Lin’s aunt brings a fan to keep dust away while the paint dries.

Mei asks whether you will help him hold the light.

The daughter chooses the warmer pigment. The apprentice tests it on a spare piece before touching the real box.

## ev.tanpiju.mulberrybasket.title

The Mulberry Basket Handle

## ev.tanpiju.mulberrybasket.body

At Lin’s garden, the handle of a mulberry basket has begun to split. His daughter wants to wrap it with the same blue cord she used on the tea parcel. Lin says the cord is too smooth for a heavy basket. She asks what would hold better, and he shows her a rougher strip of fibre kept beside the gate. A neighbour arriving for leaves says the handle broke on her last visit too; she had thought it was her own fault for lifting from one side.

You ask how many baskets must go out today. Lin checks the names tied to the handles and finds three for the neighbour, one for a household down the lane. His daughter notices that the split basket is the one for the farthest delivery. The neighbour offers to take a sound basket instead and come back for the repaired one. Lin agrees, but the child wants to learn how to bind it properly first. He gives her the rough strip and asks you to hold the handle steady while she starts.

‘Start on the sound side,’ Lin tells his daughter. She turns the basket and asks you to keep that edge level.

## ev.tanpiju.mulberrybasket.result.bind

You hold the handle while Lin’s daughter binds it with the rough strip. The neighbour lifts it with both hands to test the repair. It holds, and she takes the sound basket first as planned.
