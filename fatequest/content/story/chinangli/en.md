## city.chinangli.name

Chinangli

## ev.chinangli.entry.body

At Chinangli’s riverside market, a merchant has hung his ledger beneath the awning while porters unload cloth. His son carries a small bowl of sand to dry the fresh ink. A gust shakes the awning, and the boy catches the ledger before it falls into the wet cargo. ‘You can hold it by the string, not by the page,’ his father says. A porter arrives with spice jars and asks where to leave them. The merchant checks the name on the jars against his ledger, then sends them to another shop across the street. You ask which way to leave town toward Cambaluc. The porter has come from that road and names the gate he used. The boy wants to know why the merchant keeps both river cargo and road carts in one book. ‘Because the same buyer may come by either,’ his father says, showing him two separate columns. A temple keeper passing the market asks for a cloth order; she will be at the shrine later if you wish to visit. The boy offers to walk you through the stalls tomorrow after he has finished the day’s count.

## ev.chinangli.entry.title

The River Merchant’s Dry Ledger

## ev.chinangli.a.title

The Two Loads at the Quay

## ev.chinangli.a.body

A broker at the quay has two loads of silk, one newly unloaded from a boat and one brought in by cart. His apprentice has written the river seller’s name beside both. ‘The cart owner will come for his receipt,’ the broker says. The apprentice runs to the cart and checks its painted mark. ‘The river seller’s mark is round. This one has a corner,’ she says when she returns. ‘Then give the cart owner his own line,’ the broker replies. She corrects the entry. You ask why the silk prices differ. The broker opens a wrapper on each load and lets you feel the weave; one is heavier and has been kept dry for longer. A spice trader waiting nearby asks about carts travelling north toward Cambaluc. The broker knows a carter who recently came from there and asks the apprentice to fetch him. The apprentice wants to count the quay loads for a day so she can stop mixing them with road deliveries. The broker says she can, if every name is checked against its own mark. The spice trader offers to tell you which roads bring his jars toward Chandu and Cacanfu while they wait.

## ev.chinangli.a.choice.quay

Spend a day checking the quay loads

## ev.chinangli.a.choice.silk

## ev.chinangli.a.choice.spice

## ev.chinangli.b.title

A Paper Note at the Counting Stall

## ev.chinangli.b.body

A shop clerk has brought a paper payment note to the counting stall. Its corner is worn, but the issuing merchant’s mark is still clear. The clerk’s daughter wants to know whether the shop must accept it. ‘Only if we can check the mark and the name,’ he says. The merchant who issued it works two streets away, so they send a runner to confirm it. You ask how a traveller knows which paper a shop will take. The clerk shows you the merchant’s name written beside the stamp and the date it was issued. He makes clear that not every stall accepts every paper promise. A porcelain seller waiting next door has a packed full load ready for anyone who wants to buy it with agreed payment. The runner returns with the issuing merchant’s confirmation. ‘The amount is still owed,’ he says. The daughter checks the note once more. ‘Then I can record it under his name,’ she tells her father, and carefully copies it into the register. The clerk asks whether you will stay a day to see the process, inspect the porcelain, or ask him more about how he checks old notes.

## ev.chinangli.b.choice.exchange

Stay a day to see how notes are checked

## ev.chinangli.b.choice.porcelain

## ev.chinangli.b.choice.clerks

## ev.ev_chinangli_entry.choice_1_result

The boy hangs the ledger higher, away from the damp cargo, and the porter points you toward the land gate used for Cambaluc. You cross town while the merchant finishes counting cloth and spice jars.

## ev.ev_chinangli_entry.choice_2_result

You spend another day among the stalls. The merchant’s son shows you which goods came by river and which by cart, then introduces the people who keep the market accounts. He finds the right column for the spice jars without his father’s help.

## ev.ev_chinangli_entry.choice_3_result

At the shrine, the keeper is setting out water for visitors. You help her clear the entrance before offering a greeting. She shows you where the families meet and gives you a small mark to remember the visit. The merchant’s cloth order arrives while you are there.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a.choice_1_result

You stay another day while the apprentice checks each load’s mark before writing its owner’s name. The carter from Cambaluc arrives and describes that road to you. By evening she no longer confuses the cart silk with the river silk, and the broker invites you to look at his route book.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a.choice_2_result

You inspect the silk and pay for the full load. The apprentice ties your name to the correct wrapper. The cart owner who delivered a nearby load mentions the road toward Cacanfu before the broker asks you to wait for his route book.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a.choice_3_result

The spice trader shows which jars came from carts on the northern and southern roads, then names Chandu from a journey he made with his brother. The apprentice copies the place into her own list and asks him to check its spelling.

## ev.ev_chinangli_b.choice_1_result

You spend another day with the clerk as he checks paper notes against the issuing merchants’ marks and names. His daughter shows you an old sample that is no longer used, then files it beside the register. You leave knowing how this stall decides what it can accept.

## ev.ev_chinangli_b.choice_2_result

You inspect the packed porcelain and pay for the full load in the form accepted by the seller. He checks the payment with the clerk, ties the wrapping securely, and introduces you to the nearby merchants who will help carry it.

## ev.ev_chinangli_b.choice_3_result

The clerk holds a worn note beside a fresh impression of the issuing merchant’s mark. His daughter points out the name and date that must match their register. You ask two further questions, and both answer from the notes they have actually handled.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.title

The Broker’s Two Route Columns

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.body

The broker lays his route book beside the corrected cargo list. One column records boats that bring goods to the quay; the other records carts that carry them onward. His apprentice points to the silk you inspected. ‘It belongs on this line, with the cart mark,’ she says. The broker agrees. ‘Show our guest how you found it,’ he says. She turns the book so you can see the mark beside the carter’s name. ‘I checked the paint on the cart first,’ she tells you. A silk seller arrives with a second load ready for purchase and waits while she closes the book. You ask about the road toward Cambaluc. The carter who came from there draws his last stopping place; the broker adds where the road branches toward Cacanfu. A spice trader at the door says another carter will leave for Chandu after the next day’s count. The apprentice wants to help with that count herself, and asks you to stay if you have time. The broker gives her the chalk board, but keeps the ledger open so she can check her figures.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_1

Buy the second full load of silk

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_1_result

The seller opens a corner of the second load for inspection. You pay the full cargo price, and the apprentice writes your name beside the right cart mark. The broker shows you the branch toward Cacanfu while the wrapper is tied shut.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_2

Ask the carter about Cambaluc

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_2_result

The carter marks Cambaluc on your map and tells you which land gate he used on his last trip. The apprentice compares his stops with the route book and corrects a missing name. You leave with a better account of how goods reach the town.

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_3

Stay a day to help count the spice chests

## ev.ev_chinangli_a_followup.choice_3_result

You spend another day checking spice chests with the apprentice. She puts the river arrivals and road carts in separate columns, then balances the final count with the broker. A carter leaving for Chandu names that city to you while he collects his load.

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.title

A Knot for the Right Customer

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.body

A porter outside the riverside market has lost the coloured cord from a customer’s parcel. His apprentice wants to tie on any spare strip, but the porter stops him. ‘That colour names the buyer,’ he says. They have two similar parcels, and only one is due for the cloth shop today. You help open the outside wrapping just enough to see the shop’s own seal beneath it. The apprentice finds the matching name on the delivery board. A girl from the cloth shop arrives and says her mother is waiting to finish an order. She can recognize the seal, but asks them to put the proper cord back so the next porter will know it too. The older porter makes the apprentice choose from the spare strips. ‘And the other parcel?’ he asks. The apprentice reads its mark and names the street. ‘Good. Leave its cord alone,’ the porter says. You can help carry the corrected one now; if you come back tomorrow, the porter will teach you the marks his crews use for different streets.

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.choice.check

Help deliver the corrected parcel

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.result.check

You carry the parcel with the apprentice to the cloth shop. The girl’s mother checks her seal and thanks both of you for bringing the right order. The porter hears of the delivery when you return and adds your name beside the apprentice’s on the board.

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.choice.stay

Return a day later to learn the cord marks

## ev.chinangli.rope-mark.result.stay

You return the next day. The porter lays out the coloured cords and has his apprentice sort fresh parcels by street while you read the delivery board. By noon the apprentice can explain every mark himself. You leave able to see how a small knot helps keep the accounts straight.
