## city.yarcan.name

Yarcan

## ev.yarcan.entry.body

At a Yarcan cloth stall, a merchant named Nasir is tying up a bolt his sister has finished weaving. She arrives with a narrower strip and asks him to add it to the parcel. ‘That is for our aunt,’ he says. ‘If I sell it with the rest, she will come looking for her border.’ His sister unrolls the strip to show the little flower their aunt asked for.

You ask whether travellers often buy from this family. Nasir says they trade with caravans and neighbours alike. Across the lane, a cobbler who grew up with them is repairing Nasir’s boots. He asks the sister to bring the aunt’s strip closer so he can compare its blue thread with the cord he has for the boot. She laughs that he has found yet another reason to leave his workbench.

Nasir can point you toward the wells used by departing caravans. His sister invites you to stay a day if you want to see how cloth, leather and river stone pass from one household to the next. The cobbler holds up the repaired boot and asks whose foot it belongs to.

Stay a day with Nasir’s family and neighbours

## ev.yarcan.entry.title

The Cloth Stalls at Yarcan

## ev.ev_yarcan_entry.choice_1_result

Nasir names the wells used on the road out of Yarcan. The cobbler returns his boot before you leave, and you mark the town on your map.

## ev.ev_yarcan_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day following Nasir’s sister through the cloth stalls and the cobbler’s lane. Her aunt comes for the flowered border at dusk and thanks everyone who kept it aside. You record what you saw of Yarcan.

## ev.yarcan.site_gap.title

The Orchard Keeper’s Grafting Knife

## ev.yarcan.site_gap.body

In an orchard, keeper Farida has wrapped cuttings in damp cloth. Her son wants to graft them before the afternoon heat rises. She says the tree is ready, but first he must ask whose branch he has brought. It came from their neighbour’s old tree, whose fruit the two families have shared since the children were small. The neighbour arrives with a basket and asks whether one cutting will be enough.

You ask how the new branch will take. Farida shows the sloping cut and where the bark layers should meet. Her son holds the tree steady; the neighbour promises to water it while Farida is away at market. Farida says she will leave the binding loose enough for a new bud to grow. The son asks if the fruit will taste exactly like the old tree’s. ‘We will have to wait and see,’ his mother says.

The neighbour has travelled toward Cascar recently and can name the stopping places. Farida sets another cutting on the damp cloth for you to watch her fit before the sun reaches the row.

## ev.yarcan.site_gap.choice.route

Ask the neighbour about Cascar

## ev.ev_yarcan_site_gap.choice_1_result

The neighbour names the stopping places toward Cascar and points out where the next water lies. Farida’s son helps you mark the route, then runs back to hold the young tree.

## ev.yarcan.site_gap.choice.interact

Watch Farida fit the new cutting

## ev.ev_yarcan_site_gap.choice_2_result

Farida lines up the bark and asks you to hold the cloth while her son ties it. The neighbour fills the watering jug. You leave knowing what the family will check when the bud begins to grow.

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.title

The Broken Reed in the Loom

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.body

In a weaving room, Dilara has stopped her loom with half a border unfinished. A narrow reed has split, and the blue thread keeps slipping out of line. Her little sister has gone to fetch a replacement but returns with a reed for a wider loom. ‘It fits the frame,’ the girl says. Dilara shows her why the teeth leave too much space for this pattern.

You ask when the cloth must be ready. Dilara says their aunt wants it for a family gathering tomorrow, after a cousin returns from the caravan road. She could borrow the right reed from a neighbour, though she would rather replace her own first. Her sister says she can walk over with a message and explain the delay; the aunt will understand if she knows what happened. Dilara asks the girl to read the note aloud before taking it.

The proper reed costs 1 silver at the next yard. Dilara has laid out the threads in order so she can resume as soon as it arrives. Her sister holds the note and waits for a decision.

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.choice.buy

Pay 1 silver for the proper loom reed

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.result.buy

You bring the proper reed to Dilara. She fits it while her sister holds the threads, then weaves the blue border straight. The aunt arrives before dusk and thanks you for helping them finish in time.

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.choice.letter

Walk with the sister and explain the delay

## ev.yarcan.loomreed.result.letter

You walk with the girl to her aunt’s house. The aunt reads Dilara’s note and offers the right reed from her own storeroom. The sisters finish the border together; you remember how quickly the family found a way through the trouble.
