## city.dufar.name

Dufar

## ev.dufar.entry.body

In Dufar, a resin collector has brought a basket of frankincense down from the groves. His daughter is sorting the pale pieces from those still carrying bark. “This one came loose before the cut had healed,” she says, holding one up. Her father looks at it and agrees. A trader waiting beside them asks whether the better grade will be ready for his coastal boat. “After she finishes,” the collector answers. The girl asks him why he promises the cargo before they have counted it. “Because I know you count more carefully than I do,” he says. You ask which road leads through the town. The trader points between the storehouses and offers to introduce you to the resin sellers if you stay. At the far end of the street, horses are being led toward another vessel. The collector tells his daughter to keep the incense away from the wet harnesses while they finish the basket.

## ev.dufar.entry.title

The Frankincense Basket

## ev.ev_dufar_entry.choice_1_result

The trader points you through the lane between the storehouses. The daughter is still checking the last pale pieces when you pass. Her father raises a hand in farewell, and you leave knowing the way through Dufar.

## ev.ev_dufar_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day in the incense market. The daughter shows you why some pieces are kept for later sorting, while the trader explains which cargo goes by boat and which by road. The collector finishes his count before evening, and you leave with the town's trades and streets in mind.

## ev.dufar.site_gap.title

The Incense Seller’s Wrapping

## ev.dufar.site_gap.body

An incense seller is wrapping resin in thick paper while his nephew tests the seals on two clay jars. “This one smells of dried fish,” the boy says, pushing a jar away. His uncle laughs. “Then it can carry water, not incense.” He gives you a pale piece to smell and asks what you notice. The scent comes through before you crush it. He shows the boy how to keep the paper dry and how to tie the bundle without breaking the resin. A customer asks whether the shop will send a parcel toward Esher. “If the carrier still has room,” the seller answers, “but tell her which door to find before she leaves.” The nephew offers to walk to the corner with you and point out that road. His uncle calls him back for a moment: the last jar still needs a clean lining, and the boy is the only one with small enough hands to set it neatly. The customer says she will bring the receiving name tomorrow, when she knows which cousin is at home.

## ev.dufar.site_gap.choice.route

Ask about the road to Esher

## ev.ev_dufar_site_gap.choice_1_result

The nephew leads you to the corner used by carriers travelling toward Esher. He points out the first turning and says which shop they use to ask about water farther on. You follow his directions on the ground before he returns to the jars.

## ev.dufar.site_gap.choice.interact

Help line the clean incense jar

## ev.ev_dufar_site_gap.choice_2_result

You hold the jar while the boy fits clean paper inside. His uncle tests the lid and shows you how he separates good resin from bits still carrying bark. The boy tries the next jar himself, pleased that this one smells only of clay.

## ev.dufar.sail.title

A Sail to Bring Home

## ev.dufar.sail.body

A sailmaker near Dufar's shore is patching a long tear in a small boat's sail. Her brother brought it in before dawn and promised to collect it after he helped unload another vessel. “He said that yesterday too,” her son tells you. She smiles, draws the torn edges together and asks him to bring more thread. The boy has wound it around the wrong spindle, so he sits down to sort the tangles. You ask whether the sail can still catch wind after the repair. “It will if the patch lies flat,” she says, showing where the old cloth has stretched. Her brother arrives carrying a basket of dates for their lunch. He sees the work is nearly done and offers to pay someone to help finish the seam before the tide changes. The son wants to carry the sail to the boat himself. His mother tells him to finish untangling the thread first; then they can go together. The boy asks whether they can eat the dates after the sail is hung; his uncle says he will save the best for him.

## ev.dufar.sail.choice.sew

Help finish the seam for a fee

## ev.dufar.sail.result.sew

You hold the cloth flat while the sailmaker and her son finish the seam. Her brother tests the patch against the light and pays you the share he promised. The boy carries the folded sail to the boat with his mother and returns the spindle before he leaves.

## ev.dufar.sail.choice.tide

Ask her brother about the tide and coastal route

## ev.dufar.sail.result.tide

Her brother draws the route toward Aden on a board and shows where his boat waits for the tide. The boy asks why he never points out that turn when they sail together. “You were busy watching the birds,” he says. You leave with the coastal passage clearer in mind.
