## city.balc.name

Balc

## ev.balc.a.body

Balc was once the mother of cities — the capital of Bactria, where Alexander the Great
took Roxana of Sogdiana as his bride. Its walls enclosed more ground than any city in
Asia, and its temples held treasure from India, Persia, and Greece. Now its broken
ramparts, collapsed domes, and empty palaces tell the same story as every city east of
the Oxus: the Mongol rider came, and nothing was left standing. Yet the caravans still
stop here, and the city is slowly rebuilding from the rubble. The new houses are
smaller, built from the stones of the old, but the market is alive again. The King of
Balc pays tribute to the Great Kaan, and the trade of lapis lazuli, rubies, and Turkoman
horses keeps the city's heart beating.

## ev.balc.b.body

The caravanserai of Balc is one of the largest on the Silk Road — a fortress-sized
courtyard ringed with stables for a thousand camels, storerooms stacked with bales of
silk and leather sacks of lapis, and chambers for merchants of every nation. Lapis
lazuli from the mines of Badakhshan, which lies twelve days east, is the city's chief
treasure; it is sold in rough blocks and traded for silver, silk, and spices. Balas
rubies from the mountains fetch prices that would buy a ship in Venice. Horses from the
Turkoman plains are auctioned in the inner courtyard. The caravanserai never sleeps;
fires burn in the arcades all night, and the smell of lamb kebab and saffron rice hangs
in the air.

## ev.balc.c.body

On a hill outside the city, a fire-temple of the ancient Persian faith still burns — the
Magi tend a flame they say has never been extinguished since the days of Zoroaster, a
thousand years before the Prophet. The people call it the Tomb of the Magi, and they say
that the three wise men who visited the infant Christ are buried here, in three stone
sarcophagi that still bear traces of gold leaf. Pilgrims from Persia, India, and even
distant Cathay climb the hill to leave offerings of incense and silver coins. The Magi
in their white robes chant the ancient hymns at dawn and dusk. They say that a man who
sleeps at the shrine will dream of his own death — and that every dream they have
recorded has come true.

## ev.balc.entry.body

Balc is a noble city and a great, though it was much greater in former days. But the
Tartars and other nations have greatly ravaged and destroyed it. There were formerly
many fine palaces and buildings of marble, and the ruins of them still remain. The
people of the city tell that it was here that Alexander took to wife the daughter of
Darius. Here, you should be told, is the end of the empire of the Tartar Lord of the
Levant. And this city is also the limit of Persia in the direction between east and
north-east. Now, let us quit this city, and I will tell you of another country called
DOGANA. When you have quitted the city of which I have been speaking, you ride some 12
days between north-east and east, without finding any human habitation, for the people
have all taken refuge in fastnesses among the mountains, on account of the Banditti and
armies that harassed them. There is plenty of water on the road, and abundance of game;
there are lions too. You can get no provisions on the road, and must carry with you all
that you require for these 12 days.

## ev.ev_balc_a_followup.title

Balc: The Guardian of Bactra

## ev.ev_balc_a_followup.body

As you walk among the ruined palaces, an old man who keeps the ground wakes from his rest and rises to meet you. He has watched every digger and every traveller who came to pick at the bones of the mother of cities. He does not forbid you; he only asks that you listen first. The broken stones of Balc, he says, remember more than the Mongols left standing. If you mean to carry a fragment of Bactra away, carry also some knowledge of what it was.

## ev.ev_balc_a_followup.choice_1_result

The guardian lifts a carved stone from a niche no raider has reached, and presses it into your hand. Bactria's last unbroken piece now travels with you, and the old man's account of the city goes with it.

## ev.ev_balc_b_followup.body

The caravan masters of Balc do not scatter their knowledge like grain. When one of them finally settles on a bench beside the arcade and lays out his ledger, the page is covered in names: toll-houses, wells, pastures, and the days between each. He reads them aloud for you, then turns the book so you can see the road drawn in the margin. What you do with the page is up to you.
