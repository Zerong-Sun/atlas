## city.tripolis.name

Tripolis

## ev.tripolis.entry.title

The Sugar Crate at the Harbour

## ev.tripolis.entry.body

After reaching Tripolis, you follow a cart carrying goods for a ship down to the harbour. It stops beside the quay. The bottom of a wooden crate has split, leaving sugar stuck to a wheel. Hassan and another porter lift it onto an old piece of sailcloth.

"My sister packed this one," Hassan says. "She draws a little moon on the side. When I find it, I'll know whose account it goes on."

His sister is writing on the next batch of crates under a shed. She runs over, sees the crack, and says she will fetch a carpenter. Hassan starts to lift the crate again. "Gather the corners of the cloth first," she tells him. "We can still get the sugar back in."

A ship is waiting for its cargo. The coastal road leads south; people here also travel north to Antioch. For now the brother and sister are trying to get this one crate aboard.

## ev.tripolis.entry.choice.pass

Ask them for the southern road and keep travelling

## ev.tripolis.entry.choice.rest

Help gather the sugar and rest in town for a day

## ev.ev_tripolis_entry.choice_1_result

Hassan wipes his sticky hand on the sailcloth and points towards the road along the coast. "Leave by that gate. You can reach the next station before dark." His sister adds, "There is a well with a whitewashed rim. Fill your water there."

You make a note of the gate and pass through the carts. Hassan has gone back to folding the cloth.

## ev.ev_tripolis_entry.choice_2_result

You hold up the cloth while Hassan gathers the spilled sugar in its centre. A carpenter replaces the bottom board, then the brother and sister put the sugar back. His sister draws her little moon on the crate again. She watches until the sailors set it safely aboard.

The two give you space to rest in the shed. In the morning Hassan says the southbound caravan has just left through the sea gate. After a night's rest, you feel stronger.

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.title

Olive Jars in the Courtyard

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.body

Samia packs olives into clay jars in a courtyard behind the harbour. Her ten-year-old son passes her washed stones to weigh down the fruit. A piece of red thread is tied around one jar.

"That one goes on a ship to my uncle," the boy says. "Last year he left a whole jar there and came home saying ship rats ate it."

Samia pours in brine and laughs. "This year we'll write his name on it. If the rats can read, I'll let them have it." The boy reaches for a piece of charcoal, then asks you whether a ship or the road through the mountains is faster.

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.choice.talk

Help the boy write the name and hear about his uncle

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.choice.route

Ask where travellers leave for Antioch

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.result.talk

The boy writes so large that his uncle's name wraps halfway around the jar. Samia says he will recognise it. Her brother works as a cook aboard ship. On his first voyage he took a full jar, worried he might feel sick, then shared it all with the crew before landfall. The boy laughs and adds one more stone under the lid.

Samia gives you an olive preserved last year. The boy takes one too and drinks water after tasting the salt.

## ev.gap.tripolis.scene.result.route

Samia points beyond the courtyard. "Leave by the north gate for Antioch. Snow can close the mountain road in cold weather; go with a caravan that knows it."

The boy says he will tell his uncle the route next time the ship docks, so his uncle can come home by land for once. Samia tightens the red thread. "First let him bring this jar back."

## ev.tripolis.quay.title

The Blue Rope on the Boats

## ev.tripolis.quay.body

At the quay, a young woman looks from one boat to another. She holds a short length of blue rope tied with three knots. Her father ties its match to the family boat's oar so that she can recognise it when he comes in.

"The boat is here, but the oar isn't," she says. "Father says he lent it to the next boat. They say he didn't. I need to find it before they leave."

A porter overhears her. "Your father went to buy a new oar this morning. Ask him before you search every boat in the harbour." She blushes but still wants to know what happened.

## ev.tripolis.quay.choice.help

Help her ask about the oar on both boats

## ev.tripolis.quay.choice.road

Ask the porter for the coastal road to Tyre

## ev.tripolis.quay.result.help

The other boat's master takes you to the stern. The old oar is there, with its blue rope still tied to the handle. He says he borrowed it that morning while her father went into town to buy a new one. He had promised to return it by noon. She remembers that her father spoke in a hurry that morning; she caught only the words "the oar is next door."

They carry the old oar back together. The master grins. "Next time, tell your father to write it down. It'll save you a walk round the harbour." She offers you some water and remembers your help.

## ev.tripolis.quay.result.road

The porter takes you to the end of the quay and points south along the coast. "For Tyre, follow those mule carts. They leave through the south gate this afternoon."

The young woman comes over. "Father sails to Tyre in a few days. If you meet him, tell him I found the old oar. Next time he lends something, he can tell me the whole story." The porter laughs, and so does she.

## ev.tripolis.oven.title

Bread for the Ship

## ev.tripolis.oven.body

The communal oven is hot. People wait with dough on wooden boards as the baker loads one batch after another. An old man arrives last with three rounds covered in sesame seeds. "For my grandson's ship," he says.

The baker asks when it sails. The man only knows that his grandson asked him to come before noon. Two women ahead of him move their boards aside to make room.

The man thanks them, scattering sesame seeds from his sleeve. His grandson is sailing to Alexandria for the first time. Before leaving he complained that the bread was too hard; now he has asked for three rounds and seems to be in a hurry for them.

## ev.tripolis.oven.choice.bake

Help carry the bread to the harbour

## ev.tripolis.oven.choice.damascus

Wait for this batch and ask the baker about the road to Damascus

## ev.tripolis.oven.result.bake

The bread is hot when it comes out. You and the old man carry the board between you to the quay. His grandson sees him from the ship and runs down the gangplank. "Share them on the way," the old man says. "Don't eat them all at once." The young man takes a bite and says this batch is just right.

Back in town, the baker is making room for the next batch. The old man collects his board and says he will come tomorrow to keep an eye on his neighbour's dough.

## ev.tripolis.oven.result.damascus

As he loads the oven, the baker tells you that caravans for Damascus gather east of town. Before leaving, their drivers often bake thin loaves to carry for several days. He points to the long boards against the wall; they are used to slide the dough into the oven.

The old man hears where you are headed and offers you a piece of fresh sesame bread. You remember where the caravan gathers.
