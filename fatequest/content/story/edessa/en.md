## city.edessa.name

Edessa

## ev.edessa.entry.choice.pass

Thank Miran and continue through Edessa

## ev.edessa.entry.choice.rest

Stay with Miran's family for a day

## ev.edessa.entry.body

The road into Edessa passes a water trough. Miran is filling a leather bag from a jar, while his niece Derya sits nearby with a pile of flat stones. She has arranged them into a winding line and says it is the road her uncle took when he went away to buy wool.

“That road had more turns than this,” Miran says. Derya adds two stones. He went away long enough for her to outgrow the shoes she was wearing when he left, and she has made him tell the story of his return three times.

Miran asks where you are headed and shifts his bag so you can fill yours. Derya wants to know if your road has a river. When you show her the map, she finds Edessa before Miran does. He tells you where travellers stop for water beyond the town; she puts another stone beside the trough to mark the place where you met.

## ev.edessa.entry.title

Arriving at Edessa

## ev.ev_edessa_entry.choice_1_result

You fill your bag, thank Miran, and move on. Derya calls after you that a stone is still waiting for the place you visit next. You mark Edessa and the water stop on your map.

## ev.ev_edessa_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day with Miran's family. Derya takes you along the water street and introduces you to everyone who knows the story of her uncle's return. In the evening the family shares a meal and gives you a place to sleep. You leave the next morning with renewed strength, and mark Edessa and its water stop on your map.

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.title

The Wool Comb in Narin's Courtyard

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.body

In a courtyard, Narin combs a bundle of wool while her sister Saba winds the finished strands. They are making a blanket for their mother, who complains that the old one lets the night air through. Saba says their mother also said the last blanket was too heavy. Narin holds up a handful of loose wool. “Then this one has to be just right.”

A boy from the next house has brought back the comb he borrowed. He asks whether the blanket is for their mother's bed or for the road. Saba once carried wool west toward Berrhoea, and he hopes she will tell that story again. Narin offers you a small tuft to try while Saba takes the boy's questions.

You ask whether their mother has seen the wool yet. ‘Only the bundle before we washed it,’ Saba says. Narin wants to keep the finished blanket hidden until the evening meal. The neighbour’s boy asks if their mother will notice it is lighter than the old one. ‘She notices every thread we leave hanging,’ Narin answers, pulling one loose fibre away. Saba puts the finished roll beside the loom and asks the boy to hold the door open while she brings in the next bundle. He does, then returns to the question he really came to ask about the road.

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.choice.talk

Try the comb and hear about the blanket

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.choice.route

Listen to Saba's journey toward Berrhoea

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.result.talk

Narin shows you how to draw the comb through a little wool without pulling it apart. Her mother calls from the next room to ask whether the blanket is ready. “We have a traveller helping now,” Narin calls back. The mother asks you to make it light. You remember the courtyard and add this part of Edessa to your map.

## ev.gap.edessa.scene.result.route

Saba tells the boy how she travelled west with a bundle of wool, stopping where other travellers could find water and shelter. You ask for the names, and she repeats them while Narin goes on combing. The boy arranges pebbles on the floor for each stop. You add Edessa, Berrhoea, and the route between them to your map.

## ev.edessa.clayletter.title

A Letter at the Potter's Yard

## ev.edessa.clayletter.body

At a potter's yard, Safa holds a letter flat against a board with a lump of clay. Her father sent it from Tarsus, where he took a load of bowls to sell. A corner got wet on the journey home. She can still read the part where he asks if her little brother has stopped hiding his shoes, but the directions for his return are fading.

Her brother says he hid the shoes because their father always set them beside the door as though the boy were about to leave too. Safa reads that line aloud again, and both of them laugh. Their father promises to bring back a pair that fits.

Several bowls must also be moved into the shade before they dry too quickly. Safa has put off that work to save the letter. She asks you to help copy the remaining directions, or stay and move the bowls while she writes. She has money set aside for a day's help.

‘Does Father know you hide my shoes?’ Safa asks her brother. ‘He found them last time,’ he says, ‘and put them back by the door.’ She smooths the damp corner of the letter without rubbing the ink. You ask whether another carrier might remember the road their father named. Safa says the pottery buyer knows the same route and will come for the bowls before sunset. Her brother takes the wooden board at the dry end so she can lift the letter carefully. ‘Ask him about the shoes too,’ he says. Safa laughs, then notices the shade moving across the clay pieces and calls him to help shift them.

## ev.edessa.clayletter.choice.copy

Copy the surviving route in the letter

## ev.edessa.clayletter.choice.work

Stay a day and move the bowls into shade

## ev.edessa.clayletter.result.copy

You hold the board steady while Safa copies the legible names and turns onto a clean sheet. Her brother reads them back, slowly enough for her to catch a missed stop. She adds a line of her own, asking their father to bring shoes with room to grow. You copy the road toward Tarsus into your map as she speaks, and mark the city at its far end.

## ev.edessa.clayletter.result.work

You spend the day carrying unfired bowls beneath the awning. Safa finishes copying the letter while her brother counts the bowls, losing his place whenever he finds a pair that matches. When the light fades, she pays you the agreed wage. Her brother shows you where he has hidden his shoes this time; he intends to leave them there until their father comes home. You leave the next morning.
