## city.paukin.name

Paukin

## ev.paukin.entry.body

At the edge of Paukin, a cloth merchant is trying to steer her cart past a puddle on the raised road. Her apprentice has covered the bundles with a sheet but left the corner dragging. ‘You wanted me to keep the rain off,’ he says. She asks him what will happen when the wheel runs over the sheet. He catches it up and tucks it under the rope.

You ask where the town begins. The merchant points toward the houses beyond the causeway and says the fields on either side make the road narrow after heavy rain. Her sister sells thread in the market and can tell you which stalls stay open when the weather turns. The apprentice asks whether there is time to stop for lunch. ‘After we put the cart on dry ground,’ she says, handing him the lead rope.

An older farmer passes with an empty grain rake and offers to show you the drying yard. The merchant takes the offer of help with the cart but tells him to keep the rake out of the wheel spokes.

‘Will the road be clear by supper?’ the apprentice asks. The merchant looks at the wheels and answers, ‘If you keep that sheet tied up, yes.’ He checks the knot again.

The Cloth Cart by the Causeway

## ev.ev_paukin_entry.choice_1_result

You follow the dry side of the causeway into Paukin. The merchant and apprentice bring the cloth cart through behind you; the farmer lifts his rake clear of the wheels.

## ev.ev_paukin_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day seeing the thread market and the grain yard. The merchant’s sister shows you how she covers her stock when rain begins, and the farmer lets you help turn one spread of grain. You remember both parts of the town.

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.title

At the Grain Drying Yard

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.body

The farmer from the causeway is spreading grain with a wooden rake. His daughter lifts a handful and says the top is dry already. He asks her to reach deeper. The grains below are still cool and damp. ‘If we sack it now, whose fault will the smell be?’ he asks. She says it will be theirs, so she takes the rake and spreads the thicker part thin.

You ask how to go on toward Tiju. He knows the land road beyond the yard and points out where the embankment turns away from the water. His daughter adds a low patch that fills after rain; she went that way with the cloth merchant last week. The farmer asks her to draw the turn for you after they finish this row.

A younger child comes with lunch in a covered pot. He wants to set it beside the grain, but his sister sends him to the bench where no loose husks will fall into it. The farmer hands you the rake while they clear the last wet patch.

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.choice.route

Ask the daughter about the land road to Tiju

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.choice.route_result

She draws the turn beyond the embankment and marks the low patch that fills after rain. You record the road toward Tiju. Her father checks the grain beneath the top layer once more before they sack it.

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.choice.scene

Help the family spread the damp grain

## ev.ev_gap_paukin.choice.scene_result

You take the rake and work beside the daughter. The younger child moves the lunch to the bench and brings water for everyone. The farmer thanks you when the last thick patch has had its turn in the sun.

## ev.paukin.threadcover.title

A Cover for the Thread Stall

## ev.paukin.threadcover.body

At the market, the cloth merchant’s sister is sewing a rain cover for her thread stall. The apprentice from the causeway holds the sheet up, but one corner is too short to reach the rear post. ‘I measured the front twice,’ he says. She asks whether he measured the back at all. He admits he did not, and both of them laugh.

You ask why she does not use the old cover. She shows you a long tear where water ran onto the dyed thread last week. She has saved the good half for a smaller basket, and wants a new seam for the stall. A customer arrives asking for blue thread; the sister asks him to keep his hands dry before touching the skeins. The apprentice offers to fetch a spare strip from the cart, but he does not know which bundle the merchant packed it in. The sister points him toward the marked one, then asks you to hold the rear post while she measures again.

‘The marked bundle is the one I nearly put in the puddle,’ the apprentice says. The sister tells him he has remembered the right lesson.

## ev.paukin.threadcover.choice.measure

Help measure and fit the stall cover

## ev.paukin.threadcover.choice.sew

Stay a day learning to patch the torn cover

## ev.paukin.threadcover.result.measure

You hold the post while the sister measures the missing length. The apprentice finds the marked bundle and brings a matching strip. The cover fits before the next shower, and the customer takes home dry blue thread.

## ev.paukin.threadcover.result.sew

You stay a day stitching the good half of the old cover into a smaller basket cloth. The sister shows you how to turn the torn edge inward. The apprentice tests it with a cup of water, and the thread beneath stays dry.
