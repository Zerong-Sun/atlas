## city.constantinopolis.name

Constantinopolis

## ev.constantinopolis.entry.title

Water at the Golden Horn

## ev.constantinopolis.entry.body

At the Golden Horn, a water carrier named Anna finds that one of her jars is empty. Her brother has taken the full jar to a household above the harbour and left her the empty one by mistake. She laughs, then counts her customers aloud so she can decide whom to visit first.

A channel brings water into the city, and covered cisterns hold supplies for its many households. Anna has spent years carrying water up these streets. She points out the dome of Hagia Sophia between the roofs, then asks whether you have a place to stay; she can show you the quay or the lodging lane once she gets her jar back.

A baker by the quay asks, ‘Have you forgotten my water again?’ Anna lifts the empty jar for him to see. ‘My brother has yours. I’ll bring it as soon as he comes down.’ He offers her a piece of bread while she waits. You ask whether the cistern is far. ‘The small covered one is around that corner,’ she says. ‘If you go up the hill, take the lane with the stone steps. My brother always chooses the flatter way and arrives late.’

## ev.gap.constantinopolis.scene.title

The Fish Seller’s Lunch

## ev.gap.constantinopolis.scene.body

At the quay, a fisherman named Petros sorts the morning catch. He puts the bruised fish in one bowl for his family’s soup and sets the sound fish on a wet board for sale. His daughter asks why she cannot sell the whole lot.

‘Because your mother is cooking lunch,’ he says, handing her the bowl. She smiles and carries it away. Petros has heard a captain call for cargo bound for Caffa and can tell you which boat it is.

When the girl returns, she asks whether there is enough for supper as well. Petros counts the remaining fish and says her mother can decide that after lunch. ‘She’ll say to save the best one for Grandfather,’ the girl replies. He puts one aside and asks her to fetch bread from the baker. You ask about the boats waiting beyond the quay. Petros knows which captain is bound for Caffa, but he wants to finish selling this catch before the tide changes.

## ev.gap.constantinopolis.scene.result.talk

Petros shows you how he checks the eyes and gills, then lets you arrange the sale fish on the board. You mark this quay on your map of Constantinople before going on.

## ev.gap.constantinopolis.scene.result.route

Petros points to the cargo boat at the far end of the quay. Its captain is taking passage to Caffa; you note the boat, the Black Sea route, and its destination on your map.

## ev.constantinopolis.waterjar.title

Anna’s Brother and the Empty Jar

## ev.constantinopolis.waterjar.body

Anna’s brother returns carrying the empty jar and an apology. The household on the hill has asked for more water for a neighbour who is ill. Anna still has two regular deliveries to make before dark.

‘Come with me up the hill and I’ll manage the rest,’ she says. Her brother offers to carry your pack if you would rather pay for a filled cup and rest by the cistern. He is determined to deliver the next jar correctly.

Her brother says the sick neighbour is the baker’s mother. ‘I told her I’d come back,’ he says, taking the heavy end of the pole. Anna asks why he left an empty jar at the quay. ‘I thought you would want one ready to fill.’ She taps his shoulder with her free hand. You ask whether there is time for the hill before dark. Anna looks at the light on the roofs: ‘If we leave now, yes. Otherwise I can still sell you a cup from the cistern.’
