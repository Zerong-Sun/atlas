## city.delli.name

Delli

## ev.delli.entry.body

At a Delhi water yard, a carrier is marking household jars with a bit of chalk. His younger sister has just returned from a lane where two neighbours share the same doorway. She put the full jar at the wrong threshold and wants to fetch it back before either household starts supper. ‘Read the mark to me,’ her brother says, holding out the empty jar. She does, then notices that rain has blurred the chalk.

You ask whether the two families will mind. ‘They know us,’ she says, ‘but one has a sick child and needs the water first.’ Her brother takes a fresh piece of chalk and draws a wider sign. A caravan scribe waiting nearby asks if they can fill his skin as well. He is copying a long route list toward Khotan, with many stops between Delhi and that far-off city, and needs water before the drivers leave.

The sister carries the corrected jar to the sick child’s home and returns for the other. Her brother asks the scribe to hold the chalk while he fills the skin. The scribe looks at you and says he can show you his route list after the water is delivered.

## ev.delli.entry.title

The Water Carrier’s Chalk Mark

## ev.ev_delli_entry.choice_1_result

The sister returns with both jars at their proper doors. You mark Delhi on your map and continue while the water carrier fills the caravan’s last skin.

## ev.ev_delli_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day with the scribe, checking his list of intermediate stops before the distant road reaches Khotan. The water carrier’s sister reads the place names aloud while the drivers pack. You mark Delhi, Khotan, and the long route between them on your map.


## ev.delli.jarbands.title

The Blue Thread on the Jar

## ev.delli.jarbands.body

In the water yard, a young potter is tying a blue thread around a household jar. Her aunt ordered two of the same size, one for drinking water and one for washing, and wants the family to tell them apart at a glance. The potter has only enough thread for one full loop. Her little cousin suggests painting a mark on the other; the potter worries the paint will rub off when the jar is carried.

You ask why the aunt did not choose different shapes. ‘They have to fit the same shelf,’ the girl says. Her cousin fetches a scrap of red cloth and asks whether it will last longer than paint. The aunt arrives with a carrying pole, sets the jars side by side, and has both children lift them. One handle catches on the pole. She asks the potter to smooth it before she takes either home.

The potter can pay you 0.6 silver if you stay the day to help finish and test the jars. If you are leaving soon, she will show you how she tests the handles and marks vessels for the family.

## ev.delli.jarbands.choice.finish

Stay a day and help finish the jars

## ev.delli.jarbands.result.finish

You spend the day helping smooth the handle and testing both jars on the pole. The aunt chooses blue thread for drinking water and red cloth for washing. The potter pays you 0.6 silver before they carry the jars home.

## ev.delli.jarbands.choice.learn

Watch how the family marks and tests jars

## ev.delli.jarbands.result.learn

The potter lets you feel the rough spot beneath the handle before she smooths it. Her aunt shows why the pole catches there, and the cousin ties on the red cloth. You record the small craft of keeping the two jars distinct.
