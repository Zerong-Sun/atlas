## city.berrhoea.name

Berrhoea (Aleppo)

## ev.berrhoea.entry.choice.pass

Thank Safiya and continue through the streets

## ev.berrhoea.entry.choice.look

Spend a day seeing the town with Safiya

## ev.berrhoea.entry.body

The citadel rises above the streets of Aleppo. At a covered stall below it, Safiya is laying out sheets of paper. Her father has written the prices on a scrap, but rain has blurred one number. She holds it up to him.

“If you cannot read it, ask me,” he says. “I am asking,” she replies. He laughs, gives her the price, and makes her write it again in larger figures. She says the writing is for him too: last week he sold two sheets at the old price and then wondered where the money went.

Safiya asks where you are headed. Travellers leave names of towns at her stall when they buy paper for letters. She has heard of routes from Aleppo to the northern coast, although she has never gone that far. She shows you the nearby water place and offers to take a day to walk the streets with you if you have time.

## ev.berrhoea.entry.title

Safiya’s Paper Stall

## ev.ev_berrhoea_entry.choice_1_result

You thank Safiya and continue through the streets. Her father calls after her to make sure she has written the new price on every sheet. You mark Aleppo on your map as you leave.

## ev.ev_berrhoea_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day with Safiya. She takes you past the water place, the bread ovens, and the sellers whose letters go farthest. One of them has a brother near Trebizond; Safiya asks him to repeat the stopping places on the northward road. By evening you have marked Aleppo, Trebizond, and the route between them on your map. Safiya goes back to her stall with the revised price list, and you depart the next morning.

## ev.gap.berrhoea.scene.title

The Flour at Rashid’s Mill

## ev.gap.berrhoea.scene.body

Rashid's niece Dima is sifting flour at the mill. She has covered the sack but left her own sleeves open, and a puff of white dust rises whenever she moves. Rashid holds out a cloth for her wrists. Dima says she is nearly done; then she sneezes and accepts it.

The flour is for her mother's bread oven. Rashid has promised to bring the first sack over himself, because Dima's mother thinks he spends too much time telling travellers stories beside the mill. He says he does, but only when they listen.

Dima asks if you would like to see how they keep the fine flour separate. Rashid once carried grain west toward Antioch and is happy to describe the road while she finishes the sack.

You ask Dima whether the flour will reach the oven before the first batch of bread goes in. ‘If Uncle Rashid stops telling this story,’ she says. Rashid protests that he is tying the sack while he talks, then asks her to show him which bag has the finer flour. She points to the one she set by the door and says her mother uses it for the thinner flatbreads. He lifts it to test the weight. ‘I will take this one first,’ he says, and Dima finally gets the empty sieve off the table so she can sweep up the flour she spilled.

## ev.gap.berrhoea.scene.result.talk

Dima shakes the last flour through the sieve and ties the sack. Rashid shoulders it at once, to prove he has remembered his promise. You walk with them to the bread oven and learn how the mill lane joins the market street. You add this part of Aleppo to your map.

## ev.gap.berrhoea.scene.result.route

Rashid names the places where he stopped on the way west to Antioch. Dima interrupts once to ask whether he really delivered the grain before telling stories there. He admits he arrived late that day. You mark Aleppo, Antioch, and the route between them; Dima takes the finished sack from him before he can start another tale.

## ev.berrhoea.lentilpot.title

A Pot of Lentils

## ev.berrhoea.lentilpot.body

At the edge of a busy lane, Nadwa ladles lentils from a clay pot. Her son Harun watches the bread oven next door. Nadwa promised to save his grandmother a bowl, and he has put a small wooden spoon beside it so nobody serves that portion by mistake.

Harun's grandmother used to cook here. She now sits at home with a sore knee and sends him back whenever the soup needs more salt. “She could tell from across the lane,” Nadwa says. Harun insists that he can tell too, takes a careful taste, and asks for a little more.

Nadwa offers you a bowl for the road. If you stay to help prepare tomorrow's pot, she can feed you and let you sleep indoors. Harun says his grandmother will inspect the soup either way.

Harun asks whether his grandmother will come down when she smells the fresh bread. "Her knees will decide that," Nadwa says. A neighbour arriving for soup offers to carry the bowl up to her. Harun puts the wooden spoon beside it and asks the neighbour to bring it back; his grandmother likes that small spoon. Nadwa tastes the broth after her son and adds a pinch of salt. She offers you a clean bowl while Harun goes to fetch another loaf from the oven.

## ev.berrhoea.lentilpot.choice.buy

## ev.berrhoea.lentilpot.result.buy

You pay for soup and bread. Harun carries the reserved bowl to his grandmother before taking yours from the pot. He returns to report that she approves of the salt, though she wants the bread warmed longer next time. You eat on the road and regain some strength.

## ev.berrhoea.lentilpot.result.stay

You spend the day washing lentils and carrying water for the next pot. In the evening Harun brings his grandmother over on his arm. She tastes a spoonful, tells Nadwa it is good, and then quietly asks you to warm her bread a little longer. Nadwa gives you supper and a place to sleep. You leave the next morning rested.
