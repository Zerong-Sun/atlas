## city.java-major.name

Java Major

## ev.java-major.entry.body

At the landing, a sailor has laid a cargo list beneath a roof where the rain cannot reach it. A corner still curls from yesterday’s squall. His younger brother is lifting bags from the boat and calling out each mark, but he hesitates at a wooden crate whose label has washed away. ‘Is this for the timber seller or the spice house?’ he asks. The sailor opens the lid enough to show bundles of dry bark inside. A woman from the spice house recognizes the knot on the wrapping. ‘My father ties ours with a double loop,’ she says. She shows him the matching entry on her own list. You ask how they keep the bags dry when another storm comes. The sailor points to the raised boards inside the storehouse; the woman says the last load was moved there before nightfall. She has an uncle sailing toward Chamba and knows where travellers can ask about the route. The brother writes a fresh label for the crate, then reads it aloud to be sure he has put it on the right goods. The sailor asks whether you are going straight across town or will stay to learn the harbour’s roads.

## ev.java-major.entry.choice.look

Stay a day and ask about the onward route

## ev.java-major.entry.choice.pass

Continue across the harbour town

## ev.java-major.entry.title

The Wet Cargo List

## ev.ev_java_major_entry.choice_1_result

The younger brother ties the fresh label securely while his sister from the spice house holds the crate steady. The sailor points out the road beyond the landing, and you cross the town with a clear sense of where its warehouses stand.

## ev.ev_java_major_entry.choice_2_result

You spend another day with the family as they finish sorting the wet shipment. Their uncle arrives from the Chamba boat and marks its port and sea road on a board for you. The younger brother copies the names onto a new cargo list, this time keeping it beneath the roof.

## ev.java-major.reed-case.title

The Sailmaker’s Reed Case

## ev.java-major.reed-case.body

A sailmaker is trying to close the reed case that holds her needles. Its lid has split along the woven edge, and her apprentice has already gathered the needles twice from the floor. ‘I can wrap them in cloth,’ he says. She shakes her head. ‘Then you will put the whole bundle down near the brazier again.’ He admits he did that yesterday, when a ship’s captain came in with a torn sail. You ask whether they have a spare case. The sailmaker has one, but its strap is missing. Her apprentice finds the loose strap under a pile of old canvas and brings it over. The captain arrives to ask if his sail can be ready before the next tide. ‘First we need the needles where we can find them,’ the sailmaker tells him. She gives the apprentice a strip of binding to stitch around the case and asks him to choose where the strap should sit. You can help him hold the split edge together, or stay through the next day while she teaches him to repair the larger tear in the sail.

## ev.java-major.reed-case.choice.repair

Help mend the needle case now

## ev.java-major.reed-case.result.repair

You hold the reed edge together while the apprentice stitches the binding and fixes the strap. The sailmaker shakes the closed case; no needle falls out. The captain sees it working and offers to carry their repaired canvas to the ship himself. She thanks you by name.

## ev.java-major.reed-case.choice.stay

Stay a day for the sail repair

## ev.java-major.reed-case.result.stay

You stay another day as the sailmaker shows her apprentice how to set patches without pulling the cloth out of shape. The captain returns to check the work, then waits while they test it in the wind. By the time they finish, you know why a good repair needs the cloth to rest between stitches.
