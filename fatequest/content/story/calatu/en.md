## city.calatu.name

Calatu

## ev.calatu.entry.body

A grain boat has just come into Calatu, and a woman is distributing its sacks between the town store and the market. Her son counts aloud, then pauses when a fisherman asks for the sack she promised his household. “I wrote his name on the other side,” she tells the boy. He turns the board over and finds it. You ask why a harbour town keeps such close track of grain. “The hills give us stone,” she says, “and the boats bring much of our food. My neighbours will ask before sunset whether their flour has arrived.” The fisherman offers to carry a sack for the older porter beside him. A guard at the wall calls down that he will leave the gate open until the last load is inside. His friend asks whether there will still be room for dates at the market. “If you stop eating them before they reach the stall,” the woman answers. She shows you the street through town and the mosque door near the wall.

## ev.calatu.a.title

The Watchman’s Harbour List

## ev.calatu.a.body

On the wall above Calatu's harbour, a watchman has drawn the morning's ships with charcoal: two low boats, one high-sterned vessel and a grain carrier still outside the bend. His young assistant insists the high-sterned ship arrived first. “Then why is its gangplank still on deck?” the watchman asks. They look again and see that it has only turned to find deeper water. The assistant crosses out his mark and calls to a porter below to hold a space for the grain boat. You ask where the ships came from. The watchman points across the gulf toward Ormus, then along the coast toward Dufar. A merchant waiting on the steps says her pepper is on one of those vessels and wants to know when she can collect it. “When it ties up and the clerk reads your mark,” the assistant answers, pleased to say something certain. He invites you to walk the wall while he finishes the list.

## ev.calatu.b.title

The Storehouse by the Wall

## ev.calatu.b.body

At the storehouse, a guard is checking the grain reserved for the people on the wall. A younger porter has brought the wrong size of sack and wants to swap it quietly. “Put both weights on the board,” the guard tells him. “Then we can fix the count together.” The porter admits he was hurrying to meet his sister at the quay. You ask why the guard needs such a careful reserve. He points to the fortified harbour. “People from Ormus have used Calatu as a safer place when trouble came to the gulf. The walls are useful only while the town can feed the hands behind them.” A date seller nearby says her small boat brought food that morning and asks where to unload. The guard gives the porter a new tally and tells him to help her. Before they go, he offers to show you the coast road and the inland caravan names written on the storehouse map. The porter holds the corrected tally against his chest so he will not lose it on the way.

## ev.ev_calatu_entry.choice_1_result

The grain seller points you through the street behind her stall. Her son is still reading names from the board as she hands out the sacks. You continue with Calatu's harbour and town lanes fixed in mind.

## ev.ev_calatu_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day among the stalls. The fisherman collects his household's grain, and the grain seller introduces you to the date seller and the porters who keep the town supplied. By evening you know which lane reaches the wall and where the food boats unload.

## ev.ev_calatu_entry.choice_3_result

You leave your shoes at the mosque door and wait until the attendant shows you where visitors may sit. Afterwards he walks you back to the wall. The grain seller recognizes you from the harbour and thanks you for treating the gathering with care.

## ev.ev_calatu_a.choice_1_result

You spend a day on the wall while ships enter the harbour. The assistant erases his mistaken arrival mark and writes each vessel in the order it ties up. The watchman shows you the turn across the gulf toward Ormus, and later asks you to join him for a closer look at the shipping records.

## ev.ev_calatu_a.choice_2_result

You pay the merchant for a bag of pepper after the clerk checks its mark. She has the porter sew a loose corner before handing it over. The watchman tells you why ships bring spices here on their way inland and invites you to look over his harbour list.

## ev.ev_calatu_a.choice_3_result

The assistant points to the vessels preparing for the Dufar coast, correcting himself when one turns toward another berth. The watchman explains how to distinguish a ship still waiting outside from one ready to depart. You leave with a better sense of the harbour's movements.

## ev.ev_calatu_b.choice_1_result

The guard shows you the grain tallies kept for the wall and names Ormus across the gulf. He tells you how the harbour could shelter people arriving from there, then asks the young porter to read today's reserve aloud. The porter gets it right, and the guard sends him to help his sister at the quay.

## ev.ev_calatu_b.choice_2_result

You buy a small serving of dates from the seller's boat and eat them beside the quay. She puts the rest of her stock beneath the storehouse awning. The guard explains why arriving food goes first into the town's count; after the meal you feel ready to continue.

## ev.ev_calatu_b.choice_3_result

The guard opens the storehouse map. He shows where caravans turn toward Camadi and where the coastal road continues toward Dufar. The porter copies both names onto a scrap for you, then asks his sister to check the spelling before you leave.

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.title

Calatu: The Watchman’s Second List

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.body

The watchman calls you to the wall again when the next ship ties up. His assistant has made a fresh harbour list, and the pepper merchant is waiting to see whether her cargo has arrived. “Read the mark first,” the watchman tells him. The boy squints at the painted boards on the quay and finds the right one. The merchant gives him a smile and asks whether the road toward Camadi is busy this week. “Ask the caravan master below,” the watchman says. “I can tell you which boats have come in; he knows the inland wells.” The caravan master joins them with a packet of pepper for sale. As he and the merchant talk, the watchman traces Ormus across the gulf and Dufar along the coast for you. The assistant puts a pebble on each name so the wind will not fold the map.

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_1

Buy the caravan master’s pepper

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_1_result

You pay the caravan master for a sealed bag of pepper. The merchant checks the seam with you and makes him replace a worn tag. The watchman tells you which ship brought this cargo before it passed to the inland buyer.

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_2

Ask the caravan master about Camadi

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_2_result

The caravan master names the stopping places toward Camadi and marks the point where the road leaves the coast. The assistant copies the turn beside his harbour list. You compare the two and leave with a clearer picture of the inland route.

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_3

Spend a day following the harbour list

## ev.ev_calatu_a_followup.choice_3_result

You spend a day with the assistant, matching the ships on the water to the names on his list. A sailor explains which one will sail toward Ormus and which must first call at Dufar. By evening the assistant no longer confuses the high stern with a ship that has already docked.

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.title

Shoes for the Guard’s Daughter

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.body

A shoemaker near the wall has been asked to mend the sandals of a guard's daughter before she walks to her grandmother's house. The girl brings them in with one strap hanging loose. “You tied it again,” the shoemaker says. “I thought it would last until I got here,” she replies. Her father is at the harbour gate and cannot leave his post, so he has sent a little money with her. The shoemaker tests the leather and tells her the strap can be replaced. She asks whether he can use the old one; it belonged to her mother. He holds it against the light and finds a strong section near the buckle. “This part will do. Sit here and tell me how far you have to walk.” She names the lane and the grandmother's door. You can help the shoemaker cut and fit the strap, or accompany the girl through the lanes once it is ready.

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.choice.mend

Help the shoemaker fit the old strap

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.result.mend

You hold the sandal while the shoemaker cuts the sound piece of leather and sets it beneath the buckle. The girl tries both sandals on the shop floor and runs a few steps. Her father brings the promised payment; the shoemaker gives you a share for the work.

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.choice.walk

Walk with the girl to her grandmother’s door

## ev.calatu.shoemaker.result.walk

With her sandals mended, the girl leads you along the lane she described. Her grandmother opens the door before you knock and asks why she came with a stranger. “Because Father was at the gate,” the girl says, showing the repaired strap. The grandmother thanks you and sends a message back to him.
