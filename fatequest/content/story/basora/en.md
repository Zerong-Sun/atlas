## city.basora.name

Basora

## ev.basora.entry.body

Basra stands where the waters of Irāk run down toward the Gulf.
You travel hither with the Badawīn of Khafāja, for there is no safe going
in these parts except in their company. Date-palms line the canals; gardens
and plastered walls mark the richer houses. Upstream lie Wāsit and the
shrines of Meshhed Alī, where the Shīah keep their treasury of vows; down
the river the dhows take dates, grain, and cloth toward Omān and India. You
must know that Basra has been a gate of the sea since the first centuries of
Islam, and that its markets still speak Arabic, Persian, and the tongues of
the Gulf.

## ev.basora.a.title

Basora: The Date Groves at the Mouth of the Rivers

## ev.basora.a.body

At the station where the two great rivers meet the sea, the date men of Basora work the groves that line the water on every side. A grove master who has counted the harvests for years says the city lives by the palm and the ship alike, and that the roads from here run up the rivers to the old capitals and down the gulf to the sea of India — a traveller who reads the water here may choose his whole road. He asks which way your business lies.

## ev.basora.a.choice.1

Buy dates at the grove master's price

## ev.basora.a.choice.2

Ask the grove master the river road toward Ctesiphon

## ev.basora.a.choice.3

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.title

Basora: The Grove Master's Second Word on the Water

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.body

The grove master takes you to the water's edge where the river meets the sea. He says the tide comes up the rivers twice a day, and the palms drink salt and sweet both, and the ships ride the same tide that waters the groves — the city's two lives are one water. He asks whether you will sail on it or trade beside it.

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.choice_1

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.choice_1_result

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.choice_2

## ev.ev_basora_a_followup.choice_2_result
