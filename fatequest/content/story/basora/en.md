## city.basora.name

Basora

## ev.basora.entry.title

The Basra Landing

## ev.basora.entry.body

The canal reaches the Tigris between date groves and low houses. At the landing a woman named Nura counts jars onto a boat. Her son keeps calling out numbers before she does, and she makes him start again whenever he skips one.

‘The jars are for my sister downstream,’ she tells you. ‘She sent the boat up for them, and now everyone wants to leave before the tide turns.’ She leaves a space on the bench for your pack while she checks the last rope. From here, river craft carry dates and cloth toward the Gulf; the road inland returns toward Baghdad.

Her son Samir checks the last jar against a scratched list and asks why his aunt needs so many. Nura says she is taking them to families who bring dates to her yard, and several of their old jars have cracked. ‘Will she send us back the empty ones?’ he asks. ‘Only if you remember to tell her which belong to whom.’ You ask whether the boat will return tomorrow. The boatman says he must watch the water first; Nura tells Samir to write the owners’ marks on a dry board so the boatman can read them. The boy runs for charcoal and comes back before the last jar is loaded.

## ev.basora.entry.choice.pass

Continue along the landing

## ev.basora.entry.choice.rest

Stay the day and rest by the canal

## ev.ev_basora_entry.choice_1_result

Nura’s son points out the path between the warehouses. You thank him, mark Basra on your map, and follow it into town.

## ev.ev_basora_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day in the shade while boats come and go. By evening your strength has returned. Nura’s son draws the canal bend in the dust for you, and you add Basra to your map.

## ev.gap.basora.scene.title

The Date Boat

## ev.gap.basora.scene.body

Nura’s son, Samir, is sorting soft dates from the ones ready to dry. He keeps one small basket apart. ‘For my aunt,’ he says. ‘Last year I ate half of hers before the boat left. She sent back the empty basket with my name written inside.’

A boatman calls for the next load. Samir picks up the basket, then turns to you: ‘Can you watch this one while I carry the others? If I lose it again, she’ll write my name on every jar.’

You ask what his aunt said when she sent the basket back. Samir says she wrote, ‘Next time count before you taste.’ Nura hears him and adds that the note was kinder than the teasing he got at home. The boatman asks where to set the drying dates; Samir points to a covered tray, then notices the loose seam in the sack beside it. He tells his mother he will mend it after the small basket is safe. Nura tells him to deal with one thing at a time. A second boat noses in, and the crew begins making room along the landing.

## ev.gap.basora.scene.choice.talk

Keep an eye on the basket

## ev.gap.basora.scene.choice.route

Ask Samir about the Baghdad road

## ev.gap.basora.scene.result.talk

You wait until Samir returns and place the basket in his hands. He counts it twice, grins, and shares two fresh dates with you. ‘She’ll get the rest this time.’ You mark the landing and its place in Basra on your map.

## ev.gap.basora.scene.result.route

Samir has watched the inland caravans leave from this landing. He points northwest toward Baghdad and shows you where they turn away from the canal. You mark that road and the city on your map.

When the road is clear in your book, Samir checks his aunt’s basket once more and sets it on a dry board beside Nura’s jars. She sees the mark and says she will watch it while he carries the next load. The basket is safe for the boat.

## ev.basora.dateboat.title

The Unmarked Basket

## ev.basora.dateboat.body

When you return to the landing, Samir is arguing with the boatman over a count. His aunt’s basket is safe, but a second basket has no mark. Nura is busy tying up the jars and asks him to settle it before the boat leaves.

‘If it goes without a name, no one will know who should be paid,’ Samir says. He asks you to stay while he opens the baskets and counts their contents. The boatman offers a small wage for the time. A vendor nearby has fresh dates and cool water if you would rather get on with your journey.

You ask Samir whether the second basket came from the same garden. He looks at its contents: one layer of dates is softer than the rest, which makes him think two loads were stacked together. The boatman says his younger brother tied the rope at dawn and may have covered the mark by accident. Nura calls across the landing for the boy to come and look; he is still unloading jars. Samir offers him a piece of charcoal to write the name clearly once they find it. The vendor brings a cup of cool water for Nura, who has been lifting jars all morning, and tells her to sit for one moment before the tide turns.

## ev.basora.dateboat.choice.count

Stay for the day to count the baskets

## ev.basora.dateboat.choice.eat

Buy dates and water for 0.6 silver

## ev.basora.dateboat.result.count

By dusk, Samir finds the missing mark beneath a knot in the rope. The boatman pays you 0.6 silver for the day. Nura puts the basket aboard with its owner’s name facing up.

## ev.basora.dateboat.result.eat

You pay 0.6 silver and sit by the canal with dates and a cup of water. Samir calls out the final count from the boat; this time Nura nods at once. You feel ready to travel again.
