## city.medina.name

Medina

## ev.medina.entry.body

At a Medina guesthouse, a traveller from the north is trying to remember where she left her bundle of dates. The keeper's young son finds it beside the water jar. “I moved it out of the sun,” he says. She thanks him and asks if there is room to sit while she sorts the food for her return journey. The keeper brings out a mat and asks where she is headed. She names the road toward Petra, then admits that she needs to ask about the next water stop before leaving. You have the same question. “Let us ask together after the morning crowd has had water,” the keeper says. Outside, people travelling to visit the city's sacred places pass along the street. The woman tells the boy to keep one date for finding her bundle; he says he would rather hear how far she has come. She begins with the first town she can remember clearly and the keeper sets another cup down for you.

## ev.medina.entry.choice.pass

Ask for the onward road and continue

## ev.medina.entry.choice.rest

Rest at the guesthouse for a day

## ev.medina.entry.title

The Bundle beside the Water Jar

## ev.ev_medina_entry.choice_1_result

The keeper points out the road beyond the guesthouse and names the first place where you can ask for water. The traveller returns to her mat to finish packing. You continue with Medina's streets clear in mind.

## ev.ev_medina_entry.choice_2_result

You stay for a day at the guesthouse. The keeper and the northern traveller compare their routes and share a meal with you after the crowd thins. You sleep well, regain your strength and leave knowing the station's doors and streets.

## ev.medina.site_gap.title

The Guesthouse Water Jar

## ev.medina.site_gap.body

At the guesthouse door, the keeper has set a shared water jar in the shade. His daughter is turning the ladle upside down on a clean cloth after each visitor. A man leading an animal asks whether he may water it from the jar. “Use the trough beyond the wall,” she says, “then come back for your own drink.” He thanks her and leads the animal around. You ask why she keeps the ladle off the jar's rim. She shows you the dust on the wall where the wind meets it and says the cloth is easier to wash. A traveller setting out toward Petra asks about the north road. The keeper comes out with a full bucket and points from the gate. The daughter asks him to wait before pouring: a leaf has blown into the jar. She lifts it out, and he lets her make the next round of cups herself. She asks the keeper to leave the clean cloth beside the jar for the evening visitors.

## ev.medina.site_gap.choice.route

Ask about the road to Petra

## ev.ev_medina_site_gap.choice_1_result

The keeper takes you to the gate and points out the road toward Petra. He names a place to replenish water before the next dry stretch. His daughter repeats the turn for the traveller beside you, making sure both of you heard it.

## ev.medina.site_gap.choice.interact

Help refill the shared jar

## ev.ev_medina_site_gap.choice_2_result

You bring the bucket while the daughter lifts the lid and clears away the leaf. The keeper pours the water slowly so no grit falls from the rim. She sets the ladle back on its clean cloth and offers the waiting traveller the first cup.

## ev.medina.lantern.title

The Lantern at the Guesthouse Gate

## ev.medina.lantern.body

A lantern maker has brought a repaired lamp to the guesthouse gate. The keeper's mother ordered it after two travellers missed the turn in the dark. Her granddaughter takes the lamp and asks why its glass has a faint crack. “It is the old pane, cleaned and set into a new frame,” the maker says. “We tested it at home.” The grandmother lights it and notices the flame sits too low to show the painted arrow above the gate. The maker brings a small hook from his bag, but the granddaughter knows a better place on the post. She holds the lamp there while the grandmother stands across the lane to check what a traveller will see. “There,” the older woman calls. “I can read the arrow.” The granddaughter asks you to help carry the maker's tools back, or stay until evening to see whether the lamp really guides the first arrivals.

## ev.medina.lantern.choice.tools

Help the maker return his tools

## ev.medina.lantern.result.tools

You carry the maker's tools as he walks back through the lane. The granddaughter stays to light the lantern at the gate. When the keeper's mother sees you return, she thanks you for helping them finish before dusk; nearby households remember the new light.

## ev.medina.lantern.choice.evening

Stay until evening to test the lantern

## ev.medina.lantern.result.evening

You stay for the day and watch the lantern as the sky darkens. The first travellers see the arrow and turn into the guesthouse without calling for directions. The grandmother serves you supper and makes up a place to sleep; you leave rested the next morning.
