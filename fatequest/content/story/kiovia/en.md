## city.kiovia.name

Kiovia

## ev.kiovia.entry.title

The Dnieper Landing

## ev.kiovia.entry.body

Boats are being loaded on the Dnieper below Kyiv’s hill. A woman named Olena wraps a wax candle in cloth so it will not bend against the other cargo. Her son has brought an extra bundle of honeycomb and is trying to find room for it.

‘Take it to your uncle first,’ she tells him. ‘He asked for it yesterday.’ The boy runs uphill with the bundle. Olena shows you where travellers gather for the northern road. The town has old walls and churches above the river; people still trade here, even as the streets carry traces of earlier wars.

You ask why the uncle needs honeycomb today. Olena says he melts some wax for household candles and shares the rest with a neighbour who keeps bees farther uphill. Her son calls back from the steps, asking whether he should bring the empty basket home. ‘Bring it if your uncle is finished with it,’ she says. A boatman asks which of her wrapped candles should travel in the shaded crate. Olena picks up the one she protected first and shows him how its wick lies straight. You ask when the northbound group meets. She points to a courtyard above the landing, where a driver is checking his wheel before setting out.

## ev.kiovia.entry.choice.pass

Continue past the landing

## ev.kiovia.entry.choice.look

Spend a day talking with the river people

## ev.ev_kiovia_entry.choice_1_result

You follow the riverside path and mark Kyiv on your map before continuing.

## ev.ev_kiovia_entry.choice_2_result

You spend the day by the landing. Olena points out the northern road to Moscow and the people preparing to take it. You mark Kyiv, that road, and Moscow on your map.

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.title

Olena’s Candle

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.body

Olena’s son returns from his uncle with a small wooden case. Inside is a candle mould he borrowed without asking. His uncle wants it back before evening prayers, and the boatman wants Olena’s wax on board now.

‘You could have told me,’ Olena says. The boy admits he wanted to make a candle to give his uncle. He asks you to take the mould uphill while he helps load the boat. Olena sets the mould on a flat board and checks whether the wick will stay centred. Her son puts down a small piece of wax, waiting to hear if there is still time to make the gift before the boat leaves.

You ask why he took the mould without asking. ‘Uncle always lets me watch, and I thought I could surprise him,’ the boy says. Olena answers that a surprise can wait until the owner knows where his tool is. The boatman calls again from the cargo ramp. The boy says he can load the wax crate while you carry the mould uphill. Olena checks the pot: it is warm enough to pour one small candle if they work steadily. Her son looks from the ramp to the mould, then asks whether his uncle will still be pleased if the gift arrives a little late.

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.choice.return

Return the mould to his uncle now

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.choice.pour

Stay to make a candle with Olena

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.result.return

His uncle has already received the honeycomb. You return the mould and explain why the boy borrowed it. He smiles, then tells you where the northbound road leaves the landing and how travellers follow it toward Moscow. You mark the route and city on your map.

## ev.kiovia.waxcandle.result.pour

You hold the mould while Olena pours the warm wax. Her son waits until the candle is firm, then takes it uphill with the mould. He returns later to say his uncle lit it for the evening prayers. Olena thanks you, and the family remembers your help.
