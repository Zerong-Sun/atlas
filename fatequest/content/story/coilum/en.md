## city.coilum.name

Coilum

## ev.coilum.entry.body

In Coilum, a porter sets down a basket of pepper beneath the warehouse eaves and argues with a young ship's cook over a torn label. “It says whose basket this is,” he tells her. “The rain took half the ink,” she replies. An older woman sorting galangal recognizes the knot in the basket handle. “My sister ties hers that way. She will be here before the tide turns.” The porter does not open the basket; he moves it to a dry bench and asks the cook to write a new label when the owner arrives. You ask how many ships are waiting outside. “Enough to make every porter claim he has worked all night,” the cook says. “Most of them have,” the older woman answers, passing him a cup of water. Merchants of several communities keep houses near the harbour, and the lanes between them are busy with carts of pepper, galangal and cloth. The porter points out the road through town. If you stay for the day, he says, you can meet the owner and see how the harbour sorts its cargo.

## ev.coilum.entry.title

The Basket Under the Eaves

## ev.ev_coilum_entry.choice_1_result

The porter sets his cup down and walks you to the lane that carries travellers through Coilum. He calls back to the cook to leave the basket where its owner can see it. You continue with the harbour's turns in mind.

## ev.ev_coilum_entry.choice_2_result

You spend a day near the warehouses. The basket's owner arrives, recognizes her knot and asks the cook to write the new label in front of her. Later the porter shows you the separate sheds where pepper and galangal are kept. You leave knowing the people and the paths behind the harbour bustle.

## ev.coilum.site_gap.title

Pepper at the Weighing Shed

## ev.coilum.site_gap.body

A woman who weighs pepper has two sacks open in front of her. One smells sharp and dry; the other has a damp patch along its seam. Her son wants to hurry the weighing because a buyer is waiting. “If I put them both on the same account, he will come back with the wet one,” she says. She makes him lift each sack from the floor so he can feel the difference. You ask how she can tell whether the weight comes from pepper or moisture. She shows you the empty sack first, then rubs a few grains between her fingers. The boy notices that they clump together. “Set this one on boards and spread it,” she tells him. “We'll weigh it again when it dries.” Outside, a carrier asks which road goes toward Maabar. The woman points along the coast, away from the boat landing, and the boy repeats the turn so he will remember it himself. Before you leave, the son offers you a dry grain to compare with the clumped ones. The son asks whether the wet bag should be sold later at the same price. His mother tells him to wait until it is dry and weighed again; then they can answer a buyer honestly.

## ev.coilum.site_gap.choice.route

Ask about the land road to Maabar

## ev.ev_coilum_site_gap.choice_1_result

The weigher walks to the door and shows you the road that follows the coast toward Maabar. “The boats use the landing behind us; you want the carts' road,” she says. Her son points out the first turn as you leave, proud that he has learned it too.

## ev.coilum.site_gap.choice.interact

Help her weigh the pepper again

## ev.ev_coilum_site_gap.choice_2_result

You hold the scale steady while she sets the dry sack on it. Her son writes down the weight and leaves the damp sack on boards for later. She shows you the empty sack's weight, then lets him make the next calculation without her hand over his shoulder.

## ev.coilum.baker.title

Bread for the Late Porters

## ev.coilum.baker.body

A baker near the harbour has kept a tray of bread warm for porters coming off a late ship. Her grandson counts the loaves and frowns. “There are six men on that boat and only five left.” She opens the oven and finds a small loaf she set aside for herself. “That makes six,” she says, “but what will you eat?” He offers to split his supper with her. The first porter arrives carrying a wet rope over his shoulder and asks if the others have come. “They are helping a boat make fast,” the boy tells him. The baker passes the man water and asks him to wait until everyone is there. When the rest arrive, one says the youngest porter has gone to bring an older neighbour home through the dark lanes. The baker puts the tray back by the oven so his bread stays warm too. You can help the boy find the neighbour and bring him here, or spend the evening at their table while the porters tell you which coast road they use when ships cannot sail.

## ev.coilum.baker.choice.fetch

Go with the boy to bring the neighbour in

## ev.coilum.baker.result.fetch

You and the boy meet the young porter and his older neighbour at the lane's bend. You carry the neighbour's bag while the boy lights the path. At the bakery the last loaf is still warm. The porters thank you, and the baker makes room so the neighbour can sit beside her.

## ev.coilum.baker.choice.table

Stay for supper and hear the porters’ roads

## ev.coilum.baker.result.table

You stay until the bread is shared. One porter draws the land road toward Maabar in spilled flour, then brushes it away when the baker brings another plate. She gives you supper, and the company leaves you feeling welcome in the town. You depart with a clearer sense of the coastal road.
